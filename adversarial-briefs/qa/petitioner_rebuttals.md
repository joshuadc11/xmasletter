## Rebuttal 1 — The magistrate's sentence classifies *Smith* on first application; the "two criteria" are one criterion seen from both ends.

**Verdict: (a) wrong**, with one loose phrase absorbed.

The challenge's engine is the claim that the carrier "performs" the call — switching, connecting, billing — so a pen-register log records "the carrier's side of the bargain." That is *Smith*'s own reasoning, and it proves too much by exactly *Katz*'s width: the phone company performed Katz's call too — switched it, connected it, billed it — and the call was protected anyway. Performance-of-carriage cannot make the customer's communication a bilateral dealing with the carrier, because the call's *counterparty is someone else*. That is the magistrate's sentence working, not failing: a ledger memorializes a transaction conducted **with** the business — the purchase whose object is the drug, the booking whose object is the room, the check whose object is the payment; the dealing terminates at the counter. A pen register memorializes conduct directed **through** the business at the world — whom the customer contacted. The pharmacy log records a purchase; "what he ingests" is an *inference* (he may be buying for his mother). The registry records a booking; "with whom he slept" is an inference (he may have given the room away). The pen register records whom he called — not an inference from a transaction but the protected activity itself. Record-versus-inference was our stated test from Q1, Q5, and Q7; Q7's "entire content is the activity" is the same line read from the other end, not a second criterion. The magistrate who asks "counterparty or conduit?" classifies *Smith* without fiat.

And the mirror: Respondent's framework must *also* say something strained about *Smith* — it keeps dialed numbers as "bilateral commercial dealings" on a "folk knowledge" presumption (Bench Q11–Q12) while surrendering knowing exposure wherever data is "generated invisibly." The switch's log is generated invisibly too. Both frameworks must classify *Smith*; only ours says openly what its classification costs.

Absorbed: the Q12 aside that a pen register is "also surveillance under our Part I logic" was shorthand for the logic (government-initiated prospective acquisition), not the dictated holding, which is deliberately confined to location (Bench Q18). Nothing turns on it: *Smith* falls under ring three regardless.

## Rebuttal 2 — The pharmacy gap is real, priced, and identical under Respondent's own rule; the "double standard" confuses calibration above a floor with calibration as the floor.

**Verdict: (b) right that the gap exists — we said so ourselves ("knowing its rhetorical cost," Resp. Q4) — but wrong twice over on what it proves.**

*First*, the double-standard charge fails on a distinction our answers drew before the challenge was written. We invoke Congress to layer protection **above a stated constitutional floor** — the ledger side is governed by *Oklahoma Press* reasonableness, a constitutional rule, plus a customer interest *Miller* denies. Respondent invokes Congress **in place of** any constitutional rule. Bench Q25, verbatim: "that is calibration *above* the floor, which we have never rejected. Our objection was only ever to calibration *as* the floor." Nothing must be surrendered, because the two positions were never the same position. Abdication is declining to have a rule; HIPAA atop a rule is federalism.

*Second*, "this is the answer that prices the revolution" gets the arithmetic backwards. Run Respondent's own test (their Bench Q29 framework, their Bench Q12) over the same parade: pharmacy histories, hotel registries, credit-card statements, brokerage records are *all* "bilateral commercial dealings" on the *Miller/Smith* side of *their* line too — obtainable by subpoena, at any volume, forever. The gap they price into our rule is a gap their regime contains identically, with two differences, both running our way: under our rule the customer in every one of those categories holds a reasonableness interest *Miller* strips from him, and calling and browsing histories move to the protected side. There is no record category on their parade that our rule protects *less* than theirs; there are several it protects more. A "price" computed entirely from the cost of *Respondent's own baseline* is not the price of the revolution. It is the price of the status quo, billed to the wrong party.

## Rebuttal 3 — One variable, applied uniformly: category, fixed by what the record memorializes. The regime where volume transmutes is Respondent's.

**Verdict: (a) wrong.**

Set the three answers side by side properly. Financial records: ledgers, at any volume — volume never moves a record *across* the line (Resp. Q2). Tower dump: location data is categorically a chronicle, so one datum of it sits on the protected side — brevity never moves a record across the line either (Bench Q19). Those are not two metrics; they are the same anti-volumetric rule pointed in both directions: *quantity neither promotes a ledger nor demotes a chronicle*. The challenge's incredulity — "a single datum is not a 'chronicle' of anything" — mistakes the category's name for a per-record audit, which is precisely the audit our rule abolishes: a single cleared check is not a "ledger of a life" either, and it is a ledger.

ALPR (Bench Q21) is not a counterexample because it is not a record-classification question at all: there is no record-keeper, no compelled production, no third party — it is a *surveillance-method* question under Part I/*Carpenter*, asking when government observation (*Knotts*) becomes government tracking. Aggregation there marks the boundary between two surveillance categories; it does not transmute any third-party record type, because none is in play.

Now the mirror, which decides this challenge. If aggregation-sensitivity convicts a framework of being "a table of results," Respondent's framework is convicted first and worse: its **convergence principle** — "load-bearing throughout" its answers (Resp. Bench Q2) — holds that the *same fixes*, no search one by one, *become* a search when accumulation matches the archive. And its Bench Q5 holds a pattern-of-life **summary** of tower data is a search while the underlying fixes are not — aggregation transmuting category within a single dataset. Respondent's entire defense of this judgment is that volume transmutes constitutional character at day seven. It cannot prosecute us for a vice that is its own load-bearing wall.

## Rebuttal 4 — The forced-choice answer is about votes, not merits; the outcome-change claim is false on the record of these answers; and Respondent made the identical alternative election.

**Verdict: (b) right that the disposition is novel in form and that a narrower path exists — both conceded long ago — but wrong that the concessions, combined, surrender QP I.**

*First*, "his proposal changes none [no outcomes]" is flatly contradicted by answers Respondent elsewhere locks in. Resp. Q13 lists three concrete outcome changes: *Smith*'s entire line (comprehensive calling/e-mail logs become warrant territory — Respondent's preamble Item 1 locks that one in itself), compelled browsing and search histories, and the *Miller* depositor's newly cognizable reasonableness challenge. *Katz* changed outcomes prospectively while leaving *Olmstead*'s judgments in their graves; we propose exactly that operation. The *Miller* judgment survives; the rule of decision dies.

*Second*, the acceptance of the "no longer states the governing rule" styling concedes nothing, because the styling *is* an affirmative answer to QP I. The question asks whether to "invalidate the third-party doctrine"; the doctrine is the rationale; a holding that it no longer states the governing rule invalidates it. What distinguishes this from *Carpenter*-style trimming is the default (Resp. Q11) — the trim leaves the citizen losing every novel category until the next grant; the holding flips the default. That difference is relief obtainable *only* by answering QP I.

*Third*, Bench Q29. We will not pretend the answer away; we reaffirm it. But look at what the question stipulated: a forced choice between a *unanimous* narrow opinion and a *5–4* overruling "with a dissent inviting reversal." That is a question about the value of fragility, and any advocate who prefers the fragile opinion is grandstanding. It is not a concession that an overruling commanding a solid Court — the disposition we actually seek — is unwarranted, and the same answer said so in terms: "asked whether *Miller* should be overruled, we say yes, for the reasons briefed." The "squarely dispositive" clause describes the forced-choice world, where five votes are stipulated away and prudence counsels husbanding the question.

And the mirror: Respondent made the identical election. Its Q22: this case "is a poor vehicle for remaking third-party law *in either direction*." Its Bench Q10: if the Court merely *reserves* *Miller*'s fate, "the United States wins that case too, and says so." If pressing in the alternative forfeits QP I, Respondent has forfeited the reaffirmance it requests. Both parties briefed dispositions in the alternative; only one is being indicted for it.

## Rebuttal 5 — The attribution was wrong and is withdrawn; the vehicle never depended on it.

Candor first. Q16's sentence — "The Government argues the opposite at every step: that Morgan voluntarily conveyed his location" — was wrong as a description of Respondent's filed answers (Resp. What follows is far less than the challenge claims, because ring three's vehicle never rested on the Solicitor General's preferred theory. It rests on three things the withdrawal does not touch. **(1) The decision below.** The Thirteenth Circuit resolved the search question under a framework whose stated default is *Miller* — the opinion quotes the risk language as the doctrine that "continues to hold" — and the dissent would have overruled it. The judgment under review was produced by the doctrine; review of that judgment is review of the doctrine. **(2) The grant.** This Court directed the parties to brief whether to overturn *Miller*. **(3) Respondent itself.** Its Q22: "the question will be answered"; its Bench Q10: "the question presented requires an answer" — and the answer it requests is a merits disposition in its favor ("decline to overrule"). A question that one party asks the Court to answer "no" and the other "yes" is a live controversy on anyone's account. Respondent cannot demand a stare decisis holding for itself and call the same question, decided the other way, advisory. If answering QP I here is advisory, Respondent's requested reaffirmance falls by the same stroke (see our Challenge 11); if it is not — our position — both parties get their answer, and the dispute is over which one.

## Rebuttal 6 — Ring two's conclusion is now common ground; rings answering different questions do not "collapse"; and the pen-register consequence was accepted in writing, not evaded.

**Verdict: (a) wrong on collapse; (c) absorbed on the record point, which Q22 itself conceded.**

The record point first, briefly, because we conceded it before being challenged: the record does not establish GPS activation, and "we do not rest on any such finding" (Resp. Q22). "Pinged" is the record's word and we read it for its ordinary technical sense; if the Court reads it narrower, nothing changes, because of what follows.

The collapse charge confuses *facts shared* with *questions answered*. Ring one answers the *Katz* question: was the acquisition a search? Ring two answers the *defense* question: does the third-party doctrine independently defeat the expectation because the customer volunteered the information to a record-keeper? The same facts — government initiation, prospectivity — can establish the search and defeat the defense without the grounds merging, exactly as a single fact pattern can both prove a contract and defeat a statute-of-frauds defense. Ring two's independence was always independence from *ring three*: it lets the Court hold the doctrine inapplicable without deciding its validity — the minimalist exit, intact.

And here is what the challenge omits: **ring two is no longer contested.** Respondent's Q23: "No. The Government does not contend that Morgan voluntarily conveyed the responsive fixes." A ground the opposing party has stipulated cannot "collapse"; it has prevailed.

The proves-too-much point fails on its own premise that we "nowhere accept" the consequence. Resp. Q12 accepts it without hedging: *Smith* falls; prospective pen-register acquisition becomes warrant territory. As for "which process suffices" — that question was never assigned to agency theory. Agency answers *attribution*; *sufficiency* is answered by classification: chronicles require warrants (Resp. Q1; Bench Q18). No circle. And Congress itself already classifies prospective location monitoring as warrant territory, Fed. R. Crim. ## Rebuttal 7 — Expiration is not retroactive creation: the asymmetry is *Mincey*'s and *McNeely*'s, not ours.

**Verdict: (a) wrong** — and this is the challenge the case turns on, so we take it slowly.

The alleged ratchet: we measure justification continuously when it condemns the government (hiker, day three) and at inception when it would excuse it (October 10). But hold the two cases against the doctrine rather than against each other. The hiker stream was *lawfully initiated*; the question is its **scope**, and the scope rule is black-letter: a warrantless intrusion "must be strictly circumscribed by the exigencies which justify its **initiation**." *Mincey*, 437 U.S. at 393. Authority born of an exigency dies with the exigency — that is not episode-by-episode review; it is the boundary of a single authorization, the same way a warrant's authority ends at its expiration date. The October 8 stream was initiated on *no justification at all*; there was never any authority whose scope could be measured, and the question is whether a later-arising emergency can **create** authority for an acquisition the government set in motion in response to nothing. *Mincey*'s formula itself contains the asymmetry the challenge calls ours: the exigency that counts is the one justifying *initiation*. Authority can expire; it cannot spontaneously generate. A search begun without probable cause is not validated by probable cause arising mid-search, and Respondent's own answers concede the gap: "No decision of this Court adopts the per-arrival measure in terms" (Resp. Answers Q8), and the episode-justification theory "is an extension of *Karo*'s logic, not its holding" (Resp. Answers Q9). The one-way valve is built into *McNeely* and *Mincey*. We did not install it.

The "no act" point rests on conflating two roles the word "acquisition" plays. As a *threshold* matter, each obtaining of a fix is the constitutional event — which is why the October 10 fixes are searches at all, and why Respondent's framing of this point concedes our Part I. As an *excuse* matter, exigency justifies a governmental **undertaking** — a volitional act responsive to circumstances confronting the officer (*McNeely*: "when it undertakes the warrantless act"; *Brigham City*). On October 10 the government undertook nothing in response to the emergency: no request, no certification, no communication — Respondent's own concession ("a single fax on October 8 and nothing thereafter," Resp. Answers Q7). The fixes would have arrived identically had the rangers stayed home; an emergency that played no role in producing an acquisition cannot be the acquisition's justification. Doakes's accessing, inferring, and relaying that day are *use of fruits*, not fresh searches in need of excuse. There is no inconsistency in holding that every acquisition is a search and that the justification for proceeding without a warrant is assessed at the volitional undertaking that produced the acquisitions. That is what "the search's inception was the entire constitutional event" meant, and what the brief's "the government took none" meant: no responsive act an exigency could attach to.

## Rebuttal 8 — *Brigham City* removes motive, not knowledge; and record silence on exigency facts condemns the party who bears the exigency burden — the Government.

**Verdict: (a) wrong on the doctrine; (b) right about the silence, which is immaterial because the burden is Respondent's.**

The claimed contradiction dissolves once *Brigham City*'s objective test is stated precisely: the officer's subjective *motive* is irrelevant, and the inquiry runs on the *circumstances known* to the officers at the time. Objectivity strips intent from the analysis; it does not conjure knowledge into it. So we cite one case for two distinct components, both orthodoxly: Doakes's grudge is irrelevant (motive out — which, note, *helps* the Government), and the facts known when the material acquisitions occurred define the inquiry (knowledge in). *Devenpeck* and *McNeely* run on the same architecture. There is no head-by-head subjectivity in asking what law enforcement knew; that question *is* the objective test's input.

On the record: we accept that silence is silence — which is why our answers used the litigation-accurate formulation "no officer **is shown** to have" connected the discovery to the stream (Resp. Q25–Q26), a statement *about* the silence, not a negative finding. And here the challenge's burden allocation is backwards. Warrantless searches are presumptively unreasonable, and the *Government* bears the burden of proving the exception that excuses them. *Coolidge*, 403 U.S. at 455; *Welsh*, 466 U.S. at 749–50. The movant's burden is to show the warrantless search; it is then Respondent who must establish exigency's factual predicates. A silent record on those predicates defeats the *excuse*, not the motion. So yes — "the same discipline applies": contested predicates belong on remand, *under the burden of the party asserting them*, which for exigency, as for *Nix* and *Murray*, is the Government. That discipline is precisely what our Q25 demanded, and we accept its application here without amendment — noting only that the Court never reaches the knowledge question unless it first adopts the per-arrival framework that, by Respondent's own concessions, no decision of this Court supports (Rebuttal 7).

## Rebuttal 9 — One causal chain, two datasets: precision attribution and taint are different questions. The invented noun is withdrawn; the chain's operative link is record fact — and Respondent's own Q30 concedes the silence we are accused of exploiting.

**Verdict: (c) on the phrase "records request" — absorbed and restated; (a) on the alleged fork.**

The absorption: the record says only that Scuteri worked with "cell-site data **provided by** the wireless carrier"; it does not disclose when, how, or under what process — a silence Respondent's own Q30 concedes in terms ("the record does not disclose when or under what legal process the trial dataset was obtained, and the Government will not invent a chain of custody"). Neither will we. The chain in our Q28, item 6, is restated without the invented noun: fax → fixes → arrest → Morgan's identification as defendant → *the carrier's provision of his cell-site data for use at his trial*. Every link in that restated chain is record fact, and the load-bearing link was never the form of process — it is *why Morgan's number*: *Murray* asks whether the decision to acquire was prompted by the unlawful acquisition, and the maps were built for the trial of a defendant the tracking produced. Respondent's Q30 concedes that too: "the arrest the tracking produced is part of the causal history of the prosecution."

The fork is not a fork, because the two answers answer different questions. Q22's "different dataset" is about **precision attribution**: the quarter-mile figure quantifies the trial exhibits, not the stream. Q28's chain is about **causation**: a distinct dataset acquired *because* the tainted stream identified, located, and charged Morgan is classic derivative evidence, *Wong Sun* — two datasets, one taint chain. Distinctness defeats *conflation*, not *fruit*. Nothing forces an election, and we elect nothing: the datasets are two; the chain is one. (Our Part I, meanwhile, never needed the quarter-mile figure — the stream's functional precision is shown by record events: a moving car traced along State Road 9336 to a particular drive-thru.)

Preservation: Morgan moved to suppress the tracking; derivative-evidence questions travel with a suppressed search to the fruits stage — which is the remand *Respondent's own brief and Q30 request* ("the disposition is a fruits remand"). An unpreserved-fruits objection aimed at the very remand Respondent asks for refutes itself. And on Q29's counterfactual: we accept the symmetrical discipline — it is *Murray* argument, assigned to the forum we conceded, where the "untainted predicates" Respondent lists (the fixation, the profile) are *its* preponderance showing to make. We will stop pre-litigating the finding the day Respondent does.

## Rebuttal 10 — Category-threshold protection of an aggregate interest is how the Fourth Amendment always works — including in *Carpenter*, and including in Respondent's own convergence rule, which has the same "mismatch" plus an unknowable trigger.

**Verdict: (b) right that the rule triggers before any "whole" has accumulated, and right that exigent disclosures face after-the-fact review — both immaterial, because both describe every search doctrine this Court administers.**

The mismatch charge proves too much and lands on its author. *Katz* protects a single phone call though the interest is in private communication generally; the mails protect one sealed letter though no one letter is "the whole" of a correspondence; and *Carpenter itself* keyed its rule to a category-plus-threshold (CSLI, seven days) rather than to demonstrated invasion of any particular life's whole — the final fix on day seven reveals nothing the first fix on day one did not. Every rule protecting an aggregate interest must pick a trigger, and the choice is between a trigger the officer and magistrate can apply *ex ante* (category, first fix) and one nobody can locate until it is crossed. Respondent's convergence principle is the second kind, and Respondent has conceded both halves of why that fails: the officer "could not know in advance which fixes would cross that line" (Resp. Answers Q20), and at six days a court "may well find it crosses" (Resp. Answers, Bench Q1). The "mismatch" is symmetrical; the administrability is not.

The operational bill is the ordinary bill of exigency doctrine, and the middle band is its home turf. Exigency requires an objectively reasonable basis, not ironclad proof, *Michigan v. Fisher*, 558 U.S. 45, 49 (2009) (per curiam) — the unconfirmed welfare check and the 911 hang-up *are* exigency's daily diet, and the officer who enters a *home* on them today "learns whether he was right at the suppression hearing" too. No one calls him a felon of the Fourth Amendment; he is protected by the doctrine's reasonable-belief standard, and the carrier by §§ 2703(e), 2707(e). Respondent's alternative "resolves" the band only by stripping constitutional protection from everyone in it — and then rebuilds the identical litigation at the convergence line, where its own answers cannot say whether day six crosses. The fresh-pursuit officer under our rule has a search, a justification, and a stated standard. Under Respondent's he has no search until an unknowable day, and then a suppression hearing anyway.

## Rebuttal 11 — The fractured-circuits claim has names; classification at a stated rule's margin is adjudication; and counted fairly, Respondent's answers minted more sub-rules than ours.

**Verdict: (c) on the missing citations — absorbed and supplied; (a) on the substance.**

The omission was real, so here is the record the assertion rested on. Pole cameras: *United States v. Moore-Bush*, 36 F.4th 320 (1st Cir. 2022) (en banc) — the full court split evenly on whether months-long pole-camera surveillance is a search, producing affirmance without a rule; *United States v. Tuggle*, 4 F.4th 505 (7th Cir. 2021) — eighteen months upheld while the court lamented the absence of guidance and all but asked for it. Geofences: *United States v. Chatrie*, 107 F.4th 319 (4th Cir. 2024), taken en banc and resolved on *good faith alone* over a sheaf of competing opinions on the search question — a circuit unable to answer it choosing not to. Reverse keyword: *People v. Seymour*, 536 P.3d 1131 (Colo. 2023) (fractured). "Each court guessing whether *Carpenter* or *Miller* supplies the default" is those courts' own self-description, now cited.

The "five sub-rules" tally counts applications as amendments. The content overlay (Resp. Q6) is older than *Miller* — *Ex parte Jackson* — and sits identically in Respondent's own test (its Bench Q29, category (1)). Retrospective/prospective is the brief's architecture and *Carpenter*'s own line. The tower dump is not a sub-rule but standing arithmetic: a protected category, applied per person whose data is taken. ALPR is classification of a non-third-party surveillance method, outside the rule's domain (Rebuttal 3) — where Respondent's answers do the same work in the same words ("an accumulating mosaic of strangers, a different case," its Bench Q3). And the pen-register "override" does not exist (Rebuttal 1). Now count Respondent's round: a convergence principle announced in the answers rather than the brief; a seven-day ceiling that softens to "may well" at six; a "targeted" condition declared "necessary" (Bench Q1) and abandoned at the first tower dump (Bench Q3); a pattern-of-life override under which the summary is a search but its inputs are not (Bench Q5); plus the content and protected-space overlays it shares with everyone. If minting distinctions in sixty answers proves a regime is case-by-case discretion relabeled, Respondent loses the count it proposed. The difference between the regimes was never that ours has no frontier — every categorical rule has one. It is that ours states the rule the frontier is classified under, and the post-*Carpenter* regime, on the lower courts' own published account, does not.

## Conclusion

Respondent closed by saying Petitioner "is not entitled to both halves of any of these pairs." Agreed — and each rebuttal above states which half we keep: the magistrate's sentence (and *Smith* falls under it, not against it); the categorical line (and the tower dump's protection is its application, not its exception); the inception rule (with *Mincey*'s scope boundary, which was always inside it); two datasets and one taint chain; the *Katz* election; the forced-choice answer *with* the merits answer it sat beside. Where a half had to be surrendered, we surrendered it by name: the Q16 attribution, withdrawn; the "records request," restated; the missing citations, supplied; the commanded-generation finding, never claimed. What remains after the surrendering is the case we filed: a search at inception, no exigency that any responsive act ever invoked, a remedy the falsifier cannot escape, and a question presented that both parties — by their own filings — agree this Court must answer. The pairs still awaiting election are Respondent's: one unit of analysis for threshold and excuse, one dataset theory, one reading of the stipulation, one definition of "targeted," and one answer to whether seven days is a ceiling or upholstery. Our challenges stand; our answers, as corrected above, stand with them.
