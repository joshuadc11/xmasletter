# Petitioner's Questions to Respondent

**Morgan v. United States, No. 850-418 (October Term 2026) — Q&A Phase**

*Submitted by counsel for Petitioner Dexter Morgan. Respondent is required to answer each question in writing. Where a question calls for a yes-or-no answer, answer yes or no before explaining.*

---

## A. The October 8 Certification and the Stipulation

**Q1.** Yes or no: on October 8, when Detective Doakes signed and faxed the "Emergency Situation Disclosure" form, did any emergency involving danger of death or serious bodily injury to any person exist anywhere in this record?

**Q2.** Your brief states that "the Government does not defend Doakes's certification" — do you concede that the certification was false at the moment it was made?

**Q3.** State, with record citations, every objective fact known to any law enforcement officer on October 8 that could have supported a finding of exigent circumstances at the moment the warrantless acquisition was undertaken.

**Q4.** Given the parties' stipulation that the SCA's emergency exception does not apply, identify the record evidence that permits you to argue an "emergency" existed for Fourth Amendment purposes that did not exist for purposes of § 2702(b)(8) — a provision triggered by the very same danger-of-death standard.

**Q5.** If a neutral magistrate had been presented on October 8 with everything Doakes then knew, do you contend a warrant would have issued — and if so, state the probable cause and the crime it would have attached to.

## B. October 10: The Missing Governmental Act and the Severability Theory

**Q6.** Identify, with record citation, each affirmative act any government officer took on October 10 to *acquire* location data, as distinct from receiving fixes already flowing under the October 8 authorization.

**Q7.** Yes or no: between the October 8 fax and Morgan's arrest, did any officer transmit to Verizon any new request, certification, renewal, or communication of any kind?

**Q8.** State the rule: is the reasonableness of a warrantless acquisition measured by the circumstances confronting the officer when the warrantless act is undertaken, as *McNeely* holds, or by the circumstances prevailing whenever each datum happens to arrive — and cite the decision of this Court adopting the latter measure.

**Q9.** Quote the language in *United States v. Karo* holding that each transmission from a monitoring device is a discrete governmental *act* whose justification is separately assessed at the time of transmission, as opposed to parsing what an already-installed device *revealed*.

**Q10.** Under your episode-by-episode theory, were the October 8–9 fixes each separate unlawful searches — and if so, state why no taint attaches to the October 10 fixes that arrived under the same unrescinded, falsely procured authorization.

**Q11.** Your brief invites the Court to "assume the October 8–9 stream was unlawful": unlawful under what source of law, given your position that no fix was a search and that the SCA supplies the only nonconstitutional norm — and with what consequence?

**Q12.** If exigency can attach to passively arriving fixes whenever an emergency later ripens, state the limiting principle that prevents an officer from opening a falsely certified stream first and waiting for events to supply the justification.

**Q13.** Yes or no: do you concede that the authorization Doakes actually obtained — time frame "current," no end date — was a single open-ended request, and not a series of discrete daily requests?

## C. The "No Search" Position: Knotts, Karo, Hammond, and Riley

**Q14.** Yes or no: had Doakes instead obtained the identical several days of location fixes as *historical* records on October 11, would *Carpenter* govern that acquisition? If yes, state the principle under which the same multi-day record of the same movements is protected when compiled yesterday but unprotected when compiled in real time as it accumulates.

**Q15.** Your brief says *Carpenter*'s engine was the "retrospective archive": by October 10, Doakes held days of accumulated movements — at what point, if ever, does a running real-time stream become the archive you concede is protected?

**Q16.** State the maximum duration of warrantless, open-ended real-time tracking your theory permits — a week, a month, indefinitely — and identify the doctrinal source of that limit.

**Q17.** Name one federal appellate decision holding that a multi-day, open-ended real-time location stream — as opposed to *Hammond*'s and *Riley*'s hours-long fugitive locates — is not a Fourth Amendment search.

**Q18.** *Riley* involved phone-GPS-grade data, yet your brief insists this record shows only tower-scale, quarter-mile accuracy: state which precision characterization the United States adopts for this case, and whether your answer changes which precedents govern.

**Q19.** Your brief argues the Fourth Amendment "judges what the Government actually acquired, not what a form might have permitted": is the lawfulness of a wiretap or a warrant likewise judged solely by what it happened to capture rather than by the scope of its authorization — and cite your authority.

**Q20.** Yes or no: under your *Karo* actual-revelation theory, if the overnight October 9 fixes had placed Morgan's phone inside his home, those fixes were searches — and if so, did Doakes have any way of knowing in advance which fixes in his open-ended stream would be lawful?

**Q21.** Your brief warns that Petitioner's rule would impede locating an abducted child's phone: do you concede that a genuine abduction satisfies the exigent-circumstances exception, and if so, state what work your no-search holding does that the exigency exception does not already do.

## D. Miller, Carpenter, and What the Third-Party Doctrine Still Does

**Q22.** Your brief states that where the surveillance revealed public movement, "the conveyance debate is academic": if the third-party doctrine does no independent work on these facts, state the Court's warrant for *reaffirming Miller* in this case rather than reserving the question for a case in which the doctrine matters.

**Q23.** Yes or no: do you contend Morgan "voluntarily conveyed" to Verizon the location fixes generated in response to Doakes's request — and if yes, identify Morgan's affirmative act of conveyance.

**Q24.** *Carpenter* holds that CSLI is not "shared" in any meaningful sense because the phone logs location "without any affirmative act on the part of the user beyond powering up": explain how prospective fixes the user cannot perceive, refuse, or avoid are *more* voluntarily conveyed than the historical CSLI *Carpenter* protected.

**Q25.** State the rule that tells the next court whether a record sought from a third party falls on the *Miller* side or the *Carpenter* side of the line — and apply it to a carrier's prospective location stream.

**Q26.** Your brief attacks Petitioner's chronicle/ledger rule on the ground that "nearly every consumer record is both at once": does that objection apply equally to *Carpenter*'s own distinction between protected CSLI and unprotected "ordinary business records" — and if not, state why not.

## E. Remedy, § 2708, and the Trial Record

**Q27.** Yes or no: do you concede that § 2708's exclusivity clause is limited by its own text to "nonconstitutional violations" of the chapter, and therefore supplies no answer to suppression if this Court holds a Fourth Amendment search occurred?

**Q28.** Identify the decision of this Court extending *Leon*/*Davis* good-faith treatment to an officer who himself falsified the predicate for the very warrantless process he invoked.

**Q29.** Your deterrence argument is that on October 10 police "could have" obtained the same data lawfully "within minutes": is that anything other than an inevitable-discovery or independent-source claim — and if it is such a claim, do you concede its factual predicates must be proven on remand under *Nix* and *Murray* rather than assumed here?

**Q30.** Your brief asserts the trial maps "rested on historical cell-site records distinct from the real-time stream": cite the record evidence establishing that this is a distinct dataset, state when and under what legal process it was obtained, and explain why it is not the fruit of an arrest produced solely by the real-time tracking.
