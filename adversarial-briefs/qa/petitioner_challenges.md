# Petitioner's Challenges to Respondent's Answers

**Dexter Morgan v. United States, No. 850-418 (October Term 2026) — Q&A Phase, Challenge Round**

*Submitted by counsel for Petitioner Dexter Morgan.*

---

## Preamble: What We Do Not Challenge — and Why That Matters More Than What We Do

Respondent answered candidly in many places, and Petitioner does not challenge candor. But concessions made in writing in this phase are now part of the case, and Petitioner gives notice that he will hold Respondent to every one of them at argument and on any remand. The following admissions stand unchallenged and are, in Petitioner's submission, now locked in:

1. **October 8 is indefensible on every theory.** No emergency existed anywhere in the record; the certification was "false when made"; there were no exigency facts; "Doakes had suspicion and obsession, not probable cause as to Morgan"; and no magistrate would or should have issued a warrant. (Answers to Pet. Q1–Q3, Q5.)
2. **No governmental act after the fax.** "The record reflects a single fax on October 8 and nothing thereafter" — no new request, certification, renewal, or communication of any kind; the authorization was "one open-ended request." (Pet. Q6–Q7, Q13.)
3. **Voluntariness is surrendered.** Morgan did not voluntarily convey the fixes; "knowing exposure" is "surrender[ed] . . . as to this category of data"; voluntariness for carrier-generated fixes is "indefensible after Carpenter." (Pet. Q23–Q24; Bench Q8, Q10–Q11.)
4. **§ 2708 is no answer to constitutional suppression.** (Pet. Q27.)
5. **No good faith.** The good-faith exception is never invoked, and *Leon* itself withholds it from the officer whose falsehood procured the process. (Pet. Q28.)
6. **Maximum culpability.** Doakes's fabricated certification "sits at the top of" *Herring*'s culpability scale. (Bench Q22.)
7. **The statutory remedy is unproven.** No reported § 2707 false-certification case exists; carrier auditing is "sparse"; the remedial road is "thinner than we would wish"; the "other means to punish" argument is to be weighed "at that discounted value." (Bench Q16, Q19–Q20.)
8. **Footnote 3 is no ratchet.** The Court may rule for Petitioner on real-time data "without disturbing a single precedent." (Bench Q7.)
9. **If a search occurred, October 8–9 was dozens of discrete violations**, and any post-arrest fixes were unlawful too. (Bench Q14–Q15.)
10. **No duration-matched authority supports the Government.** No federal appellate decision holds a multi-day, open-ended stream is not a search. (Pet. Q17.)
11. **A narrow warrant rule costs the Government nothing.** No enforcement program fails if commanded prospective tracking requires a warrant; the United States loses "not much, in the run of cases." (Bench Q25, Q27.)
12. ***Nix*/*Murray* predicates require proof on remand**, "not assumed at this lectern." (Pet. Q29.)
13. **The convergence principle is accepted and "load-bearing throughout."** (Bench Q2.)

These concessions are not challenged because they are correct. The challenges below are directed at the places where Respondent has not accepted the *consequences* of its own concessions, has contradicted its brief or its other answers, has misread the record, or has dressed a remand request as a defense of the judgment.

## The Challenges

### Challenge 1 — The convergence principle contradicts the brief's entire Part II theory. [Answers challenged: prefatory framework ¶(3); Bench Q2; Pet. Q14–Q16; Bench Q6]

**Defect: contradiction with the brief; unaccepted consequence of a concession.**

The brief argued that *Carpenter*'s engine was the *retrospective* archive and that a prospective stream categorically lacks its features: "A prospective, targeted location fix has none of those features. It cannot reconstruct the past . . . the rationale does not translate, and the reservation would have been pointless if real-time tracking were covered *a fortiori*." (Resp. Br. Part II.A.) The Q&A abandons that position. The convergence principle — "a real-time stream becomes a search no later than the point at which its accumulated fixes would, if sought as historical records, constitute the archive *Carpenter* . . . protects" — concedes that **accumulation, not retrospectivity, is the constitutional variable**, and says so in terms: "once the government holds the encyclopedic record, it matters not whether it arrived in one production or in thirty-minute increments" (Bench Q2); "[t]he direction of assembly changes nothing" (Pet. Q14).

That is Petitioner's *a fortiori* argument, adopted with a delay timer attached. Once direction of assembly "changes nothing," the brief's only remaining distinction between this case and *Carpenter* is duration — and on duration Respondent concedes (Pet. Q15) that "footnote 3 *reserved rather than blessed* the sub-seven-day zone, so the Court could draw a shorter line." Respondent's whole no-search case has thus contracted, between brief and answers, from "*Carpenter*'s rationale does not translate to real-time data" to "this particular stream was three days, and we hope the Court draws the line above three." A position that needs the Court to fix an unset dial in its favor is not a defense of the judgment; it is a request. And note what the concession does to the brief's own facts: by October 10 the "prospective" stream that supposedly "cannot reconstruct the past" *was* a retrospective archive of Morgan's preceding three days, sitting in Doakes's hands.

### Challenge 2 — Convergence (an aggregate threshold) cannot coexist with the episode theory (a divisible threshold). Respondent must choose, and either choice forfeits a Part of its case. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q9 against Bench Q2 and Pet. Q15]

**Defect: internal contradiction between answers.**

In Pet. Q9, defending episode-by-episode exigency, Respondent reasons: "a search occurs, if at all, when protected information is acquired, and *Karo* establishes that acquisition through a continuous device is divisible — some episodes searches, some not. **If the unit of analysis is divisible for the threshold question, it is divisible for the reasonableness question.**" But the convergence principle (Bench Q2; Pet. Q15) determines the threshold question by *welding the episodes together*: no individual fix on day seven reveals anything different from a fix on day one; the stream "becomes a Carpenter search" only because the accumulated whole equals the archive. So Respondent's threshold analysis is aggregative when answering the duration questions and disaggregative when answering the exigency questions — the precise "welds the episodes together for one inquiry and severs them for the other" move its Q9 answer disclaims.

The fork is closed at both ends. If the unit of analysis is **divisible**, the convergence cap dissolves — each later fix, taken alone, reveals only a public position, so nothing ever converges, and the answers to Pet. Q16 and Bench Q6 ("Not indefinite") were false comfort: the theory permits exactly the indefinite tracking the bench asked about. If the unit is **aggregative**, then the stream is one undertaking, *McNeely* (which Respondent "accept[s] without qualification," Pet. Q8) dates its reasonableness to its inception on October 8 — a day Respondent concedes is "indefensible on every theory" (Pet. Q5) — and Part III's per-fix exigency severance fails on Respondent's own logic. Respondent's rebuttal must pick a unit of analysis and keep it for both inquiries.

### Challenge 3 — The seven-day line is asserted as a ceiling and disavowed the moment it is applied. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q16; Bench Q1; Bench Q6; with Pet. Q20]

**Defect: evasion; internally contradictory standard.**

Asked for the maximum duration its theory permits, Respondent answers: the convergence principle "caps warrantless real-time acquisition at, at most, the historical line — **seven days** on *Carpenter*'s holding" (Pet. Q16). Asked about the six-day case, Respondent answers that six days "presses against the seven-day line and a court applying the convergence principle **may well find it crosses**" (Bench Q1). Both cannot be true. Six is less than seven; under the announced metric the six-day stream does not cross, and Respondent should say so. The "may well" reveals that the seven-day figure is not Respondent's rule but its advocacy posture: a line firm enough to be cited as a limiting principle when the bench fears limitlessness, and soft enough to be abandoned when applying it produces an unattractive answer. A "ceiling" that gives way in the first hypothetical is not a doctrinal source; it is upholstery.

The defect compounds with Bench Q1's chosen metric: the search question is measured by "the duration and density of the record the government **ends up holding**" — an ex post test — while Respondent concedes (Pet. Q20) that the officer "could not know in advance which fixes would cross that line." On Respondent's framework, the constitutional character of the surveillance is fixed retroactively by how long the police take to find their man. The next Doakes opens his open-ended stream knowing it is no search unless he fails for a week.

### Challenge 4 — Exigency without an act: an admitted extension of *Karo*, resting on no holding, applied to a day on which the Government concedes it did nothing. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q6, Q8, Q9; with Pet. Q21; Resp. Br. Part III]

**Defect: concession whose consequence is not accepted; misstatement of law by extension.**

Respondent's own answers dismantle Part III piece by piece, and then announce that the Part still stands. The pieces: (1) "the record shows **no new communication to Verizon on October 10**" (Pet. Q6); (2) *McNeely*'s rule — reasonableness measured "when it undertakes the warrantless act" — is "accept[ed] . . . without qualification," and "**[n]o decision of this Court adopts the per-arrival measure in terms**" (Pet. Q8); (3) *Karo* contains no language treating each transmission as a separately justified act — "There is no such language" — and the episode-justification theory "is **an extension of *Karo*'s logic, not its holding**" (Pet. Q9). What remains after those three concessions is a doctrine Respondent itself defines as an *excuse*: "exigency is an *excuse* for a search" (Pet. Q21). An excuse presupposes an act to be excused. On October 10 the Government, by its own concession, *acted* not at all — the fixes arrived automatically under the October 8 authorization, procured by a falsehood, never renewed, never rescinded.

Respondent's fallback in Q6 — "receipt and use of the information *is* the constitutionally significant acquisition" — proves too much for its own case: if passive receipt is the constitutionally significant event, then each October 8–9 receipt was equally significant, the "acquisition" was continuous from the moment the fax was honored, and the only volitional governmental conduct anywhere in the chain remains the October 8 fax, which fails every standard Respondent has conceded. The Court should hold Respondent to its own arithmetic: McNeely accepted + no act on October 10 + no holding supporting per-arrival assessment = Part III has no doctrinal vehicle, only an invitation to create one for this case.

### Challenge 5 — The stipulation covers "the data collected," not the October 8 form — and Respondent's "two days, not two standards" reading rewrites the record. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q4; Bench Q18]

**Defect: misstatement of the record; contradiction between the identity-of-standards concession and the constitutional-emergency claim.**

Respondent reads the stipulation as "a stipulation about that October 8 request" only (Pet. Q4), so that October 10's facts may satisfy the constitutional emergency standard untouched. The record says otherwise. The opinion below recites: "Both parties agree today that (1) **the data collected by Sergeant Doakes** is within the scope of that which is covered by the SCA and (2) that i[t] does not fall under the good faith exception." The stipulated subject is *the data* — all of it, which necessarily includes the October 10 fixes. And on Respondent's own episode theory, each arriving fix was a separate disclosure by Verizon; the stipulation says none of those disclosures falls within the emergency exception.

Now add Respondent's Bench Q18 concession: asked what daylight it stands in between the statute's danger-of-death standard and the constitutional one, Respondent answered "**None** — and that is precisely our answer. . . . The substantive standards are aligned." Identical standards, stipulated failure as to the very disclosures at issue. *Virginia v. Moore* protects the statute/Constitution distinction where the two norms *diverge*; it has nothing to offer where the Government concedes they are the same. Respondent cannot honor the stipulation "fully" (its phrase) while arguing that the same disclosures, measured by a standard it concedes is identical, succeeded constitutionally. Either the stipulation means what the opinion says it covers — in which case the October 10 disclosures flunk the aligned standard by agreement — or Respondent is asking this Court to relieve it of a stipulation it now finds inconvenient. The rebuttal should say which.

### Challenge 6 — The "lawful paths" of October 10 lead to no phone number: every counterfactual presupposes probable cause as to *Morgan* that only the tainted stream supplied. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q12 (third limit), Pet. Q29; Bench Q13, Q20, Q21; Resp. Br. Part IV ("within minutes")]

**Defect: circularity; unsupported factual premise running through four answers.**

Respondent's deterrence architecture rests on one proposition, stated as its third "interlocking limit": "the post-emergency yield is only what a *truthful* emergency request made that day would lawfully have produced — **the falsification purchases zero evidentiary advantage over honesty**" (Pet. Q12; same premise in Bench Q13's draft paragraph, Bench Q20 ("lawful access existed and exclusion deters nothing"), and Bench Q21).

Walk the truthful officer through October 10. The rangers found a scene and *no suspect*: the perpetrator "had fled and was unlocated." The untainted record connecting that scene to Dexter Morgan, specifically, is — nothing. Respondent concedes that as of October 8 "Doakes had suspicion and obsession, not probable cause as to Morgan" (Pet. Q5), and identifies no intervening untainted fact. The FBI's profile pointed at Miami Metro's homicide department generally, not at Morgan. So the honest officer faxing a truthful certification that morning has a genuine emergency and **no phone number to put on the form**. A "truthful emergency request made that day" produces fixes on Morgan only if someone already had reason to track *Morgan* — and on this record the only thing supplying that reason is the falsely procured stream itself, which had been silently pre-positioned on him for two days. Far from purchasing "zero evidentiary advantage," the falsification purchased the only thing that mattered: targeting, before any lawful basis for it existed.

Respondent has already conceded the predicate is unproven: Pet. Q29 lists "probable cause as of October 10" among the facts that must "be found below, not assumed at this lectern." Yet the same answer reserves the *Herring* "marginal deterrence" weighing — which turns on the *genuine availability* of those same lawful avenues — "for this Court." That is the identical unproven fact, claimed twice: once as a remand question (conceded) and once as an appellate premise (assumed). The rebuttal cannot keep both.

### Challenge 7 — The "four interlocking limits" operate only in the world where the Government has already lost. On its lead theory, the open-first officer's scheme is cost-free. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q12; Bench Q13; with Bench Q23 and Bench Q21]

**Defect: internal contradiction between the limiting principle and the primary submission.**

Respondent's answer to the open-the-stream-and-wait hypothetical begins: "First, every pre-emergency acquisition remains unlawful — statutorily always, **and suppressibly so if a search**." Note the conditional. Respondent's *primary* submission is that none of this is a search; and its position on statutory violations is categorical: "the exclusionary rule **never applies** . . . we hold it categorically" (Bench Q23). Run the hypothetical on Respondent's own lead theory, then: the officer falsely certifies; the pre-emergency yield is admissible (no search, statutory violation only, no suppression ever); the post-emergency yield is admissible (exigency or no-search); and the officer's "personal exposure" is a § 2707 action of which, Respondent concedes, **not one reported example exists in the statute's history** (Bench Q20), audited by a system whose review is "sparse" (Bench Q16). Every limit in the Q12 answer either presupposes the search holding Respondent resists or relies on a deterrent it concedes is unproven. The Bench Q13 paragraph — "the pre-emergency yield is unusable and actionable" — is simply false within Respondent's own framework: *unusable* only if a search (denied), *actionable* only in theory (conceded).

Respondent's Bench Q21 answer completes the lesson plan it claims to avoid, conceding that on its theory "suppression was denied *here* only because the material evidence **coincided** with a genuine emergency and an independently discovered crime scene." Coincided. The next Doakes reads that sentence and learns exactly what the bench feared: open the stream, wait, and hope for coincidence — the house pays out and has never once collected.

### Challenge 8 — After the Q&A concessions, Part IV's headline is gone: "in all events suppression is not the remedy" has become "in all events, a remand." [Answers challenged: Pet. Q27–Q30; Bench Q19, Q20, Q22, taken together; Resp. Br. Part IV]

**Defect: accumulated concessions whose aggregate consequence Respondent has not accepted — a right without a remedy, and a defense of the judgment that no longer defends it.**

Tally what survives of Part IV after Respondent's own answers. § 2708: "supplies no answer" if a search is found (Pet. Q27). Good faith: never invoked, unavailable to a falsifier (Pet. Q28). Culpability: "the top of" *Herring*'s scale — "the core case for excluding the October 8–9 fruits" (Bench Q22). The lawful-paths point: "sounds in inevitable discovery and independent source," predicates "found below, not assumed" (Pet. Q29). The trial maps: "the record does not disclose when or under what legal process the trial dataset was obtained, and the Government will not invent a chain of custody," and "the arrest the tracking produced is part of the causal history of the prosecution" (Pet. Q30). The statutory deterrent: zero reported cases, to be weighed "at that discounted value" (Bench Q20); the whole remedial road "thinner than we would wish" (Bench Q19).

What is left that this Court could *affirm on*? Nothing. Every surviving strand of Part IV is, by Respondent's own description, a remand question — and Respondent says so: its "stated ceiling" is "a remand for fruits-and-harmlessness analysis" (Pet. Q29, Q30; Bench Q21). But a ceiling that is also the floor is the position. The Court should treat Part IV, as reconstructed in these answers, as what it now is: a concession that if a search is found, the judgment below cannot stand and the case goes back. Respondent should not be permitted to argue "affirm" from a Part whose every operative component it has converted into "remand." And the bench's Q19 question stands unanswered beneath the candor: a right enforced by a statute never once used, against officers *Egbert* immunizes, on behalf of victims who must first be prosecuted to have standing to object, is a right without a remedy — which is not a reason to withhold the one remedy that exists; it is the classic reason to apply it.

### Challenge 9 — "Attenuation" mislabeled: the rangers' discovery intervened in nothing. [Answer challenged: Pet. Q10]

**Defect: misstatement of law.**

Respondent argues the October 10 fixes are not tainted because "the intervening, wholly independent event — the rangers' discovery of an in-progress murder — is exactly the kind of changed circumstance that attenuation and exigency doctrine recognize." That misdescribes attenuation. Under *Brown v. Illinois* — which Respondent itself cites — an intervening circumstance attenuates taint when it **breaks the causal chain** between the illegality and the evidence (the paradigm: a defendant's independent act of free will). The chain here is fax → stream → October 10 fixes: direct, short, mechanical, and *entirely unaffected* by what the rangers found. The discovery did not cause, interrupt, redirect, or contribute to the acquisition of a single fix; the fixes would have arrived that day if the rangers had stayed home. An event that is causally external to the acquisition but would have supplied a hypothetical *alternative justification* for it is not attenuation — it is the vocabulary of independent source and inevitable discovery, which Respondent has already conceded (Pet. Q29) carries factual burdens to be discharged on remand under *Nix* and *Murray*. The Q10 answer is the Q29 concession wearing a different doctrinal label to avoid the burden of proof that travels with its real name. (And the flagrancy factor Respondent concedes "would weigh in Morgan's favor" is, per its Bench Q22 answer, at the *top* of the scale.)

### Challenge 10 — The quarter-mile fork: one dataset or two? Each answer forfeits an argument. [Answers challenged: Pet. Q18 against Pet. Q30; Resp. Br. Statement & Part IV]

**Defect: internal contradiction; record assertion conceded to be unsupported.**

In Pet. Q18, Respondent characterizes the *real-time stream* as tower-scale: "the only quantified accuracy anywhere in this record is the trial expert's 'within a quarter mile.'" In Pet. Q30, defending the trial maps from taint, Respondent insists those maps were "a retrospective reconstruction of past position" built from a dataset *distinct from* the real-time stream — while conceding "the record does not disclose when or under what legal process the trial dataset was obtained." Both answers cannot stand. If the trial dataset is **distinct** from the stream (Q30), then the quarter-mile figure quantifies only the trial exhibits, and Respondent's precision characterization of the stream (Q18) rests on *nothing in the record* — against the functional evidence that the stream was precise enough to trace a moving car along State Road 9336, infer a direction of travel, and deliver agents to a particular drive-thru. If the quarter-mile figure **does** describe the stream, then the datasets are one, and the brief's third "independent" ground — "the trial maps rested on historical cell-site records distinct from the real-time stream" — collapses into the tainted stream itself. Respondent must elect a dataset theory; under either election, one of its arguments is gone. And in all events, Q30's candor confirms that the brief's "distinct dataset" assertion was made without record support — a representation the rebuttal should either substantiate with a citation or withdraw.

### Challenge 11 — The *Miller* Respondent defends is not the *Miller* on the books: its rule of decision is reclassified as "dictum," and the requested "reaffirmance" is, by Respondent's own definition, advisory. [Answers challenged: Bench Q24, Q12, Q4, Q10; Pet. Q22; Resp. Br. Conclusion]

**Defect: misstatement of precedent; contradiction between brief and answers; unaccepted consequence of the "advisory" concession.**

Asked whether *Miller*'s reasoning is correct on its own terms in 2026, Respondent answers "no as to the maximal dictum the question quotes" — disclaiming the proposition that disclosure for a limited purpose forfeits protection. But that proposition is not dictum; it is *Miller*'s rule of decision: the depositor "takes the risk, in revealing his affairs to another, that the information will be conveyed by that person to the Government . . . even if the information is revealed on the assumption that it will be used for a limited purpose and the confidence placed in the third party will not be betrayed." 425 U.S. at 443. It is the holding that decided *Smith*, and it is the very language the Thirteenth Circuit quotes below *as the doctrine* Respondent asks this Court to reaffirm. In its place, Bench Q12 installs a "documents leg" as "*Miller*'s actual core" — a rationale assembled in 2026 for a precedent decided in 1976. A precedent retained only after its operative rationale is reclassified as dictum and replaced is not being reaffirmed; it is being rewritten — which is Petitioner's request with the Government's label pasted over it.

The contradiction with the brief is verbal. The brief's conclusion: "The Court should **reaffirm** *United States v. Miller*." The answers: the doctrine is "**not load-bearing for this judgment**," and overruling it here "would change nothing about this record. That is the definition of an **advisory** overruling" (Bench Q4); and Respondent will happily accept an opinion "expressly reserving *Miller*'s long-term fate" — "the United States wins that case too, and says so" (Bench Q10). Respondent cannot have it both ways: if a disposition that does no work on this record is "advisory" when it runs against the Government, a reaffirmance that does no work on this record is equally advisory when it runs for it. By Respondent's own logic, the only non-advisory things this Court can say about *Miller* on these facts are the things Petitioner asked it to say about the doctrine's *rationale* — which Respondent now declines to defend.

### Challenge 12 — "Targeted" is announced as a necessary condition and abandoned one question later. [Answers challenged: Bench Q1 against Bench Q3]

**Defect: internal contradiction; the proposed holding's limiting language does no work.**

Bench Q1's dictated holding makes targeting load-bearing: "'Targeted' (one identified suspect, on a specific request) is a **necessary condition**, answering *Knotts*'s dragnet reservation." Two questions later, asked about a tower dump — thousands of unsuspected bystanders, targeted at no one — Respondent answers that it "is not a search as to any individual" (Bench Q3). If wholly untargeted, population-scale acquisition is no search, then "targeted" was never a condition of anything; it was a reassurance word inserted into the holding to soothe the dragnet concern and discarded the first time the dragnet appeared. The defect matters because *Knotts*'s reservation is the precise doctrinal pressure point Respondent's no-search theory must survive: its proposed opinion answers the reservation with a condition its own answers treat as optional. The rebuttal should state whether targeting is an element of the no-search holding or not — and if it is, give the tower-dump answer that element requires.

### Challenge 13 — The "front-line teachers" of Respondent's deterrence theory are, by Respondent's own concessions, truants. [Answers challenged: Bench Q13 against Bench Q16, Q17, Q20]

**Defect: contradiction across answers; doctrine calibrated to safeguards conceded inoperative.**

Bench Q13 closes: "the front-line teacher in this statutory scheme is **the carrier's gatekeeping under § 2702 and the falsifier's personal exposure** — suppression litigation was never the system's only classroom." Respondent's other answers expel both teachers. The carrier's gatekeeping: "Yes, Verizon should have done differently, and the Government will not pretend this is the system working as intended" (Bench Q17) — the gate, on this record, was a checkbox honored for days without verification, particulars, or endpoint. The falsifier's personal exposure: no reported § 2707 case in the statute's four-decade history (Bench Q20), against an auditing regime "so far as we are aware, sparse" (Bench Q16), down a remedial road Respondent itself calls "thinner than we would wish" (Bench Q19). Respondent's answer to all of this is that Congress "can rebuild [the gate] next session" (Bench Q30). Petitioner will take that as the admission it is: the safeguards are broken *this* session, in *this* case, as they were in October 2007 — and a constitutional doctrine may not be calibrated to the future diligence of a legislature whose existing scheme both parties agree was defeated by one pen-stroke. The system that is to teach the next Doakes has, on Respondent's own account, never taught anyone anything.

### Challenge 14 — The wearable gets categorical protection from its first heartbeat; the location stream waits seven days. The asymmetry has no principle behind it. [Answer challenged: Bench Q28, against the convergence framework of Pet. Q15–Q16]

**Defect: inconsistent classification method within Respondent's own framework.**

In Bench Q28, Respondent classifies a wearable's biometric stream as "protected — a continuous record of the body's functioning is a comprehensive involuntary chronicle on *Carpenter*'s side of the line, with depth exceeding location data." Protected *as a category* — no accrual period, no convergence wait, no suggestion that the first six days of heartbeat data are fair game until the archive ripens. Yet for the location stream — the *paradigm* of *Carpenter*'s protected interest, "the whole of [one's] physical movements" — Respondent's framework supplies a seven-day free-fire zone in which accumulation is constitutionally invisible. Respondent cannot explain why the record *Carpenter* actually protected is classified volumetrically while a record *Carpenter* never addressed is classified categorically, except that this case happens to involve the former at day three. If categories with "depth exceeding location data" earn protection from the first datum, location's claim to the same treatment is *a fortiori* under Respondent's own depth metric; if instead everything waits for convergence, Respondent should tell the Court — and the wearable's wearer — how many days of heartbeats the Government may take for free. Either answer is fine doctrine; holding both is advocacy sorted by docket number.

## Conclusion

Respondent's answers are candid in detail and contradictory in structure: a convergence principle that concedes Petitioner's premise while contradicting the brief and the episode theory both; an exigency defense for a day on which the Government admittedly did nothing, built on an admitted extension of *Karo*; a stipulation re-read against the record that recites it; lawful-path counterfactuals that presuppose the very probable cause the tainted stream supplied; and a remedial Part whose every component Respondent has now converted into a remand question while still asking for affirmance. Petitioner respectfully submits that the rebuttal must do more than restate the framework — it must choose: one unit of analysis, one dataset theory, one reading of the stipulation, one definition of "targeted," and one honest answer to whether the seven-day line is a rule or a costume.
