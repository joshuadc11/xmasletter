# Respondent's Challenges to Petitioner's Answers

**Dexter Morgan v. United States, No. 850-418 — Q&A Phase, Challenge Round**

*Submitted by the Office of the Solicitor General, counsel for Respondent United States.*

## Preamble: The Answers We Do Not Challenge — and What They Now Cost Petitioner

The Government does not challenge every answer it dislikes. Several of Petitioner's answers are candid, legally sound, and unfavorable to him — and because they were given in writing, in a phase whose premise is that answers bind, the Government's first submission is that the following concessions are now **locked in** and will be held against Petitioner for the remainder of this case:

1. **Smith v. Maryland is overruled outright** under his rule (Answer to Resp. Q12).
2. **Pharmacy prescription histories, hotel registries, credit-card statements, and brokerage records are unprotected "ledgers" at any volume** — one year, five years, forever (Answers to Resp. Q2–Q5, Q18).
3. **Miller itself comes out the same way** under the successor rule (Resp. Q9), and **Petitioner can cite no case in this Court's history that "overruled" a precedent while leaving its judgment intact as good law** (Resp. Q11).
4. **A Part I holding gives Morgan complete relief without deciding Miller's fate** (Resp. Q14), and constitutional avoidance *permits* the Court to stop there (Resp. Q15).
5. **A truthful emergency certification on the morning of October 10 would have made the same fixes lawful** (Resp. Q24).
6. **The Everglades crime scene and Estrada's body are untainted** — a genuine independent source, conceded "without reluctance" (Resp. Q30).
7. **The new rule does not apply on collateral review, and Davis good faith secures every conviction built on pre-decision subpoenas** (Bench Q7) — the retroactivity wing of his own reliance answer, now unavailable to walk back.
8. **A single "which tower, right now?" ping in fresh pursuit is a Fourth Amendment search**, with no de minimis exception (Bench Q22; Bench Q18: "a single exigent ping is a search too").
9. **All three Knotts propositions are accurate**: government-installed beeper, no voluntary conveyance by anyone, no search (Resp. Q19).
10. **In the hiker hypothetical, "continued receipt is an ongoing warrantless acquisition" whose lawfulness is judged by its** ***current*** **justification** (Bench Q26).
11. **Given the forced choice, a unanimous narrow opinion leaving Miller untouched is the better disposition — "for the Constitution too"** (Bench Q29).

The Government also declines to challenge answers that are merely adverse but sound: the content classification of cloud-stored footage (Resp. Q6), the Katz-over-positive-law election and its § 2702-repeal corollary (Bench Q10–Q11), and the attribution/liability distinction (Bench Q15). Those answers are correctly reasoned, and challenging them would waste the Court's time.

What follows are the answers that are not sound — because they contradict the brief, contradict each other, misstate the record or the Government's actual position, or make concessions whose consequences Petitioner refuses to carry.

**Challenge 1 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q1, Q7, and Q12 — the pen register flunks Petitioner's own magistrate sentence]**

*Defect: internal contradiction between the stated rule and its flagship application.*

Petitioner's one-sentence rule for the magistrate (Resp. Q1) defines the ledger as "a transaction the customer affirmatively conducted *with* the business — the business's performance of its side of the bargain," and the chronicle as "conduct in the world that the business merely observed and logged." His Answer to Resp. Q4 adds the operative signature: "Voluntary dealing with the record-keeper is the ledger's signature," illustrated by the pharmacy customer who "handed the pharmacist a prescription."

Now apply that sentence to *Smith*. The caller hands the carrier a number. The carrier does not "merely observe" his calls; it *performs* them — switching, connecting, billing, the carrier's side of the bargain, which is precisely the reasoning *Smith* itself gave. Under Petitioner's own magistrate sentence, a pen-register log is the paradigm ledger. Yet his Answer to Resp. Q12 declares: "Yes. A pen-register log is a chronicle of the customer's communications — its entire content is whom he called and when," and *Smith* "falls." His escape in Q7 — "a pen register log memorializes nothing *except* whom he called" — is available verbatim to every record he classifies the other way: a pharmacy log memorializes nothing except what he ingests; a hotel registry memorializes nothing except where, and with whom, he slept. Petitioner is running two criteria — "transaction with the record-keeper" and "the record's entire content is the activity" — that give opposite answers for the doctrine's second-most-important case, and he resolves the conflict by fiat in whichever direction the question requires.

*Why it matters:* The entire case for overruling rather than retaining turns on whether the successor rule is administrable. A rule that cannot classify *Smith* — the doctrine's own companion case — without overriding its stated test is the Government's workability objection conceded. And note the makeweight in Q12: the claim that a pen register is "also surveillance under our Part I logic" contradicts Petitioner's own dictated Part I holding (Bench Q18), which is expressly confined to "prospective monitoring of *a cell phone's location*." The holdings expand and contract by the question asked.

**Challenge 2 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q3, Q4, Q5 with Bench Q21 and Bench Q30 — the pharmacy gap, defended with the very answer Petitioner ridicules from the Government]**

*Defect: contradiction with the brief's rationale, plus a double standard on legislative calibration.*

Petitioner's brief sells the overruling with a parade of intimacy: location data exposes "familial, political, professional, religious, and sexual associations." His answers then classify as unprotected, at *any* volume: a year of prescriptions (Q4 — what medications you take), a hotel registry (Q5 — where and with whom you slept, which the registry *memorializes*, it does not merely permit one to infer), and five years of credit-card statements (Q3 — every purchase, donation, clinic co-pay, and motel charge in a life). The rule he offers protects whom you dialed but not what you ingest or whose bed you shared. His own Q12 supplies the verdict on that kind of asymmetry: a rule "that protects browsing histories but not calling histories — would be indefensible, and Respondent would be the first to say so." We are saying so — about his.

His defense of the gap is threefold: subpoena reasonableness, and "Congress and HIPAA-type regimes remain free to layer protection above the constitutional floor" (Q4). The Government is happy to welcome Petitioner to that answer — it is the Government's Part I.C. But Petitioner is not entitled to ridicule it in our mouth and rely on it in his own. When the Government invokes legislative calibration, his Answer to Bench Q21 calls it abdication: "its answer to every hypothetical is that Congress will attend to it eventually . . . declining to have a rule is abdication"; his Answer to Bench Q30 derides "protocols in place of the magistrate." When his own rule strands the pharmacy customer, Congress is suddenly a sufficient guardian. One of those positions must be surrendered in rebuttal; the Court should ask which.

*Why it matters:* This is the answer that prices the revolution. After the overruling Petitioner seeks, the most intimate categories in ordinary American life — medical purchases, bedmates, the financial whole of a life — remain exactly where *Miller* left them, while the gain is dialed numbers. A "special justification" that buys this little while costing this much is not special; it is not even a justification.

**Challenge 3 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q2 with Bench Q19 and Bench Q21 — volume never matters, except when it does]**

*Defect: internal contradiction on whether aggregation changes constitutional character.*

Answer to Resp. Q2: "the line is categorical by record type, not volumetric. . . . Quantity does not transmute the category." That is the load-bearing wall of the ledger side — it is how Petitioner keeps five years of statements, a year of prescriptions, and a week of hotel nights unprotected.

Answer to Bench Q21 (license-plate readers): "The line between those poles is *aggregation into a chronicle* — the point at which ambient observation becomes the functional equivalent of tracking." So for police cameras, quantity *does* transmute the category: one plate-read is *Knotts*; an accumulating database is a search.

And the inversion completes itself in Bench Q19: a tower dump — one location datum, one moment, per bystander — is "a search as to each," because "category . . . is not diluted by . . . the brevity of the slice." Put the three answers side by side: a single instant of one stranger's location is a protected "chronicle of movements"; seven consecutive nights of a guest's location in a hotel registry is an unprotected ledger; and aggregation converts observation into a chronicle for ALPR data but can never convert transactions into one for financial data. A single datum is not a "chronicle" of anything — the word is doing no work. What is actually operating is an enumerated list of favored data types, with "chronicle" applied as a label after the classification is made. That is not a rule the magistrate of Resp. Q1 can apply; it is a table of results to be memorized.

*Why it matters:* Petitioner's answer to the workability objection (Resp. Q8) is that his line is "categorical" while the Government's is ad hoc. These three answers show the categories are themselves volumetric on one side, anti-volumetric on the other, and instant-sensitive for bystanders — three different metrics, selected per outcome.

**Challenge 4 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q9, Q11, and Bench Q29, taken together — the disposition Petitioner seeks is unprecedented, unnecessary, and by his own judgment inferior]**

*Defect: concessions whose combined consequence Petitioner has not accepted.*

Three locked answers now stand in a row. *First*, Resp. Q9: *Miller* — "same subpoena, same checks" — comes out the same way. *Second*, Resp. Q11: "we cannot name a case in which this Court used the word 'overruled' while formally reaffirming the judgment as good law." His proffered analogue, *Katz*'s treatment of *Olmstead*, defeats him: *Katz* *changed outcomes* — the warrantless wiretap that was no search under *Olmstead* became a search under *Katz*. Petitioner's proposal changes none; he concedes the *Miller* subpoena is enforced tomorrow. What he describes — retiring a rationale while preserving results — is precisely the operation *Carpenter* performed, and in Q11 he "accept[s] the label without complaint" if the Court styles it as "holding that the third-party doctrine no longer states the governing rule." That acceptance is the case: it concedes that everything he seeks is available by *Carpenter*-style means, without the word "overruled" and without answering QP I in his favor. *Third*, Bench Q29: offered the forced choice, Petitioner takes the unanimous narrow opinion "without hesitation" — "[a]nd, answering honestly, for the Constitution too," leaving "*Miller* to fall in a case where its rationale is squarely dispositive."

That last clause is the admission the Government has sought for three rounds: *this is not the case in which Miller's rationale is squarely dispositive* — Petitioner's own counsel says the overruling belongs to a different record. An advocate may press a question in the alternative; he may not press it as imperative for three briefs and then, asked which disposition serves the Constitution, choose the one that leaves the precedent standing. The answer to QP I, on Petitioner's own filings, is: not here, not now.

*Why it matters:* Special justification is Petitioner's burden. A movant who concedes (a) the result survives, (b) the relief has no precedent in form, (c) complete relief lies elsewhere (Resp. Q14–Q15), and (d) the narrow opinion is better for the Constitution, has carried the Government's burden, not his own.

**Challenge 5 — [Answer challenged: Resp. Q16, with Bench Q20 — the Article III "live controversy" rests on a misstatement of the Government's actual position]**

*Defect: misstatement of the opposing party's litigating position; contradiction with Petitioner's own Q20.*

To supply a vehicle for ring three, Petitioner's Q16 asserts: "The Government argues the opposite at every step: that Morgan voluntarily conveyed his location by carrying a powered phone." That is not the Government's position, and it was not the Government's brief. The brief expressly demoted voluntariness to a "secondary" theory and rested on *Knotts*; the Government's filed Answers say it in terms: "**No.** The Government does not contend that Morgan voluntarily conveyed the responsive fixes in the sense *Miller* and *Smith* use that term" (Resp. Answers Q23), and "[t]hey are not, and the Government does not argue they are" (Q24). Petitioner's Bench Q20 answer commits the same invention: "under the Government's view, the same fixes are a search from a simulator and a nullity from a fax to Verizon." The Government's own Bench Q4 answer gives the *invariant* answer — simulator or carrier, same result — in writing.

Worse, Petitioner's own Answer to Resp. Q20 concedes the consequence of the Government's real position: "If the Government now says the carrier's identity is beside the point, we agree — and then the case rises or falls on question one." The Government has said exactly that, in filings Petitioner possessed when he wrote these answers. By his own logic, rings two and three are academic, and the "live controversy" he locates in the Government's supposed conveyance defense does not exist. What remains of his vehicle argument is only the grant of certiorari — which is an argument for *answering* QP I, not for answering it his way, and the Government has urged the Court to answer it: by declining to overrule.

*Why it matters:* If the dispositive-rationale conflict Petitioner describes is manufactured, ring three is exactly what Bench Q17 feared — a request for an opinion on a doctrine no party's defense of this judgment invokes for these fixes.

**Challenge 6 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q22 and Q23 — ring two's factual predicate is conceded away, and the fallback collapses ring two into ring one]**

*Defect: record assertion abandoned under questioning; the substitute theory destroys the brief's "three independent grounds" architecture and carries unaccepted consequences.*

The brief's ring two rests on a factual claim: the fixes were "generated on government command," by a carrier that "activates a device's location function solely to answer a police demand." Asked for page and line (Resp. Q22), Petitioner concedes: "It does not say 'Verizon activated the phone's GPS,' and we do not rest on any such finding." His gloss that "pinged" is "the record's own word, denoting interrogation of the device on request" smuggles the abandoned finding back in through a definition — the record uses "pinged" once, in a sentence about the thirty-minute delay, and defines nothing.

The fallback (Q23) is that "what was commanded into existence was not the tower datum but the *reporting stream*," so the theory survives even if the network already generates the data. But examine what that substitution does. *First*, it makes ring two's trigger "a prospective reporting stream that began upon, and only because of, a police demand" — which is, word for word, ring one: Bench Q18 says "*government initiation* and *prospectivity* define the category." Ring two no longer does anything ring one does not; the "three independent grounds" Petitioner invoked at Resp. Q14 are now two, and the alternative holding he asks for ("at minimum") is the principal holding wearing a different label. *Second*, the substitute theory proves vastly more than Petitioner has accepted: if directing a carrier to "report . . . at intervals *going forward*" is per se government-commissioned surveillance, then every prospective pen-register order and every prospective § 2703(d) disclosure — regimes Congress built on court orders issued below probable cause — "commissions surveillance" and requires a warrant. Petitioner nowhere accepts that the Pen Register Act's forty-year-old architecture is unconstitutional; his Q13 (Bench) instead assures the Court that agency theory is "done" whenever "judicial process" authorizes the acquisition — without noticing that *which* process suffices is the very question his theory was supposed to answer, not assume.

*Why it matters:* Ring two was Petitioner's offer of a minimalist exit. The exit either has no record support (commanded generation) or no independent content (prospectivity-plus-initiation, i.e., ring one).

**Challenge 7 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q25, Q26, Q27 against Bench Q26 — the hiker answer refutes the indivisible-stream theory]**

*Defect: internal contradiction; the inception rule is applied as the one-way ratchet Petitioner denies it is.*

Asked whether the hiker stream's day-three catch is suppressed after the emergency ends (Bench Q26), Petitioner answers: "when the hiker is found, the justification lapses, and **continued receipt is an ongoing warrantless acquisition with no current justification** — day three's catch is suppressed."

Hold that sentence against his October 10 position. In the hiker case, *no new governmental act occurs on day three* — no request, no certification, only receipt — yet Petitioner treats each continued receipt as "an ongoing warrantless acquisition" measured against *current* justification. That is precisely the episode-by-episode framework he spends Resp. Q25–Q27 denouncing: if continued receipt is an assessable acquisition when the justification has *died*, it is an assessable acquisition when the justification has *arisen*. His attempted reconciliation — "inception fixes the lawfulness of the *act undertaken*" — cannot be squared with his own hiker holding, in which the act undertaken (a truthful request) was lawful at inception and the stream is nonetheless parsed, receipt by receipt, against the facts of each day. On Petitioner's stated principle that "the government's authority is exactly coextensive with its justification, no more, no less, **in both directions**," October 10's receipts — coinciding with an interrupted Butcher homicide and a killer in flight — are measured against October 10. The only way to avoid that result is to measure justification continuously when it condemns the government and only at inception when it would excuse it. That is the ratchet, and it is Petitioner's.

This also exposes the brief's flat misstatement that "on October 10 the government took none" — no act. The record: Doakes received and accessed two fixes that day, "concluded that Morgan was likely traveling west along Florida State Road 9336," and "called Special Agent Lundy to request he and his fellow FBI agents search for Morgan." Petitioner's own Part I holding defines the search as "*obtaining* fixes" — acquisition. He counts the government's obtaining as the constitutional event when establishing the violation, and discounts the same obtaining as a non-act when the question is justification.

*Why it matters:* This is the whole suppression case as to the only fixes that mattered. If receipt is an acquisition assessable by current circumstances — as Petitioner himself holds when the valence favors him — the October 10 acquisitions were exigency-justified, and Parts III and IV of the Government's brief decide the case.

**Challenge 8 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q25 and Q26 — "no officer could even take a responsive act": record silence converted into a finding, and the objective test traded for a subjective one]**

*Defect: assertion unsupported by the record; contradiction with the brief's own legal standard.*

Petitioner's Q26 asserts that on this record no officer "even *could* take" a responsive act on October 10, "because no officer is shown to have connected the rangers' discovery to the stream before the fixes arrived"; Q25 likewise builds on "knowledge no officer in the chain is shown to have possessed when the fixes arrived." The record is *silent* on who knew what hour what; silence is not a negative finding, and Petitioner — the movant on suppression of the October 10 acquisitions — states the silence as established fact. What the record does show is that park rangers (law enforcement officers) discovered an interrupted Butcher murder on October 10 and that the discovery entered the investigation that same day.

More fundamentally, the demand for officer-specific contemporaneous knowledge contradicts Petitioner's own brief, which invoked the opposite rule to dispatch Doakes's motives: "The inquiry is objective, *Brigham City v. Stuart*, 547 U.S. 398, 404 (2006)." Petitioner cannot have an objective inquiry when it neutralizes the officer's bad purpose and a subjective, head-by-head knowledge inquiry when it would neutralize an objective emergency. *Brigham City* is one case; it cannot be cited for both.

*Why it matters:* Whether anyone "connected the rangers' discovery to the stream" before the material fixes arrived is a remand fact, not an appellate assumption — and Petitioner's Q25 elsewhere insists that contested predicates "belong on remand" when the burden is the Government's. The same discipline applies to his.

**Challenge 9 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q22 with Q28 (item 6) and Q29 — the two-dataset theory forks against itself, and the taint chain runs through a record event that does not exist]**

*Defect: internal contradiction between answers; causal assertion unsupported by the record.*

In Q22, to keep the quarter-mile figure away from the tracking stream, Petitioner insists Scuteri's maps were "built from 'cell-site data,' **a different dataset** — the conflation is Respondent's, not ours." In Q28, to suppress those same maps, he supplies this chain: "fax → fixes → arrest → Morgan's identification as defendant → **records request**." No "records request" appears anywhere in this record. The record says only that Scuteri worked "[w]ith the cell-site data provided by the wireless carrier"; it discloses nothing about when, how, or under what process that data was obtained — a silence the Government conceded in its own answers. Petitioner has invented the decisive link in his own taint chain and stated it as record fact.

And the two answers fork against each other. The record states that Morgan "moved to suppress the GPS data provided by Verizon" — the stream. If the maps rest on "a different dataset," as Q22 insists, then the maps were never the subject of the suppression motion below, and Petitioner's demand for their exclusion arrives in this Court unpreserved and chained to an unrecorded acquisition. If instead the maps rest on the same data, then the only quantified accuracy in this record — "within a quarter mile" — describes the stream, and Part I's homing-beacon, GPS-grade rhetoric ("precise enough to trace a moving car") loses its record basis. Petitioner may choose either branch; he may not occupy both. Finally, Q29's counterfactual — "no one would have pulled Morgan's carrier records had the stream not identified, located, and charged him" — is argument, not record, and the record leans against it: Morgan was the weeks-long fixation of a detective, an insider squarely within the FBI's announced profile, and (by Petitioner's own Q30 concession) the murder itself was discovered wholly independent of any fix. Those untainted predicates are exactly what *Murray* and *Nix* weigh on remand; Petitioner "concede[s] the forum" and should stop pre-litigating the finding with facts the record does not contain.

*Why it matters:* The maps are the evidence Petitioner says "cinched the case." His entitlement to their suppression depends entirely on assertions — a different dataset, a post-arrest request, a counterfactual about investigative behavior — that are either contradictory or extra-record.

**Challenge 10 — [Answers challenged: Bench Q22 and Q18, with Bench Q23 — the first-fix rule's consequences, stated but not paid for]**

*Defect: unaccepted consequences of a locked concession; rationale and rule no longer match.*

Petitioner now concedes that a single fresh-pursuit ping is a search (Bench Q22) and that "duration and open-endedness are aggravators, not elements" (Bench Q18). Two consequences follow that his answers acknowledge nowhere.

*First*, the constitutional rationale and the constitutional rule have come apart. The interest Part I invokes — *Carpenter*'s "reasonable expectation of privacy in the whole of one's physical movements" — is an aggregate interest; that is the entire teaching of the *Jones* concurrences and *Carpenter* itself. A single tower fix invades no "whole" of anything. A rule triggered at the first fix is therefore not an application of the *Carpenter* interest; it is a per se rule about a data type, supported by a rationale that begins to operate only days later. Petitioner refused the duration element to avoid line-drawing — but he bought the mismatch, and he should be made to defend a holding whose justification does not reach its trigger.

*Second*, the operational bill. By Petitioner's rule, every one of the tens of thousands of annual § 2702(b)(8) emergency disclosures is a warrantless constitutional search, lawful only if a court later finds a genuine, objectively verifiable exigency. His Bench Q23 answer says the statute is facially valid because "where a real emergency exists, the statute channels lawful conduct; where none exists, the form is a nullity." That elides the band where real practice lives — the feared-but-unverifiable emergency: the missing adult, the unconfirmed welfare check, the 911 hang-up. Under the first-fix rule those officers conduct presumptively unconstitutional searches and learn whether they are felons of the Fourth Amendment only at the suppression hearing. "Exigency is where flexibility lives" (Bench Q22) is no answer, because exigency requires the very verifiability the middle band lacks. The Government's framework resolves that band with *Knotts*: locating a person's present public position was never a search needing excuse. Petitioner's framework leaves it to after-the-fact litigation, and his answers never accept — or even acknowledge — that cost.

*Why it matters:* The Court will write a rule for the officer, the carrier, and the magistrate. Petitioner's answers establish that his rule gives the fresh-pursuit officer a search, the rationale gives him no reason, and the safety valve gives him litigation.

**Challenge 11 — [Answers challenged: Resp. Q8 and Q17, with Bench Q5 — the "unworkability" special justification is assertion, and his own answers reproduce the regime he condemns]**

*Defect: unsupported empirical claim; the distinction offered is contradicted by the structure of his own answers.*

Petitioner's case for a special justification rests heavily on the claim (Resp. Q17) that "eight years of lower-court application have demonstrated that exception-without-rule cannot be administered — the geofence, pole-camera, and reverse-keyword litigation has fractured the circuits, each court guessing." No case is cited; no circuit split is identified; the proposition is offered as a fact of the world on counsel's say-so. The Court should weigh it as what it is: argument.

His reconciliation of "hard cases as they arrive" with "categorical line" (Resp. Q8; Bench Q5) is that classifying "genuinely novel record types at the margin" under a stated rule is adjudication, while the Government's regime is discretion. But survey what one round of questions required of his "categorical" rule: a retrospective/prospective sub-rule (Resp. Q23), an aggregation-point sub-rule for ALPR (Bench Q21), a per-bystander sub-rule for tower dumps (Bench Q19), a content overlay for cloud footage (Resp. Q6), and an ad hoc override for pen registers (Challenge 1, *supra*). Five new sub-rules in sixty answers is not a categorical line with quiet margins; it is the case-by-case regime he condemns, relabeled. The Government's framework, by contrast, has eight years of lower-court administration behind *Carpenter*'s actual factors — and Petitioner's Q13 list of cases that "come out differently" consists of one case he must overrule (*Smith*), one category litigated under standards his rule would also litigate (browsing histories), and a standing adjustment (*Miller* reasonableness objections) obtainable without overruling anything.

*Why it matters:* Unworkability-proven-by-experience is the only special justification Petitioner offers that *Carpenter* did not already consider and find insufficient eight years ago (his Q17 concedes the others are *Carpenter* itself and the ubiquity of data — both fully visible in 2018). If the empirical claim is unsupported and the proposed cure reproduces the disease, the stare decisis case is over.

---

## Conclusion

Petitioner's answers were candid in places, and the Government has credited the candor by locking its yield in the preamble. But on the questions that decide this case, the answers are a set of paired contradictions: a magistrate's sentence that misclassifies *Smith*; volume that never matters except when it does; a "live controversy" built on a position the Government disclaimed in writing; a commanded-generation theory with no record and no independent content; an inception rule that measures justification continuously against the government and never for it; a taint chain through a records request that does not exist; and — above all — counsel's own written judgment that the unanimous narrow opinion, *Miller* untouched, is the better disposition "for the Constitution too." Petitioner is entitled to one rebuttal. He is not entitled to both halves of any of these pairs.
