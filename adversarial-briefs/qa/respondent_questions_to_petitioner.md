# Respondent's Questions to Petitioner

**Dexter Morgan v. United States, No. 850-418 — Deposition/Interrogatory Phase**

*Propounded by Respondent United States. Petitioner is required to answer each question in writing. Where a question calls for a yes-or-no answer, answer yes or no before explaining.*

---

## I. The Chronicle/Ledger Successor Rule — State It and Apply It

**Q1.** State, in a single sentence usable by a magistrate judge ruling on a motion to quash, the rule of decision that determines whether a given third-party record is a protected "chronicle of the customer's own protected activities" or an unprotected "transactional ledger."

**Q2.** Yes or no: is a single bank record showing that one check cleared a "chronicle" or a "ledger" — and does your answer change at ten checks, a monthly statement, or five years of statements, and if so, at what point and by what doctrinal measure?

**Q3.** Your brief concedes that "a credit-card record of a discrete purchase" falls on the ledger side; yes or no, does a twelve-month credit-card statement fall on the chronicle side, and if both answers are yes, identify the constitutional line the issuing bank must locate before complying with a subpoena.

**Q4.** Is a pharmacy's purchase history showing a customer's prescriptions over one year a chronicle of the customer's protected activities or the pharmacy's own ledger of its sales — and is your answer consistent with your treatment of the bank records in Q2?

**Q5.** Yes or no: after Miller falls, may a grand jury obtain a hotel guest registry by subpoena — and if yes, explain how a record of where a person slept, with whom, and on what nights is not a chronicle "reconstructing the customer's conduct."

**Q6.** Is doorbell-camera footage held by Ring/Amazon — recorded by the customer's own device, stored on the company's servers for the company's service purposes — a chronicle or a ledger, and whose "conduct" does it chronicle when it captures the comings and goings of third parties?

**Q7.** Your sorting criterion asks whether a record's "evidentiary significance lies in reconstructing the customer's conduct"; since the government's interest in virtually every subpoenaed record lies in reconstructing someone's conduct, identify one category of record with evidentiary value to a prosecutor that remains on the ledger side under your test.

**Q8.** Your brief says hard cases "can be resolved as they arrive"; reconcile that case-by-case promise with your simultaneous claim that the rule supplies "a categorical line, not case-by-case sensitivity audits."

## II. "Miller's Outcome Could Survive" — What, Exactly, Is Overruled

**Q9.** Yes or no: under your proposed successor rule, would United States v. Miller — the same subpoena, the same checks and deposit slips — be decided the same way today?

**Q10.** If your answer to Q9 is yes, state precisely what concrete holding of Miller — as opposed to its rationale — you are asking this Court to overrule.

**Q11.** Name one decision of this Court that "overruled" a precedent while leaving that precedent's judgment and result intact as good law; if you cannot, explain why your proposal is an overruling rather than the rationale-trimming Carpenter already performed.

**Q12.** Yes or no: under your rule, is Smith v. Maryland — a pen-register record whose entire evidentiary significance lies in reconstructing the customer's communications — overruled outright, and if yes, why does your brief never say so?

**Q13.** Identify one concrete case that comes out differently under your chronicle/ledger rule than under Carpenter as it stands today; if you cannot, state what "special justification" remains for overruling Miller to obtain a result Carpenter already delivers.

## III. Why Reach the Question Presented at All

**Q14.** Yes or no: if the Court holds under your Part I that the real-time tracking was a search governed by Carpenter, Petitioner obtains complete relief without the Court deciding whether Miller survives?

**Q15.** If your answer to Q14 is yes, state the principle of constitutional avoidance or party-presentation that nonetheless permits — or requires — the Court to overrule a fifty-year-old precedent unnecessary to the judgment in Petitioner's favor.

**Q16.** Your Part II argues the third-party doctrine "does no work here" because nothing was voluntarily conveyed; if the doctrine does no work in your own case, identify the live controversy in this record that supplies an Article III vehicle for invalidating it.

**Q17.** Carpenter expressly declined to disturb Miller and Smith only eight years ago, after considering the same Sotomayor and Marshall critiques you press; identify what has changed since 2018 — in fact, law, or technology — that constitutes a special justification Carpenter itself found insufficient.

**Q18.** The day after Miller is overruled, state the process a grand jury investigating securities fraud must use to obtain a target's brokerage statements — Oklahoma Press subpoena or probable-cause warrant — and state which side of your line brokerage statements (a chronicle of the customer's trades) fall on.

## IV. The Agency Theory Against Knotts

**Q19.** Yes or no as to each: in Knotts (a) the beeper was government-installed, (b) the suspect voluntarily conveyed nothing to anyone, and (c) the monitoring on public roads was nonetheless not a search?

**Q20.** If you accept all three propositions in Q19, state the rule under which Verizon's "agency" converts this acquisition into a search when the government's own device — conveyed by no one, installed by the government itself — effected none.

**Q21.** Quote the language in Knotts confining its reservation of "dragnet-type law enforcement practices" and "twenty-four hour surveillance of any citizen" to open-ended monitoring of a single targeted suspect, rather than to mass or societal surveillance practices.

**Q22.** Identify the record evidence — page and line — establishing that Verizon "activated" the phone's GPS or generated data it would not otherwise possess, given that the only quantified accuracy in this record is the trial expert's "within a quarter mile" and the trial record's own label is "cell-site data."

**Q23.** Yes or no: if the fixes were ordinary tower-scale registrations the network already generates to route every subscriber's calls, does your Almonor no-conveyance theory still apply — and if yes, state what distinguishes "reporting an operational fact on request" from "generating data on government command."

## V. The Inception Theory and Exigency

**Q24.** Yes or no: if, on the morning of October 10, an officer aware of the rangers' in-progress-murder discovery had faxed Verizon a truthful emergency certification, the resulting fixes would have been lawfully obtained?

**Q25.** If your answer to Q24 is yes, your objection reduces to the October 8 paperwork; state why the Fourth Amendment requires suppressing the identical October 10 data the government concededly could have obtained lawfully that same morning, rather than remanding for independent-source and inevitable-discovery findings — and state whether you will oppose such findings on remand.

**Q26.** Yes or no: under your no-retroactive-cure rule, is it Petitioner's position that no supervening event — an abducted child located mid-stream, a verified bomb plot — could ever justify the continued receipt of fixes from a stream opened unlawfully, even one minute longer?

**Q27.** McNeely measured exigency at the inception of a single completed act, a blood draw; cite the case holding that a continuing electronic transmission is one indivisible "undertaking" rather than a series of discrete acquisitions, given that Karo assessed a single installed beeper's monitoring episode by episode.

## VI. The Two Datasets and the Remedy You Actually Seek

**Q28.** List precisely the items of evidence Petitioner contends must be suppressed — the October 8–9 fixes, the October 10 fixes, the arrest, the trunk inventory, Estrada's body and the crime scene, Scuteri's trial maps — identifying for each the causal chain from the October 8 fax.

**Q29.** Yes or no: Scuteri's quarter-mile trial maps were built from "cell-site data provided by the wireless carrier" — historical records the government could have compelled by warrant or § 2703(d) order on the probable cause it possessed well before trial — so do you concede the maps survive independent-source analysis, and if not, identify the taint that connects them to the real-time stream rather than to the carrier's ordinary historical records?

**Q30.** Yes or no: the park rangers discovered the Everglades crime scene before, and independent of, any location fix — do you concede that the crime scene and Estrada's body are untainted, and if not, state the but-for causal chain from Doakes's fax to the rangers' discovery?
