## The Rebuttals

### Rebuttal 1 — Convergence and the brief's Part II

**(c) Right as to the brief's categorical rhetoric — absorbed; the judgment line is unchanged.**

We will not pretend the hit was not a hit: the brief's sentence that *Carpenter*'s "rationale does not translate" to prospective data states the point too categorically, and the convergence principle supersedes it. The final brief will say what the answers say: the constitutional variable is the size and character of the record the government acquires; retrospectivity was *Carpenter*'s paradigm of that harm, not its definition. That is a refinement, and we accept its cost.

What it does not cost is the judgment. Under the refined frame, this acquisition — under three days, two material fixes, both public — sits inside footnote 3's reservation from either direction of assembly. Petitioner says a position that "needs the Court to fix an unset dial" is a request, not a defense. But the dial is unset because *Carpenter* set it that way, and *both* parties are asking the Court to fix it: we ask the Court to hold this record below any line it draws; Petitioner asks it to hold that the dial reads zero — a search at the first fix (his Bench Q18, Q22), a rule no federal court has adopted and his own *a fortiori* logic does not require. As between "draw the line somewhere at or below seven days, above three" and "abolish the line," ours is the modest request. And his closing flourish — that by October 10 the stream "*was* a retrospective archive of the preceding three days" — concedes our case: a three-day historical record is precisely what footnote 3 reserves.

### Rebuttal 2 — The "convergence trap": one unit of analysis

**(a) Wrong. The fork is false: the unit of analysis is divisible throughout; what varies is what each acquisition reveals.**

Petitioner demands we choose between an aggregative threshold and a divisible one. We choose neither, because the choice misdescribes the framework: the unit of analysis is the *acquisition-episode* for both inquiries, and convergence is not a different unit — it is a recognition that what an episode *reveals* depends on what the government already holds. Fix one reveals a public position. Fix three hundred, arriving atop six days of accumulated coverage, reveals something more: it extends and completes a comprehensive chronicle. The episode remains the unit; revelation is contextual. That is *Karo*'s own architecture — one installation, episodes parsed by what each revealed, where what an episode revealed (a home's interior) was always a function of context.

So both prongs of the "closed fork" open. *Divisibility does not dissolve the cap*: fixes past the convergence point are searches one at a time, because in context each reveals the protected whole — the answer to Pet. Q16 and Bench Q6 stands, and indefinite tracking is not permitted. *And aggregation does not swallow Part III*: because the episodes are the unit, *McNeely* dates each acquisition's reasonableness to that acquisition, and October 10's were contemporaneous with an objective emergency.

Petitioner is in no position to press this fork, because his own hiker answer (Pet. Ans. Bench Q26) adopts our unit: when the hiker is found, "continued receipt is an ongoing warrantless acquisition with no current justification — day three's catch is suppressed." A stream lawfully opened, no new governmental act, parsed receipt-by-receipt against each day's facts. That is episode-by-episode reasonableness, in Petitioner's handwriting. If receipt is an assessable acquisition when justification has died, it is an assessable acquisition when justification has arisen. He may not measure justification continuously against the government and only at inception for it.

### Rebuttal 3 — The seven-day "ceiling" and the six-day hedge

**(b) Right that the hedge exists; immaterial — it is the honest treatment of a question this Court reserved.**

"Seven days, at most" and "six days may well cross" are not contradictory; they are the two halves of footnote 3 accurately reported. Seven days is the only line this Court has *drawn*; everything shorter is *reserved*. A ceiling the Court remains free to lower is still a ceiling on the government — and counsel who told the bench that six days is safely lawful would be answering a reserved question this Court has not answered, which is the one thing the SG's office will not do. Candor about a reserved zone is not upholstery.

As for the ex post objection — that the search's character turns on "the record the government ends up holding": that is *Karo*'s risk allocation, which we stated and accepted at Pet. Q20: the officer who proceeds without a warrant "bears the risk that what is revealed will be protected," and the prudent officer gets the warrant. The next Doakes who opens a stream "knowing it is no search unless he fails for a week" knows three other things: every fix is statutorily unlawful from the first (§ 2702), everything past the line is suppressible, and his falsified form carries personal exposure. And Petitioner's own framework has the identical ex post feature, inverted: his ALPR answer locates the search at "the point at which ambient observation becomes the functional equivalent of tracking" — an aggregation point lower courts must find after the fact (Pet. Ans. Bench Q21). Accumulation thresholds are not a defect of our rule; they are a feature of any rule that takes *Carpenter*'s aggregate interest seriously — including his.

### Rebuttal 4 — "Exigency without an act"

**(a) Wrong. The premise misstates both the record and our concession — and Petitioner's own answers define the search as the very "act" he now says did not occur.**

The challenge runs entirely on the corrected mischaracterization (Preamble, No. 2). We conceded no new *communication to Verizon*; we never conceded governmental inaction. On October 10 the government received, accessed, analyzed, and acted upon two fixes — and on Petitioner's own dictated Part I holding, "*obtaining* fixes" is the constitutional event (Pet. Ans. Bench Q18). He counts the government's obtaining as the search when establishing the violation and discounts the same obtaining as a non-act when the question is justification. The arithmetic he urges — "no act on October 10" — proves too much *for him*: if receipt is not a constitutionally significant act, then nothing constitutionally significant happened on October 10 at all, and his motion to suppress that day's fixes has no object. The acquisition either matters both times or neither.

We concede again, as we did at Pet. Q9, that per-episode *justification* extends *Karo*'s logic beyond its holding. But Petitioner's mirror concession is on file: "no case of this Court squarely holds either way for this configuration" (Pet. Ans. Q27). Both sides argue from method; ours has *Karo*'s disaggregation and Petitioner's own hiker answer behind it (Rebuttal 2); his single-indivisible-undertaking rule has Title III — a statute. And our express fallback was built for exactly this challenge: if the Court holds the stream indivisible, Part III hands off to Part IV. The pieces Petitioner says "dismantle" Part III were placed by us, in public, with the fallback attached.

### Rebuttal 5 — The stipulation's scope

**(b) Right about the stipulation's breadth; immaterial — we accept the broad reading at a cost of exactly nothing.**

We will not quarrel with the opinion's recital. Read the stipulation as covering "the data collected by Sergeant Doakes" — all of it, October 8 through October 10 — and the Government accepts it *fully*: every disclosure Verizon made under Doakes's false certification fell outside § 2702(b)(8), because the statutory exception is keyed to the disclosure actually procured, and the only request ever made was the false one. The entire stream was statutorily unlawful. That has been our affirmative position throughout ("statutorily always," Pet. Q12), and extending it expressly to October 10 *strengthens* the § 2707 exposure we rely on. No relief from the stipulation is sought.

What the stipulation cannot do is erase the rangers from October 10. Our Bench Q18 answer conceded the substantive standards are aligned; it located the difference in *what each inquiry evaluates and when*: the statute evaluates the provider's good-faith belief upon the certification received; constitutional exigency evaluates the objective circumstances confronting the government at the acquisition. Identical substance, different evaluand, different moment — which is why one false form flunks the statute for every disclosure it procured, while the interrupted murder satisfies the Constitution for the acquisitions contemporaneous with it. *Virginia v. Moore* protects exactly that distinction: it does not require divergent standards, only distinct inquiries. The parties stipulated about a statutory exception; they did not — and could not — stipulate that no emergency existed in the world on October 10. The record recites the emergency two paragraphs above the stipulation.

### Rebuttal 6 — "No phone number on the form"

**(a) Wrong at its core: the targeting of Morgan did not come from the stream — and the emergency pathway never required probable cause.**

The circularity claim has the causation backwards. The stream supplied Morgan's *location*; it never supplied his *identity*. Doakes's fixation on Morgan — weeks of extrajudicial stalking — predates the fax and owes nothing to any fix; the FBI's insider profile is likewise untainted; both are facts the record states. So the "honest officer on October 10" is not an amnesiac: he is an officer with a genuine, objective emergency (a Butcher murder interrupted, the killer fled) and a pre-existing, stream-independent suspect. And the form he fills out is an *emergency* certification, which requires a good-faith danger-of-death belief — not probable cause as to the target. Petitioner's challenge quotes our concession that Doakes lacked "probable cause as to Morgan" as if it sank the truthful request; it is irrelevant to it. Section 2702(b)(8) asks whether an emergency necessitates the information, not whether the requester could obtain a warrant. Petitioner's own answer concedes the dispositive point: a truthful October 10 certification "would have been lawful" (Pet. Ans. Q24).

What remains right in the challenge was conceded before it was filed: whether those lawful paths were *genuinely available as to particular exhibits* is *Nix*/*Murray* material for remand (Pet. Q29), and we re-concede it. To the extent the *Herring* weighing leans on the same contested predicate, the Court may weigh the lawful-paths point at remand-contingent value — the deterrence balance still rests on the limits that need no counterfactual: the pre-emergency yield's unlawfulness, exigency's non-bankability, and the falsifier's exposure. And the symmetry should be noted: Petitioner's walk through October 10 — what an honest officer would have known, suspected, and written — is itself a counterfactual built on record silence, the precise vice his Q25 condemns. Both parties' counterfactuals belong below. Ours was conceded there first.

### Rebuttal 7 — The four limits "operate only where the Government has lost"

**(b) Right that the limits assume the search world; immaterial — that is the only world in which an exigency limiting principle is needed.**

The question we were asked (Pet. Q12; Bench Q13) was what limits *the exigency doctrine* carries. Exigency is an excuse for searches; its limiting principle necessarily lives in the world where a search has been found. Demanding that the exigency limits also operate in the no-search world conflates two objections: whether the no-search holding is *correct* (Part II's fight, waged on *Knotts*, *Karo*, and the convergence cap), and whether exigency, if needed, is bounded (it is, four ways). In the no-search world the "open first and wait" scheme is not cost-free; it is *pointless* — no emergency is needed, no falsification helps, the convergence cap and the statute supply the limits, and the officer who falsifies anyway buys § 2707 exposure for nothing. In neither world does dishonesty purchase what honesty would not.

As for "coincided" (Bench Q21): the coincidence was an *objective emergency* — the thing exigency doctrine has always turned on. To call lawfulness-upon-genuine-emergency "the house paying out" is to indict the exigency exception itself, which Petitioner accepts in writing: the truthful certification is lawful (his Q24), fresh pursuit justifies (his Bench Q22), the statute is facially constitutional because "where a real emergency exists, the statute channels lawful conduct" (his Bench Q23). The residual complaint — that the statutory deterrent for pure statutory violations is thin — is real, conceded, and congressional. Both parties have now said in writing that Congress should fortify it. That is agreement, not defect.

### Rebuttal 8 — Part IV "reduced to a remand"

**(c) Right in part, and absorbed — into an architecture in which remand was always the ceiling and never the floor.**

We accept the tally's honest core: if the Court finds a search, rejects exigency, and reaches taint, the disposition is a fruits remand. The brief said so itself — "at the very most . . . a remand for fruits-and-harmlessness analysis" — before any question was asked. But "a ceiling that is also the floor" is wrong about the architecture: the floor is affirmance on Part II (no search) or Part III (exigency); Part IV's office was never to affirm in the teeth of a found, unexcused, fruit-bearing search — it was to defeat the automatic reversal-with-suppression Petitioner seeks, and after every concession it still does.

And the challenge omits that the Q&A reduced *Petitioner's* relief identically. His own filed answers concede: the crime scene and body are untainted (his Q30, "without reluctance"); the trunk is open to attenuation argument on remand (his Q28, item 4); the maps' taint chain runs through "a records request" appearing nowhere in this record and a counterfactual about investigative behavior, with the *Nix*/*Murray* forum conceded (his Q28–Q29); and *Davis*/*Teague* insulate the closed-case universe (his Bench Q7). His maximal honest outcome, like ours, is a limited remand. The exchange has converged the parties on one proposition: no one can resolve fruits from this lectern. That convergence defeats Petitioner's requested disposition, not ours — and it makes QP I's overruling doubly gratuitous, since Petitioner concedes complete relief lies elsewhere (his Q14). On the "right without a remedy" coda: the answer stands at Bench Q19, unchallenged on its merits — exclusion serves only the tracked citizen who is charged; § 2707 serves the innocent target; the thin remedy that protects everyone should be strengthened, not traded for the rich one that protects only defendants.

### Rebuttal 9 — "Attenuation" mislabeled

**(c) Right and absorbed: we withdraw the label and keep the analysis, which already ended in the disposition the correct label requires.**

The criticism is fair. The rangers' discovery did not intervene in the causal chain from fax to fixes — nothing intervened; the fixes arrived mechanically. The doctrinally precise statement is the one our other answers already make: the discovery (i) supplied the objective circumstances making the October 10 *acquisitions* reasonable — exigency, Part III's actual ground — and (ii) bears on downstream evidence through independent source and inevitable discovery, doctrines that carry government burdens dischargeable on remand, exactly as conceded at Pet. Q29. The word "attenuation" in Q10 borrowed the wrong vocabulary for the right conclusion; note that the answer's own final sentence already named "a remand for fruits analysis under *Murray* and *Nix*" and volunteered the flagrancy weight against us. Striking the label changes no disposition.

What we do not withdraw: the distinct point that the October 10 fixes are not fruit of the October 8–9 *fixes* — no investigative chain connects them — so the taint question runs to the fax alone. And the mirror is on file: Petitioner's own taint chain for the trial maps depends on a "records request" the record nowhere contains (his Q28, item 6). Each side's fruits case needs facts found below. The party that conceded that first, by name, in its opening answers, was the Government.

### Rebuttal 10 — The quarter-mile fork

**(c) Right and absorbed: the brief's unqualified "distinct dataset" assertion is withdrawn as record-unsupported — and either branch of the fork ends in the same remand, while cutting Petitioner worse.**

We elect, and we pay. The record does not disclose the trial dataset's provenance — we said so at Pet. Q30 and say so again — and the brief's flat assertion that the maps "rested on historical cell-site records distinct from the real-time stream" overstated the record. The final brief will conform: the trial dataset's source and process are undetermined, and its admissibility is a remand question under independent source and inevitable discovery. That representation is withdrawn, not defended.

The fork, however, does not take the rest. The quarter-mile figure directly quantifies the trial exhibits; our stream characterization never rested on it alone. The record's own words say what the carrier provided — "cell-site data" — while "GPS" appears only as the label in Morgan's suppression motion; and the "functional evidence" Petitioner cites proves nothing finer than tower scale: two fixes ten miles apart, an *inferred* direction of travel, and agents who found Morgan by searching a corridor, not by a fix at a drive-thru. Tower scale remains the only record-supported characterization on either branch. And the same fork stands open at Petitioner's feet, sharper: if the datasets are distinct, his suppression motion below — addressed to "the GPS data provided by Verizon," the stream — never reached the maps; if they are one, the only quantified accuracy in the record is a quarter mile, and his homing-beacon rhetoric loses its basis. He filed both branches (his Q22 against his Q28). We have now elected; the rebuttal record will show which party still occupies both tines.

### Rebuttal 11 — The *Miller* we defend

**(a) Wrong. The trimming Petitioner calls "rewriting" was performed by this Court in *Carpenter*, and the asymmetry he calls a contradiction is the burden of stare decisis itself.**

Three answers. *First*, the "documents leg" was not "assembled in 2026": it is the first ground of *Miller* itself — "the documents subpoenaed here are not respondent's 'private papers' . . . [they are] the business records of the banks," 425 U.S. at 440–41 — stated before the risk-assumption passage Petitioner quotes. *Second*, the proposition that the maximal formulation does not control beyond its facts is not our reclassification; it is *Carpenter*'s holding: this Court "decline[d] to extend" *Miller* and *Smith* to CSLI while expressly leaving both standing. Whatever one calls the broad language, this Court has already adjudicated that it is severable from the precedent's survival. We defend the *Miller* that *Carpenter* left on the books — which is, as we said, "the only *Miller* anyone has defended since 2018."

*Third*, the advisory "symmetry" is false because stare decisis is not symmetric. Overruling is a judicial *act* requiring special justification; an act with no effect on the judgment is advisory. Declining to overrule is the *default* — the disposition that follows whenever the movant fails his burden — and it requires nothing from the doctrine on the facts at hand. Denying relief that would change nothing is ordinary; granting it is advisory. And we said in terms what "reaffirm" means in our mouth: "a refusal, not a fresh, expansive endorsement" (Pet. Q22), with an express reservation of *Miller*'s long-term fate equally acceptable (Bench Q10). Petitioner, meanwhile, cannot name a precedent ever "overruled" with its judgment intact (his Q11), accepts the relabel "no longer states the governing rule" "without complaint" (id.), and — asked which disposition serves the Constitution — chose the narrow opinion leaving *Miller* untouched, "for the Constitution too" (his Bench Q29). The party demanding an advisory act under a fought-over label is not the United States.

### Rebuttal 12 — "Targeted" and the tower dump

**(a) Wrong. It mistakes a necessary condition of one holding for a sufficient condition of a different one.**

"Targeted" is a necessary condition *of the no-search holding we proposed for prospective tracking streams*: an acquisition must be targeted to claim that holding's shelter. It is not, and was never offered as, a rule that untargeted acquisitions are therefore searches — necessary conditions do not run backwards. The condition does real work in the only direction a condition can: the next government lawyer cannot cite our holding for dragnet prospective tracking of the citizenry, which is precisely *Knotts*'s reservation and precisely what the bench feared. That a single-moment tower dump — a different acquisition, historical, instantaneous, chronicling no one — is analyzed under the archive metric and comes out no search does not abandon the condition, any more than "the automobile exception requires a vehicle" is abandoned by the fact that houses are governed by other rules. And our tower-dump answer carried its own limit in the same paragraph: serial or wide-area dumps that assemble movement profiles "cross toward *Carpenter*."

If the Court wants an example of limiting language that genuinely does no work, the exchange supplies one: Petitioner's "chronicle," under which a single instant of one bystander's location is a protected chronicle of movements (his Bench Q19) while seven consecutive nights in a hotel registry are an unprotected ledger (his Q5). Our condition confines our holding. His label decorates his outcomes.

### Rebuttal 13 — The truant teachers

**(b) Right about this record; immaterial to the doctrine — none of our grounds was ever calibrated to the teachers' attendance.**

We conceded the safeguards failed here — Verizon's gatekeeping (Bench Q17) and § 2707's empty reporter (Bench Q20) — and we re-concede both; the final brief will present the statutory deterrent at the discounted value already stated, not as a demonstrated success. But the inference fails: no ground of our case assumes the safeguards work. The no-search holding turns on what the fixes revealed, true for honest and dishonest requesters alike; the exigency holding turns on October 10's objective facts; the *Herring* balance turns on the deterrent value of suppressing *this* evidence — and the Bench Q13 sentence about "front-line teachers" described the statutory scheme's design, in an answer that conceded in the same breath that "the warrant is often the cheaper and better course."

What the challenge cannot do is convert a defeated statutory safeguard into a constitutional holding while its author relies on the identical legislature. Petitioner's own rule strands the pharmacy customer, the hotel guest, and five years of credit-card statements on "Congress and HIPAA-type regimes . . . free to layer protection above the constitutional floor" (his Q4) — the very answer he calls abdication in our mouth (his Bench Q21, Q30). If calibration-to-future-congressional-diligence disqualifies a framework, his ledger side — covering the most intimate categories in American life — is disqualified first. The honest position, which we hold, is that statutory safeguards failed in this case, the violator is exposed for it, and the repair of a statutory gate is legislative — a proposition both parties' filings now endorse.

### Rebuttal 14 — The wearable asymmetry

**(a) Wrong. The asymmetry is not docket-sorting; it is the oldest line in this field — public exposure — applied exactly as *Karo* applies it.**

The framework's variable is revelation, and what a single datum reveals differs by category. A location fix has a *Knotts* baseline: each fix reveals what any officer parked on the road could observe — a public fact — which is why individual fixes are unprotected and *accumulation* is the constitutional variable that eventually assembles what no observer could: the whole of one's movements. A heartbeat has no public baseline: the body's interior functioning is exposed to no observer, ever — it is the *Karo* home-interior case, where the first datum revealing the protected interior is a search at datum one, no accrual required. Our own answers drew this spatial ceiling before the challenge was filed: "duration aside, the moment any fix reveals information about the interior of a home or other protected space, that fix is a search" (Pet. Q16). The body is the first of protected spaces; this Court has treated intrusions beneath the skin as searches without counting days since *Schmerber* and *Skinner*.

So the classifications are one method, not two: data with a public-observation baseline is protected volumetrically (location, in public); data revealing a protected interior is protected from the first datum (the home, the body). The wearable's wearer gets her answer — zero free heartbeats, for the same reason *Karo* allowed zero free looks inside the house — and the location stream gets *Knotts* until convergence, for the same reason *Knotts* allowed the public tail. The principle Petitioner says does not exist is the one his own brief invokes on every page: the Fourth Amendment protects what is not exposed. It has never needed a docket number to apply it.

## Conclusion

The Government's answers conceded what the record compelled, and these rebuttals retrieve none of it. What the challenge round has actually produced is symmetry: a brief sentence withdrawn on each side's record assertions, a fruits analysis both parties now agree belongs below, and a Petitioner whose own filed answers — the hiker, the truthful certification, the untainted crime scene, the unnameable overruled-but-intact precedent, the narrow opinion chosen "for the Constitution too" — supply the framework, the exigency, the remand, and the disposition the United States has urged from the first page. The judgment should be affirmed; at the very most, the case goes back for the fruits findings both parties have now conceded no one can make from a lectern.
