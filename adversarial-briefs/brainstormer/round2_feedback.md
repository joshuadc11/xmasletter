## Feedback for Petitioner

**What improved.** The Statement is the best two paragraphs in either brief — the two-datasets untangling plus *Wong Sun* plant does exactly what was needed. Part I.B's three-layer *Knotts* answer, the Rule 41(b)(4)/§ 3117 structure, the Kerr rule, and Part IV are all real upgrades. The *Kimble* rebuttal forced Respondent to fix its cite, and your Payne point still stands.

**What's still weak or newly exposed.**
1. *Hammond* and *Riley* are now in the case and unanswered. Both are hours-long locates of fleeing/armed suspects; yours is a days-long, open-ended, no-predicate stream. That distinction is strong — but only if you make it.
2. "Dragnet" is doing too much work. Respondent honestly concedes the several days now; a 2–3 day targeted track of one suspect is not literally dragnet surveillance, and the label invites a correction from the bench. Your real hooks are open-endedness ("current," no end date), *Carpenter*'s "whole of their physical movements," and footnote 3's refusal to bless *any* period.
3. The severability/"continuing yield" answer is still assertion. The doctrinal anchor is available: *McNeely* measures exigency by the circumstances when the warrantless search is *undertaken*; the only undertaking here was October 8. No officer made any exigency-prompted request on October 10 — the fixes simply kept arriving. Say that.
4. Remedy remains a two-sentence flank while Respondent has a full Part. You don't need a section, but you need the textual answer: § 2708's exclusivity clause is limited by its own terms to "**nonconstitutional** violations of this chapter." If this was a Fourth Amendment search, the SCA's remedial scheme is beside the point.
5. The *Berger* parenthetical overstates — *Berger* struck New York's eavesdropping statute; it did not hold that delayed review remains interception. Soften to *cf.* or rest the latency point on Rule 41(b)(4)/§ 3117 alone.

**Final-round priorities (ranked).**
1. **Distinguish *Hammond* and *Riley* by name** — two or three sentences in I.B: hours versus days; identified fleeing/armed suspects versus a colleague under a falsified certificate; *Riley* is pre-*Carpenter* and rests on *Skinner* reasoning *Carpenter* undercuts; *Hammond* leaned on a contemporaneous armed-robbery exigency that is everything October 8 lacked.
2. **Close the severability door**: the no-fresh-acquisition-on-October-10 point plus *McNeely* inception framing, replacing the bare "continuing yield" assertion.
3. **One remedy paragraph**: the § 2708 "nonconstitutional violations" text, plus your existing *Leon*/*Franks* sentences. Defuses Respondent's cheapest exit at minimal word cost.
4. **Sharpen the chronicle/ledger criterion** to one sentence — your own draft is right: records whose evidentiary significance lies in *reconstructing the customer's conduct* rather than documenting the business's performance of its side of the transaction. Answer the credit-card hypothetical honestly (ledger side) and then do what *Carpenter* did: "we need not decide every case today."
5. **Cut to pay for it**: trim the state-cases paragraph in I.A, the *Berger* sentence, and one "dragnet" of the two. Stay at or under ~2,800 words.

**Answers to your questions.** (1) Use the criterion above; pick one hard hypothetical, resolve it against yourself, and stop — seam-probing is for oral argument, and a rule that decides hard cases both ways in a brief decides nothing. (2) Yes, the argument survives the honest reframing, but shift the weight from the "dragnet" label to open-endedness and *Carpenter*'s whole-of-movements holding; the reserved *Knotts* question is "twenty-four hour surveillance of any citizen" — an authorization with no end date *is* that, even targeted. (5) Not flinching — "overrule the rationale, cabin the result" is how this Court actually overrules, and it buys the workability credibility your QP needs. Keep it.
