# Brainstormer Feedback — After Round 1

## Overall Assessment

On the question actually granted — overrule *Miller*? — **Respondent is ahead**. The stare decisis fortress (*Kimble*'s special-justification demand, congressional reliance embodied in the SCA, *Carpenter*'s express "we do not disturb *Smith* and *Miller*") is the kind of institutional argument this Court accepts, and Petitioner's overruling section, though well-built on the *Janus*/*Ramos* factors, has not yet supplied the thing the Justices will demand at argument: a replacement rule that doesn't constitutionalize every grand-jury subpoena. Petitioner's "no legitimate reliance in unconstitutional practices" move is rhetoric, not analysis, and Respondent's workability point currently goes unanswered.

On the narrower question — was *this* a search? — **Petitioner is ahead**. The record facts (multi-day collection with no end date, a falsely certified emergency, a stalker-officer, government-commanded pings) map almost perfectly onto *Carpenter*'s rationales, and Respondent's "a swamp and a gas station" minimization is in visible tension with the record's "[f]or the next several days . . . received location data." Petitioner's agency/no-conveyance theory (ring 2) is the single strongest analytical move in either brief: Respondent's answer (tower registration is inherent to service) does not engage the on-demand *pinging* the record describes. On exigency, Respondent holds the better law (*Brigham City* objectivity, *Virginia v. Moore*) but the worse facts: the certification predates the Oct. 10 crime scene by two days, and Doakes had been *frozen out* of the investigation — a record fact neither brief has touched and which detonates the "manhunt exigency" framing.

Net: if the Court wants minimalism, Respondent wins QP I as framed but may lose the judgment; if the Court reaches the merits of the tracking, Petitioner wins the judgment without needing *Miller* overruled. Each side's Round 2 job is to close the gap on its weak flank.

## Feedback for Petitioner

**What's working.** The nested three-ring architecture is exactly right; the narrative ("a stalker with a badge," the fax form as the only safeguard) is the best fact-framing in either brief; the *Karo*/*Kyllo* "technique indifferent to the home's threshold" move and the III.F "this case refutes the doctrine's promise" section are genuinely persuasive.

**Vulnerabilities the other side will exploit.**
1. *Knotts*/open-fields is barely answered. Every fix in the record is public (Everglades, service station, drive-thru), and Respondent's *Oliver*/*Hester* pairing is strong. Your *Karo* answer has a hole: *Karo* turned on *actual* revelation of in-home information, not capability. You need *Knotts* itself — it expressly reserved "dragnet-type" 24-hour surveillance — plus *Carpenter*'s explicit refusal to extend the public-movements rationale to phone tracking (585 U.S. at 313–14).
2. The quarter-mile concession. Respondent has already weaponized Scuteri's trial testimony ("within a quarter mile") against your "GPS-grade" framing. Your single textual hook ("the GPS data provided by Verizon") won't survive unless you separate the **two datasets**: the real-time pings Doakes received (precision unquantified, precise enough to intercept a moving car) versus the cell-site records Scuteri mapped at trial. Untangle this before they do.
3. *Virginia v. Moore* / exigency. Your only answer is one sentence ("by stipulation, it was not [real]"). That's not enough; Respondent's statutory/constitutional distinction is doctrinally sound. You need the timeline: the certification was Oct. 8, the in-progress scene Oct. 10 — no specific exigency existed when the tracking began. And *Mincey v. Arizona*, which Respondent cites *for* exigency, actually **rejected a murder-scene exception** to the warrant requirement. Turn their cite against them.
4. The *Kimble* congressional-reliance point and the "no limiting principle" attack (abducted children, fleeing gunmen) are currently unanswered.

**New ideas to consider.**
1. **The Rule 41(b)(4)/§ 3117 structural argument.** Congress itself treats *prospective* location tracking as warrant territory: Fed. R. Crim. P. 41(b)(4) provides tracking-device warrants, and the SCA's compelled-disclosure provisions govern *stored* records. This answers the 30-minute-delay reframe structurally: prospective compelled generation is surveillance, not record production, no matter the delivery latency. Wiretap analogy: a recorded call reviewed the next day is still an interception (*Berger v. New York*).
2. **A Kerr-style limiting principle** for the overruling argument: distinguish records that are the *product of the customer's protected activity* (location, content, browsing) from the business's *own transactional ledgers* (the bank's record that a check cleared). Overrule *Miller* only as to the former; preserve *Oklahoma Press* subpoena-reasonableness for the latter. This single move answers workability, reliance, and the grand-jury parade of horribles.
3. **Use the freeze-out.** The FBI excluded all Miami Metro personnel from the investigation. Doakes was not hunting the Butcher when he faxed the form — he *was not allowed to*. This destroys exigency at its root and converts Respondent's "manhunt" framing into a misdescription of the record.
4. **Mincey + McNeely + telephonic warrants**: the gravity of the offense does not create exigency, and warrants are now obtainable in minutes — *Riley*'s own answer to the law-enforcement-needs objection.
5. **The Gorsuch bridge, one section only**: § 2702's disclosure ban gives subscribers a positive-law interest in nondisclosure — a property-flavored confirming argument, not a new framework. One tight section or extended footnote; don't restructure the brief around it.
6. **Carpenter already amputated part of the SCA** (the § 2703(d) order for historical CSLI) and the subpoena system did not collapse — your best empirical answer to the reliance fortress.

**Answers to your questions.**
1. *30-minute delay:* Yes — the search is the **compelled prospective acquisition**, not the delivery. The fixes were never "stored records" in *Miller*'s sense because they did not exist until generated at government command; latency cannot convert surveillance into archives (use the wiretap and Rule 41(b)(4) points above).
2. *Limiting principle:* Option (iii) (Kerr) blended with (ii), framed as "*Miller* limited to its facts — voluntary commercial transactions documented in the business's own ledgers." Pure Katz-totality (option (i)) will spook the workability-minded Justices; lead with the categorical rule, keep totality as the fallback.
3. *Stipulation scope:* Do **not** claim the stipulation forecloses constitutional good faith — that's overreach and they'll catch it. The principled bridge is *Leon*'s own carve-out: no objectively reasonable reliance where the officer *himself falsified the predicate*. Argue exclusion on falsified-certification grounds, with the stipulation as corroboration that no emergency existed.
4. *Agency-theory tension:* The rings are concentric, not contradictory, if you phrase ring 2 as *a fortiori* ("even courts that accept the doctrine recognize it cannot reach commanded generation") and note that passive CSLI is already covered by ring 1 via *Carpenter*. Never say "ordinary CSLI collection is within the doctrine."
5. *Gorsuch angle:* Yes, but contained (see idea 5). It is a fifth-vote insurance policy, not a theory of the case.
6. *Date typo (2010 vs. 2007):* Ignore it; treat as scrivener's error. If Respondent tries chronology games, a one-line footnote kills it. Don't spend credibility on it.

## Feedback for Respondent

**What's working.** The off-ramp architecture (stare decisis / no search / exigency) is sound litigation design; the three-feature decomposition of *Carpenter* (retrospectivity, comprehensiveness, indiscriminate accumulation) is the brief's best analytic passage; "scalpel, not wrecking ball" and the *Knotts*/*Oliver* pairing are effective; pre-rebutting the state cases as state-constitutional was smart.

**Vulnerabilities the other side will exploit.**
1. **The minimization is contestable on the record.** "A handful of pings," "two material facts" — the record says Doakes received location data "for the next several days" with no end date. Petitioner's brief has already called this out ("The Government will call this a single-ping case. The record refutes that."). A brief that gets caught shading the record loses the Court.
2. ***Carpenter*'s anti-voluntariness language is unanswered.** 585 U.S. at 315 — data generated "without any affirmative act . . . beyond powering up"; user does not "voluntarily assume the risk." Your tower-registration answer addresses passive CSLI, not the on-demand ping. You are simultaneously arguing the doctrine applies and that you don't need it; Petitioner will expose the hedge — pick a lead theory (*Knotts*) and make voluntariness expressly secondary.
3. ***Kimble* is miscast.** *Kimble*'s "enhanced force" attaches to **statutory** precedents; *Miller* is constitutional, where stare decisis is *weakest* (*Payne*). Petitioner will pounce. Reframe as a legislative-competence argument (Congress can layer protections above the constitutional floor) without invoking *Kimble*'s heightened standard.
4. **The Oct. 8 gap.** Your exigency case is built on Oct. 10 facts, but tracking began Oct. 8 on a false certification by an officer the FBI had *frozen out* of the investigation. Expect "Counsel, what was the emergency on October 8th?" You need a ping-by-ping answer (below).
5. *Mincey* cuts against you on its facts (it rejected a murder-scene exception). Recast your exigency theory around *Warden v. Hayden* hot pursuit and prevention-of-imminent-harm, and distinguish *Mincey* (home search vs. minimally intrusive location fix) before Petitioner does it for you.

**New ideas to consider.**
1. **Severability / ping-by-ping suppression analysis.** Only the Oct. 10 fixes mattered evidentially. Argue the search inquiry attaches to each acquisition; even if the Oct. 8–9 stream were tainted, the Oct. 10 pings coincided with a live, in-progress homicide — your strongest exigency moment. This converts the "several days" problem into a materiality argument made *honestly*.
2. **Cite the post-*Carpenter* circuit cases** holding short-term real-time tracking is not a search — *United States v. Hammond*, 996 F.3d 374 (7th Cir. 2021) (hours of real-time CSLI); *United States v. Riley*, 858 F.3d 1012 (6th Cir. 2017) (real-time tracking of a fugitive for ~7 hours). Federal authority beats your current reliance on distinguishing state cases.
3. **Promote the no-suppression argument to a full alternative section.** Circuit law is uniform that the SCA provides no suppression remedy (e.g., *United States v. Guerrero*, 768 F.3d 351 (5th Cir. 2014)), and add harmless-error/independent-source color: the trunk contents, the independently discovered crime scene, the rangers. This is the Court's cheapest face-saving exit and you've left it as one sentence.
4. **An explicit hedge against a doctrine-hostile Court:** a standalone section arguing that *even if* the third-party doctrine were narrowed or retired, short-term locating of a phone moving through public space is not a search under *Katz* directly (*Knotts* needs no help from *Miller*). Decoupling your no-search argument from the doctrine means you can lose QP I and still win the judgment.
5. **Defuse Doakes structurally:** the remedy for a rogue officer is § 2707 civil liability, discipline, and prosecution — the Fourth Amendment rule must be written for the honest officer; constitutional doctrine should not be redesigned around its worst user. Say it once, prominently, rather than hoping the Court won't notice him.
6. **Preempt the Rule 41(b)(4) structural argument** you should expect from Petitioner: Congress distinguished police-installed tracking devices from carrier-derived records; the existence of a warrant pathway for the former says nothing about the latter.

**Answers to your questions.**
1. *Exigency weight:* Keep Part III but restructure as an expressly alternative holding centered on the Oct. 10 pings (severability idea above). Frame *Moore* as *respecting* Congress: the statute sets a higher bar than the Constitution; stipulating non-compliance with the statute concedes nothing constitutional. Yes, add attenuation/inevitable-discovery/no-suppression in Round 2 — as one consolidated "remedy" section, not three scattered ones.
2. *GPS vs. CSLI:* Resolve it affirmatively. The quarter-mile trial testimony is the only quantified accuracy in the record; argue "GPS data" in the suppression-motion sentence is a party label, not a finding. Ambiguity now favors Petitioner because his imagery is better; precision favors you.
3. *Doakes taint / independent source:* Viable as a fallback, best dressed as harmless error plus "at most a remand," not a primary ground — it is factbound, and the Court took this case for doctrine.
4. *Suppression remedy:* Promote it (idea 3). It is within fair scope as an alternative ground for affirmance.
5. *Gorsuch:* Preempt briefly in Round 2: Congress defined the subscriber's interest in carrier records *and* its remedies, which exclude suppression — a positive-law interest comes with positive-law remedies. Don't give it more than a paragraph.
6. *Tone:* Recalibrate exactly as you propose — "two evidentially material fixes from a brief, targeted collection" is defensible; "a handful of pings" is not.
