# Defendants' Counsel — Change Log & Reflections

## Round 1

### Change Log (response_v1.md vs. original Doc 151)

- **Reframed the entire response around the inherent-power/Chambers framework Plaintiffs actually invoked.** The original Doc 151 argued almost exclusively that the alleged conduct was not "fraud on the court" — but Plaintiffs' motion was brought under the Court's inherent power and spoliation doctrine, not Rule 60(d)(3). The revision opens with the inherent-power standard (Chambers, Kleiner, Roadway Express, Qantum — all drawn from the motion itself), the clear-and-convincing burden (taken from Plaintiffs' own Roche Diagnostics citation), and a section-by-section attack on the three Telectron elements that Plaintiffs themselves invoked at Doc. 145 at 14.
- **Added an attribution/bad-faith section (Argument II).** The original never exploited Plaintiffs' biggest evidentiary gap: their expert identified only a username "david," an Adobe Acrobat modification, and a February 17, 2025 save date — no named Defendant. The revision argues that inherent-power sanctions require party-specific bad-faith findings, distinguishes every case Plaintiffs cite (Garcia, Franklin Livestock, Forsberg, Art Remedy, Roche, Am. Rena) as involving adjudicated or admitted fabrication by the sanctioned party itself, and weaponizes Plaintiffs' own footnote 11 concession ("[e]ven if only one Defendant altered the documents") as an admission they cannot make individualized showings.
- **Added a dedicated no-prejudice section (Argument III) anchored to Doc. 135.** New material the original omitted entirely: the Court already construed the authentic agreements in Plaintiffs' favor on the motion to dismiss (rejecting Defendants' reading as "absurd"), Plaintiffs always possessed the authentic contracts (filed by Defendants themselves with the Begovic Declaration in July 2024), the altered documents were never filed or used, and Defendants offered a curative stipulation. Also recast Plaintiffs' claimed prejudice as fee-based and curable by lesser means, and noted Plaintiffs' own delay (discovery ~Aug. 2025; operative motion Apr. 13, 2026).
- **Added proportionality, due-process, and evidentiary-hearing arguments (Argument IV).** The original asked only for outright denial. The revision argues default judgment is a last-resort remedy, catalogs available lesser sanctions (drawing on Swofford, cited in the motion), and requests an evidentiary hearing before any inherent-power sanction — giving the Court a credible off-ramp short of granting relief on the paper record.
- **Added a section attacking the alternative relief (Argument V), which the original ignored.** The requested adverse inference deeming "breach" established is a merits windfall (the interpretation half is already decided by Doc. 135; the breach half would erase Plaintiffs' burden on causation/damages); spoliation doctrine (Optowave definition, from Plaintiffs' own motion) does not fit because nothing was destroyed or made unavailable; the special master request presupposes an adjudication of untrustworthiness that does not exist (distinguishing Pycsa and Cobell); fees are premature absent a bad-faith finding (Branch-Haines).
- **Retained but demoted the fraud-on-the-court argument to a single final section (Argument VI),** reframed as: because Plaintiffs seek default-judgment-level relief, the same egregiousness/court-directed-conduct requirement applies (ESM Group, Gupta, Patterson, Gulf Atlantic, Cerrato, Idearc, Council, Bassett, Guzman, DeBose, Bijou, Adams — all carried over from Doc 151).
- **Built out individualized-defendant arguments, especially for Begovic:** she made the only sworn representation about the contracts to the Court — authenticating the *authentic* agreements — and was already dismissed from Counts I and II; collective sanctions without individualized findings would be improper.
- **Added a proper Background section** (the original had none beyond a two-paragraph introduction), marshaling only record facts from the two source filings, sequenced to emphasize that the authentic agreements predate the dispute and that the merits ruling preceded the operative motion.
- **Removed risky rhetoric from the original** ("cosmic battle between good and evil"; the footnote suggesting Plaintiffs' confusion "could have been resolved by one phone call"), which invites a judicial perception that Defendants do not appreciate the gravity of fabrication allegations. The revised tone takes the allegations seriously while disputing attribution, prejudice, and proportionality.
- **Replaced the AI certification with the required bracketed simulation note** and marked the document "DRAFT — SIMULATION EXERCISE — NOT FOR FILING."

### Reflection

My core strategic judgment this round was that the original response's all-in bet on the fraud-on-the-court doctrine was a category error Plaintiffs would exploit in reply — they moved under Chambers and spoliation, so a response that answers only Rule 60-style fraud arguments concedes the actual battlefield. I rebuilt the brief to fight on Plaintiffs' chosen ground: attribution (who is "david"?), prejudice (the Court already gave Plaintiffs their contract interpretation on the authentic documents), and proportionality (hearing first, lesser sanctions second, default never). What worries me most is the smell of the underlying facts: identical smudges, a hidden hair, a modification date two weeks before production, and a compensation term that just happens to match Defendants' litigation position. A judge may view our attribution argument as too clever — FR8 controlled the files, and a court could find that circumstantial evidence of party involvement is itself clear and convincing, or simply order the hearing we requested and let the facts come out badly. I am also braced for Plaintiffs to argue that our "no prejudice because you already won the interpretation issue" point proves bad faith rather than negates harm (why alter documents unless to deceive?), and that the unamended discovery responses are each Defendant's own adoption of the altered documents. The evidentiary-hearing request is partly a shield and partly a gamble: it positions us as process-respecting, but it invites a proceeding our clients may not survive if the testimony goes wrong.
