# Plaintiffs' Counsel — Change Log & Reflections

## Round 1

### Change Log (motion_v1.md vs. original Doc 145)

- **Reframed the opening to deny the defense its standard.** Added a new lead paragraph in the Introduction expressly disclaiming the "fraud on the court" doctrine (citing *ESM Group* affirmatively, before the defense can) and anchoring the motion in *Chambers v. NASCO* inherent power, spoliation doctrine (*Optowave*'s "alteration" definition), and Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1). The original motion mentioned inherent power but never inoculated itself against being recharacterized as a fraud-on-the-court dismissal motion — which is exactly what Doc 151 did.
- **Added a new Argument Section I ("standard-selection" section)** with three subsections: (A) the governing inherent-power/spoliation standard requires no filing with the court, emphasizing that *Qantum*'s bad-faith formulation is disjunctive ("fraud has been practiced upon it, OR... delays or disrupts the litigation"); (B) a point-by-point distinguishing of every defense case (*Gulf Atlantic* — damages spreadsheets vs. forged signed contracts; *Idearc* — exhibits withdrawn there vs. unamended certified responses here; *Cerrato*/*Council* — "expose at trial" logic rejected by *Forsberg*; *Bassett*/*Bijou* — quotidian lies vs. forensically documented forgery; *Adams* — irrelevant medical bills vs. fabricated dispositive contracts; *Branch-Haines* — fees denied only because bad faith was unproven there); and (C) a fallback showing the conduct was in fact aimed at the Court's truth-finding function (Rule 26(g) certifications, pendency of the MTD, trajectory toward summary judgment).
- **Weaponized the timeline.** Foregrounded the nine-day gap between the forensic alteration date (Feb. 17, 2025, per Hoffman ¶ 15) and service of Kajdic/Begovic's certified RFP responses identifying Bates D002379-D005171 (Feb. 26, 2025), and tied both to the pendency of the motion to dismiss that crystallized the interpretation dispute (filed Oct. 11, 2024). The original motion recited these dates in separate sections but never connected them; the revision uses them as a means-motive-timing willfulness triad.
- **Converted the discovery responses into "reliance" and "representation" evidence.** Directly rebutted Doc 151's claims that Defendants "never relied on" the contracts and made "no representation" about their provenance: identifying forged documents as "the Independent Contractor Agreements" and as the sole support for the merits contention, under a Rule 26(g) certification, is both. Also rebutted the "one phone call" footnote with the record fact that conferral triggered two withdrawals of counsel citing "ethical concerns."
- **Sharpened the attack on the stipulation.** Recast it as conceding an undisputed point (which contracts govern) while avoiding any acknowledgment, explanation, or identification of the forger; positioned it as weaker than the self-disclosure held insufficient in *Franklin Livestock*, and argued it is functionally less than exclusion — a free reset for a failed forgery — under *Carroll-Harris*'s deterrence logic.
- **Embraced the clear-and-convincing burden** rather than ducking it, characterizing the forensic record (page substitution, identical smudge across 21 agreements, hidden hairs, Acrobat metadata, user "david") as unrebutted by any defense declaration or expert.
- **Highlighted Defendants' non-denial.** Doc 151 argues "even if true"; the revision repeatedly notes Defendants never deny, explain, or attribute the alteration — converting their litigation posture into evidence supporting the bad-faith finding.
- **Added a new item of alternative relief**: an order compelling amended RFP responses within 14 days accounting for the provenance of Bates D002379-D005171, which gives the Court a graduated remedial option and underscores the ongoing Rule 26(e)/37(c) violation.
- **Housekeeping**: corrected "Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act"; moved the spoliation definition and Rule 37(c) footnotes into the body; consolidated the forensic evidence into a numbered list; replaced the AI certification with the required bracketed simulation note; added the "DRAFT — SIMULATION EXERCISE — NOT FOR FILING" header; kept all citations strictly within the two source documents (no [VERIFY] flags needed).

### Reflection

My core strategic judgment this round was that the defense's response is strong only if the judge accepts its premise — that this is a fraud-on-the-court motion subject to *ESM Group*'s "defile the court itself" standard. So I spent my best material denying them that premise in the first paragraph rather than rebutting it in the back half, and I built a standalone standard-selection section so the magistrate judge has a clean analytical off-ramp: inherent power and spoliation, not Rule 60(d). My second move was the timeline — Feb. 17 alteration, Feb. 26 certified responses — because judges sanction stories, not doctrines, and that nine-day gap is the story. What worries me: the defense will say *Gulf Atlantic* and *Cerrato* denied "sanctions," not just dismissal-for-fraud, so my doctrinal distinction may look like a label game; a judge could also find default too harsh where the authentic contracts were always in the record and the Court has already adopted Plaintiffs' interpretation (Doc. 135), which undercuts my prejudice-on-the-merits narrative — Defendants will argue the forgery, even if real, was a failed and now harmless gambit. I also lean heavily on Rule 26(g) without record evidence of who signed which response, and on out-of-circuit district cases (*Roche*, *Franklin Livestock*, *Carroll-Harris*) for the heaviest relief. If the judge splits the baby, I expect fees, a special master, and compelled amended responses rather than default — which is why I made the alternative relief section more granular and attractive this round.
