# Judge's Round 1 Feedback (Brainstorming Session)

*Husidic v. FR8 Solutions, Inc., No. 3:24-cv-00963 (M.D. Fla.) — Simulated exercise. This is coaching, not a ruling. No winner is picked here.*

Both drafts are substantially improved over the originals. Importantly, the parties are now finally arguing about the same motion: Plaintiffs have disclaimed fraud-on-the-court, and Defendants have (mostly) stopped responding as if that were the only theory on the table. The fight is now where it should be: attribution, prejudice, proportionality, and process. My comments follow.

---

## To Plaintiffs' Counsel

### Strengths

1. **The preemptive reframing is your best structural move.** The Introduction's first line — "This is not a 'fraud on the court' motion, and Plaintiffs do not invoke that doctrine" — and Section I.B's strawman analysis neutralize the entire architecture of Defendants' original response (Doc. 151), which was built wall-to-wall on *ESM Group*, *Gupta*, and *Patterson*. Your disjunctive reading of the *Qantum* bad-faith formulation ("fraud has been practiced upon it, **or** ... delays or disrupts the litigation") is the right doctrinal lever, and *Forsberg*'s "it would be strange to require a district court to wait" language is well deployed.

2. **The means/motive/timing triad in Section II.A is the most persuasive paragraph either side has written.** The February 17, 2025 Adobe save date (Ex. E ¶ 15), nine days before the February 26, 2025 Rule 26(g)-certified responses identifying *only* the altered Bates range as the merits evidence (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18), while the motion to dismiss contesting that exact term was pending — that is a genuinely powerful circumstantial case for willfulness, and you present it cleanly. The "identical smudge across twenty-one agreements" detail is the kind of concrete, visual fact that decides sanctions motions.

3. **Your point-by-point distinguishing of *Gulf Atlantic*, *Cerrato*, *Idearc*, *Bassett*, *Bijou*, and *Adams* (§ I.B) is disciplined and effective** — particularly the *Idearc* observation that the offending exhibits there were "withdrawn well before a finding of fact," whereas Defendants' certified responses here "remain operative and unamended to this day." That contrast does real work, and it directly answers the stipulation-mootness argument. The *Franklin Livestock* a-fortiori move (their stipulation is weaker than the self-disclosure found insufficient there) is likewise well constructed.

### Weaknesses & Risks

1. **The attribution gap is your soft underbelly, and you are papering over it rather than confronting it.** Your own draft says "*someone* digitally altered" the agreements (Intro) and identifies only "a username 'david'" (Ex. E ¶ 12). Footnote 5 — "[e]ven if only one Defendant altered the documents, sanctions are justified as to all Defendants" — reads as a concession that you cannot say *who* did it. Defendants' Round 1 response makes this the centerpiece of their opposition (§§ II.A–B), and they are right that every terminating-sanctions case you cite (*Garcia*, *Franklin Livestock*, *Roche*, *Forsberg*) involved a finding or admission that *the sanctioned party itself* fabricated or knowingly submitted the evidence. You ask me to default three separate defendants — including Begovic, who put the *authentic* agreements before the Court under oath (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18–19) and has already been partially dismissed (Doc. 135 at 15) — on an inference stacked on an anonymous username. As drafted, the motion gives me no individualized path to a bad-faith finding against any natural person.

2. **You never ask for an evidentiary hearing, and that silence is dangerous.** Before imposing inherent-power sanctions — certainly case-terminating ones — due process requires notice and an opportunity to be heard, and a default here will draw appellate scrutiny. Defendants have shrewdly requested a hearing as their fallback (Response § IV), which lets them look reasonable while you look like you're trying to win the case on paper. If your forensic evidence is as strong as you say, a hearing is your friend: ask for one in the alternative, with targeted provenance discovery (see Ideas below). Relatedly, your repeated refrain that the evidence is "unrebutted" proves less than you think — Defendants have not yet been put to their proof at a hearing; "unrebutted on a paper record" is not a substitute for findings.

3. **The adverse-inference alternative is half-moot and half-overreaching.** Doc. 135 already held the agreements unambiguous and adopted your interpretation. Asking me to "deem admitted and established" the *interpretation* (§ III.A) asks me to decree what I have already decided — Defendants' Response § V.A lands this punch squarely. And deeming *breach* "admitted and established" for 22 plaintiffs would relieve you of proving performance, breach, causation, and damages — that is a default judgment in costume, and the draft does not even attempt to justify it as proportionate. Separately, your spoliation framing is awkward: *Optowave*'s definition includes "alteration," but adverse inferences classically remedy an *evidentiary gap*, and you have none — you possess the authentic agreements and always have. Expect that argument (Response § V.A makes it) and answer it, or recast the alternative relief entirely.

4. **The Rule 26(g)/37(c)(1) scaffolding needs shoring up.** Rule 26(g) certifications are made by *the signing attorney*, and your own Exhibit F is designed to exonerate prior counsel — so the certification theory attributes the falsity to "Defendants" collectively without telling me which client furnished the documents to counsel. Also verify that *Dragon Jade* actually supports sanctions under Rule 37(c)(1) for failure to *amend an RFP response* (as opposed to failure to supplement disclosures); the fit between Rule 26(e) and a "we will produce Bates range X" response is arguable, and exclusion — Rule 37(c)(1)'s headline remedy — is relief Defendants would happily accept. Finally, the original motion's footnote 10 cited Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(d)(1) for the one-deposition/seven-hour limit; that is the wrong rule (see Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii), 30(d)(1)). Your v1 footnote 6 dropped the citation rather than fixing it — fix it.

### Ideas for Round 2

1. **Differentiate the defendants and build an agency theory against FR8.** A corporation acts only through agents. The altered documents came out of FR8's files, were assembled into FR8's "personnel files," and were handed by "Defendants" to counsel for production (Ex. F ¶¶ 3–5). Whoever "david" is, if the alteration occurred within FR8's document-collection process, FR8 is responsible regardless of which human pushed the button. Consider seeking default (or the harshest relief) against **FR8**, with tailored lesser sanctions against Kajdic and Begovic pending a hearing. A calibrated ask is far more grantable than "default all three," and it defuses the strongest section of Defendants' response (their Begovic argument, § II.B).

2. **Turn the attribution gap into a burden-shifting sword and a discovery demand.** The forgeries were created while the documents were in Defendants' exclusive custody and surfaced in Defendants' own production. Argue that, on that showing, the burden shifts to Defendants to come forward with an innocent explanation — and emphasize the conspicuous silence: in eight-plus months, *no Defendant has submitted a sworn declaration denying involvement or knowledge*, and no one has identified "david." Then ask, as interim relief, for: (a) an evidentiary hearing; (b) expedited provenance discovery (identification of all custodians who touched Bates range D002379–D005171, forensic imaging of the relevant machines, a deposition of "david" once identified — not counted against your merits depositions); and (c) an order compelling amended Rule 26 responses within 14 days (you already ask for this — elevate it). Note also a fact you under-use: the *authentic* agreements were apparently absent from the personnel files produced — that nonproduction is itself concealment.

3. **Rebuild the alternative relief around the damages phase, where the prejudice actually lives.** Your best prejudice fact is that you agreed to accept Defendant-prepared Excel summaries of damages data in lieu of underlying records. Instead of an inference duplicating Doc. 135, ask for: production of all underlying source documents at Defendants' expense; a presumption against the accuracy of any Defendant-prepared summary; cost-shifting for verification; a neutral forensic examiner (a cheaper, more proportionate cousin of the special master); and a ruling that the fabrication evidence is admissible at trial as evidence of consciousness of the weakness of Defendants' position. These are remedies a cautious judge can grant without writing a default-judgment opinion. Also: explicitly embrace the clear-and-convincing standard up front and explain why you meet it — owning the burden builds credibility. And consider anchoring your sanctions framework in published Eleventh Circuit authority on inherent-power terminating sanctions (e.g., *Eagle Hospital Physicians, LLC v. SRG Consulting, Inc.*, 561 F.3d 1298 (11th Cir. 2009); *In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) on due-process prerequisites) **[VERIFY holdings and pin cites]** — your current spine is unpublished and out-of-circuit district decisions.

### Questions from the Bench

1. "Counsel, point me to the evidence — not inference, evidence — that Mr. Kajdic or Ms. Begovic personally altered, directed, or knew of the alteration before September 24, 2025. If you cannot, why should I not at minimum hold an evidentiary hearing before entering judgment against either of them — and what sanction do you say survives against Ms. Begovic specifically, given that she filed the authentic contracts with this Court?"

2. "I construed the agreements in your favor in Doc. 135, you hold the authentic contracts, and Defendants will stipulate they govern. Walk me through the concrete, forward-looking harm that a fee award, compelled amended responses, source-document production, and a verification protocol would *not* cure — and explain how deeming 'breach' established for 22 plaintiffs is anything other than a default judgment by another name."

## To Defendants' Counsel

### Strengths

1. **The strategic pivot was essential and well executed.** The original response (Doc. 151) bet everything on fraud-on-the-court — a doctrine Plaintiffs now expressly disclaim — and would have lost the framing war outright. Round 1's restructuring around (i) attribution and individualized bad faith, (ii) absence of prejudice, and (iii) proportionality/due process puts you on the strongest available ground. The Introduction's three-reasons roadmap is clean and judge-friendly.

2. **Section II is your best work.** The case-by-case demonstration that every authority Plaintiffs cite involved a *finding or admission* that the sanctioned party itself fabricated evidence (*Garcia*, *Franklin Livestock*, *Forsberg* plea agreement, *Roche*, *Art Remedy*, *Am. Rena*) is exactly the right way to cabin their precedent. The Begovic-specific argument (§ II.B) — the only sworn statement she ever made attached the *authentic* agreements, which the Court construed in Plaintiffs' favor — is genuinely compelling and may well save at least one client from the worst outcome. Quoting Plaintiffs' own footnote ("[e]ven if only one Defendant altered the documents...") as an admission of the attribution gap is sharp advocacy.

3. **The mootness and adverse-inference critiques are doctrinally sound.** Section III.A's point that the merits question was "already decided in Plaintiffs' favor on the authentic agreements" before this motion was filed, and Section V.A's point that an adverse inference "presupposes an evidentiary gap; Plaintiffs have none," are the two arguments most likely to limit relief even if I find misconduct. The evidentiary-hearing request (§ IV) is smart positioning: it is the reasonable ask, and it converts your evidentiary weakness into a process argument.

### Weaknesses & Risks

1. **You never address the nine-day timing — the single most damaging fact in the record.** Plaintiffs' draft bolds it: alteration saved February 17, 2025; Kajdic's and Begovic's certified responses identifying *only* the altered Bates range served February 26, 2025; all while the motion to dismiss contesting that exact compensation term was pending. Your response characterizes the forensic showing as documents "modified by someone, at some time" (Intro) — that understates a declaration that supplies a date, a tool, a username, and replicated artifacts across 21 documents, and the mischaracterization will cost you credibility. You need a theory of February 17: if documents were being scanned, OCR'd, or processed by a vendor or staff for the upcoming production, *say so in a sworn declaration*. Silence concedes the inference.

2. **The absence of any sworn denial is becoming the elephant in the courtroom.** Eight-plus months after the September 24, 2025 letter: no declaration from any Defendant denying alteration or knowledge; no identification of "david"; no description of any internal investigation; no amended Rule 26 responses. Your brief argues Plaintiffs haven't proven bad faith — fine — but at a hearing the first question will be why three clients facing accusations of forgery have said nothing under oath. If there are good answers (including, candidly, Fifth Amendment exposure), you need a plan for that question now. Relatedly, you say nothing at all about two successive sets of your clients' prior counsel withdrawing, one citing "ethical concerns" and an inability to "meaningfully produce documents" (Doc. 107 ¶ 7). I understand the instinct to stay silent, but Plaintiffs feature it prominently, and unexplained silence lets them write the narrative.

3. **Your "Plaintiffs' delay" argument (Background; § III.C) is overplayed and partly misleading.** Plaintiffs raised the issue on September 24, 2025 and filed their *first* sanctions motion on **October 7, 2025** — two weeks later. The ensuing delay was driven by your clients' own counsel withdrawals, the Court's order requiring conferral with new counsel (Doc. 116), and a stipulated stay for mediation. Framing this as "eight months" of plaintiff-side sleeping will be punctured in about thirty seconds at argument. Trim it to the modest point it supports (August-to-September gap), or cut it.

4. **Two doctrinal stretches risk undermining your credibility.** *First*, § II.B's assertion that the discovery responses "are not sworn representations of authenticity by any Defendant," citing *Guzman* — *Guzman* is a fraud-on-the-court case about conduct directed at the court; it does not address Rule 26(g), and counsel's signature *is* a certification, made after inquiry of the client, that the response was correct when made. The responses identified the altered range as the support for *Defendants'* merits contention; minimizing them as "standard objections" identifying "a Bates range" will not survive a side-by-side reading. *Second*, § VI's claim that a default-level inherent-power sanction "demands the same species of egregious, court-directed misconduct" as fraud on the court is a doctrinal overreach — *Chambers* requires bad faith, not court-directedness, and Plaintiffs' *Forsberg* quotation directly contradicts the "wait for trial" logic you import from *Idearc*/*Great Coastal*. Section VI as written invites me to conclude you still haven't let go of the losing frame; recast it as a pure proportionality argument or cut it sharply.

### Ideas for Round 2

1. **Fill the evidentiary vacuum — or strategically explain it.** The single highest-value addition would be declarations: each individual Defendant denying alteration, direction, and pre-September 2025 knowledge; a custodian or IT declaration describing how the production set was assembled, who "david" is (or what the investigation to identify him entailed), and what happened on or around February 17, 2025. An innocent provenance story (records clerk reassembling incomplete files, a vendor processing scans) — if true and sworn — transforms this motion. If your clients cannot or will not provide such declarations, you should know why before the hearing you yourselves requested, because you will get one.

2. **Upgrade the stipulation into a full remediation package.** Right now Plaintiffs' *Franklin Livestock* a-fortiori argument lands: your offered stipulation concedes only what was never disputed, acknowledges nothing, and leaves the certified responses untouched. Consider offering, affirmatively in the brief: (a) amended Rule 26 responses within 14 days identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing reliance on D002379–D005171; (b) formal withdrawal/clawback of the altered documents and a stipulation that the fact of alteration may be presented to the factfinder; (c) cooperation with a neutral third-party forensic examination of the relevant systems at Defendants' expense (cheaper and narrower than Plaintiffs' special master); and (d) payment of Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert costs without admission. A credible remediation package is your best argument that "lesser sanctions" suffice — the *Telectron* third element — and it costs far less than a default.

3. **Sharpen proportionality with stakes and severance.** Quantify what default means: judgment on RICO claims carrying treble damages and fees, FDUTPA, and Truth in Leasing Act claims for 22 plaintiffs, entered without trial, in a putative class action. Argue that even on Plaintiffs' best record, the misconduct is attributable (at most) to one unidentified actor within FR8's orbit, so any sanction must be severed by defendant and calibrated to findings made after a hearing — and offer to be formally bound by Doc. 135's contract interpretation for all purposes, including trial, which fully extinguishes the first half of the requested adverse inference. Also engage the Excel-spreadsheet prejudice point *specifically* (Plaintiffs' § II.B item 3): offer production of underlying source records for damages, which removes the only forward-looking prejudice they articulate.

### Questions from the Bench

1. "Counsel, it has been more than eight months since the September 24, 2025 letter. Why have your clients' discovery responses still not been amended; why has no Defendant submitted a declaration denying involvement or knowledge; and who is 'david'? If the answer to any of these is privilege or potential criminal exposure, say so now, because I will draw the inferences the law permits from continued silence."

2. "If, as you argue, the 21 altered documents are irrelevant to the merits and were never relied upon, why did each of your clients identify those documents — and only those documents — in certified responses as the evidence supporting their contention that Plaintiffs are not entitled to 88% of gross revenue per load? And what would your clients have done with those documents at summary judgment had Plaintiffs never noticed the discrepancy?"

## Judge's General Observations

**Craft and structure.** Both Round 1 drafts are markedly better than the originals. Plaintiffs' draft is comprehensive but running long; Section I.B's case-distinguishing, while effective, could be compressed by a third without losing force. Defendants' draft is appropriately lean; its weakness is omission (timing, withdrawals, declarations), not bloat. Plaintiffs' tone occasionally tips from forceful into prosecutorial ("harvested," "dead letter," "should not be heard to claim credit for the fortuity that they were caught first") — most of it is earned by the record, but the speculative line that the altered agreements "were on a path to summary judgment and trial" asserts a counterfactual the record cannot support; soften to "absent detection, nothing prevented their use." Defendants wisely abandoned the original's sarcasm ("a cosmic battle between good and evil"); the residual minimization ("modified by someone, at some time") should go the same way — understatement of strong adverse evidence reads as evasion, not advocacy.

**The real battleground for Round 2.** Neither side has adequately briefed (1) the **standard of proof** — Defendants assert clear-and-convincing via *Roche*; Plaintiffs never expressly accept or contest it; both should brief what the Eleventh Circuit actually requires for inherent-power findings; (2) **due process mechanics** — what notice, hearing, and findings are prerequisites to each tier of relief (*In re Mroz* and progeny **[VERIFY]**); and (3) **attribution doctrine** — when corporate-agent misconduct is attributable to the entity, and what individualized findings are needed for individual defendants. The motion will likely be won or lost on those three questions, not on the dueling string cites currently filling both briefs.

**Citation and record-support flags.**

- Both sides rely overwhelmingly on unpublished district-court decisions and out-of-circuit authority (*Garcia* (10th Cir.), *Franklin Livestock* (E.D.N.C.), *Carroll-Harris* (E.D. Mich.), *Martin v. SGT* (D. Utah), *Am. Rena* (C.D. Cal.)). Neither cites published, controlling Eleventh Circuit authority on inherent-power terminating sanctions. Both sides should fix this in Round 2.
- *Forsberg* is an unpublished Eleventh Circuit decision (634 F. App'x). Plaintiffs repeatedly characterize it as the Eleventh Circuit having "squarely rejected" a proposition; it is persuasive only and should be labeled as such.
- Unverifiable/very recent citations both sides should confirm exist and hold what is claimed: *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026) (cited by both); *Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011 (N.D. Fla. 2025); *White Cap., L.P. v. Heyden Enters., LLC*, 2025 WL 414721 (S.D. Fla. 2025). **[VERIFY all three]**
- Defendants' use of *Guzman* (§ II.B) to argue that unamended Rule 26 responses are "not sworn representations of authenticity by any Defendant" stretches a fraud-on-the-court case well past its holding. Find authority actually addressing the legal effect of Rule 26(g) certifications, or drop the cite.
- Plaintiffs should confirm *Dragon Jade* supports Rule 37(c)(1) sanctions for failure to amend *RFP responses* specifically, and must correct the deposition-limit rule citation (the original's Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(d)(1) is wrong; the limits live in Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii) and 30(d)(1) — v1 footnote 6 currently cites nothing).
- Plaintiffs' quotation chain in § II.B item 3 (*White Cap* quoting *Bernal*) and the *Access Innovators*-quoting-*Qantum* parenthetical (pin cite 1269 vs. 1268-69 appears inconsistent across the draft) need pin-cite hygiene.
- Record-support: Plaintiffs' bolded nine-day timeline is supported (Ex. E ¶ 15; Ex. B service date) and is fair argument. Defendants' "eight months" delay framing omits the October 7, 2025 first sanctions motion (Doc. 108) — as drafted it is materially misleading and should be corrected.
- Both drafts properly flag the AI-certification issue as a simulation artifact; in a real filing under this Court's standing order, that certification is not optional.

*— Round 2 drafts should respond to this feedback. The Round 3 ruling will turn substantially on attribution, proof standard, and proportionality.*
