## DEFENDANTS' JOINT RESPONSE IN OPPOSITION TO PLAINTIFFS' MOTION FOR SANCTIONS (DOC. 145)

Defendants, FR8 Solutions, Inc. ("FR8"), Alen Kajdic ("Mr. Kajdic"), and Azra Begovic ("Ms. Begovic"), respond in opposition to Plaintiffs' Motion for Sanctions (Doc. 145) (the "Motion"), and state as follows:

## INTRODUCTION

Plaintiffs ask this Court to exercise its inherent power—"the most extraordinary of all remedies," *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026)—to enter a case-ending default judgment against three separate Defendants based on 21 documents, out of a production approaching 49,000 documents, that Plaintiffs' retained expert believes were digitally modified by someone, at some time, before they were produced. The Motion should be denied for three independent reasons.

**First**, the inherent power may be invoked against a party only upon a finding of *bad faith by that party*, established by clear and convincing evidence. Plaintiffs' own forensic showing identifies no Defendant. It identifies a username—"david"—that belongs to none of the three named Defendants, and a software program (Adobe Acrobat) and a date (February 17, 2025). It does not establish *who* altered the documents, *whether* any named Defendant knew of or directed any alteration, or *when* any Defendant learned of it. A default judgment against FR8, Mr. Kajdic, and Ms. Begovic—collectively and without individualized findings—cannot rest on an inference stacked upon an anonymous username.

**Second**, Plaintiffs cannot show prejudice. There has never been any dispute about which contracts govern: the authentic Independent Contractor Agreements have been in Plaintiffs' own possession at all times, were filed with this Court by *Defendants themselves* in July 2024 as exhibits to Ms. Begovic's sworn Declaration (Doc. 16-2), and were attested by her to be true and accurate copies—a stipulation Plaintiffs accept. (Doc. 145 at 4.) More to the point, this Court has *already construed those authentic agreements in Plaintiffs' favor*, adopting Plaintiffs' "rational interpretation" and rejecting Defendants' reading as leading to an "absurd result." (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) The challenged documents were never filed with the Court, never offered in support of any motion, and never relied upon for any purpose. Defendants have offered to stipulate that any documents contradicting the agreements attached to Ms. Begovic's Declaration are not the operative agreements. There is no merits prejudice to remedy.

**Third**, even if some sanctionable conduct could ultimately be attributed to a particular Defendant after an evidentiary hearing, the relief Plaintiffs demand is wildly disproportionate. Default judgment is a remedy of last resort, and the alternative relief—an adverse inference *deeming Defendants' breach established*, plus a special master at Defendants' sole expense—would hand Plaintiffs a merits windfall untethered from any harm actually suffered.

At minimum, the serious factual questions raised by the Motion—who altered the documents, when, and with whose knowledge—cannot be resolved on a paper record. Due process and this Circuit's precedent require an evidentiary hearing before any inherent-power sanction, let alone a case-terminating one, may be imposed.

## BACKGROUND

**The authentic agreements have been before the Court—placed there by Defendants—since July 2024.** On July 16, 2024, in support of their motion to dismiss, Defendants filed the sworn Declaration of Ms. Begovic attaching "true and accurate copies of the Independent Contractor Agreements, offered to and executed by each of the named Plaintiffs." (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19; Docs. 16-3 through 16-23.) Plaintiffs stipulate these are the true and correct copies of the contracts they signed. (Doc. 145 at 4; Composite Ex. A ¶ 5.) These are the only Independent Contractor Agreements Defendants have ever placed before this Court or relied upon for any purpose.

**The Court has already resolved the contract-interpretation question in Plaintiffs' favor.** On February 19, 2026, the Court ruled on Defendants' motion to dismiss the First Amended Complaint, held the authentic Independent Contractor Agreements unambiguous, rejected Defendants' proposed interpretation as producing an "absurd result," and adopted Plaintiffs' interpretation of the plain contractual language. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) The Court dismissed Counts I and II against Ms. Begovic. (Doc. 135 at 15.)

**The challenged production.** In early March 2025, Defendants' then-counsel produced responsive documents in hardcopy—five banker's boxes of what appeared to be personnel files—in the form provided to counsel, followed by an electronic production on April 8, 2025. (Doc. 145 at 7; Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) Within Bates range D002379–D005171 were Independent Contractor Agreements for the named Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs allege that 21 of those agreements contain a compensation term ("88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence") that differs from the authentic agreements, and their retained forensic examiner, Mr. Hoffman, opines that certain pages were modified using Adobe Acrobat—one document saved on February 17, 2025, and another bearing a modification attributed to a username "david." (Doc. 145 at 10-11; Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15.) Mr. Hoffman's declaration does not identify any of the three named Defendants as having made, directed, or known of any modification.

**Plaintiffs' delay.** Plaintiffs discovered the alleged discrepancies "around August 2025" (Doc. 145 at 8), but did not raise them with Defendants until September 24, 2025. Plaintiffs' first sanctions motion (Doc. 108) was denied without prejudice on October 15, 2025, to permit conferral with Defendants' new counsel, and the case was stayed through January 2026 pending mediation. (Docs. 116, 123, 124, 128, 130, 133.) Plaintiffs did not file the operative Motion until April 13, 2026—some eight months after their discovery of the issue, and nearly two months after this Court had already construed the authentic contracts in their favor.

**Defendants' offered stipulation.** During conferral, Defendants offered to stipulate that "any documents containing writings that contradict those attached to the Declaration [of Ms. Begovic] are not the operative agreements for the respective plaintiff drivers." (Doc. 145 at 13.) Plaintiffs declined to resolve the issue on that basis.

## ARGUMENT AND MEMORANDUM OF LAW

### I. Inherent-Power Sanctions Require Clear and Convincing Evidence of Bad Faith by the Party to Be Sanctioned, and Default Judgment Is a Remedy of Last Resort.

Plaintiffs bring their Motion under "this Court's inherent power to safeguard the administration of justice." (Doc. 145 at 3.) That power exists "to protect the orderly administration of justice and to preserve the dignity of the tribunal," *Kleiner v. First Nat'l Bank of Atlanta*, 751 F.2d 1193, 1209 (11th Cir. 1985) (citing *Roadway Express v. Piper*, 447 U.S. 752, 764-65 (1980)), and includes "the ability to fashion an appropriate sanction for conduct which abuses the judicial process." *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1991). But the inherent power is not a free-floating license to punish. The key to its invocation is a finding of *bad faith*: "[b]ad faith exists when the court finds that a fraud has been practiced upon it, or that the very temple of justice has been defiled, or where a party or attorney knowingly or recklessly raises a frivolous argument, delays or disrupts the litigation, or hampers the enforcement of a court order." *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1268-69 (S.D. Fla. 2007) (cleaned up).

Plaintiffs' own authorities confirm that the predicate misconduct must be "demonstrated by clear and convincing evidence." *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020); *see also Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021) (fee request "unavailing" where movant "failed to prove fraud on the court or bad faith"). And before entering the case-terminating sanction Plaintiffs primarily seek, this Court must find that: (1) Defendants acted willfully or in bad faith; (2) Plaintiffs were prejudiced by Defendants' conduct; and (3) lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987); (Doc. 145 at 14 (invoking the same elements)). Because a default judgment "is the most extraordinary of all remedies, [it] should be reserved for the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct." *Bijou*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1.

Plaintiffs cannot satisfy a single *Telectron* element, much less all three by clear and convincing evidence.

### II. Plaintiffs Have Not Shown—By Clear and Convincing Evidence or Otherwise—That Any Named Defendant Acted Willfully or in Bad Faith.

#### A. Plaintiffs' forensic showing identifies a username, not a Defendant.

The entirety of Plaintiffs' attribution evidence is this: their retained examiner opines that pages of certain agreements were modified in Adobe Acrobat; that one document was saved as a new document on February 17, 2025; and that one modification is associated with "a username 'david.'" (Doc. 145 at 10-11; Ex. E ¶¶ 12, 15.) None of the named Defendants is "david." Mr. Hoffman's declaration does not say—because it cannot—that FR8, Mr. Kajdic, or Ms. Begovic altered any document, directed any alteration, or knew of any alteration before Plaintiffs' September 24, 2025 letter. The affidavit of Defendants' former counsel (Ex. F) establishes only that *counsel* made no alterations beyond Bates stamping and redactions; it says nothing about who, within or outside FR8's organization—an employee, a vendor, a scanning service, or anyone else—touched the documents, or under whose direction.

That gap is fatal. Inherent-power sanctions punish a *party's* bad faith, and every authority Plaintiffs cite involved a court's finding, on a developed record, that the sanctioned party itself knowingly fabricated or knowingly submitted false evidence:

- In *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009), the district court found *the plaintiff herself* fabricated at least four documents supporting her own claims.
- In *Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962 (E.D.N.C. 2017), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018), the plaintiff *admitted* it had produced doctored records.
- In *Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 678 (11th Cir. 2015), the sanctioned party admitted *in a criminal plea agreement* that he knew the statement was false and had been submitted to the district court.
- In *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019), the party "fabricated blog posts and repeatedly relied upon and presented them" to the court "to obtain a dismissal and a sum of money."
- In *Roche Diagnostics*, the defendants produced hundreds of forged invoices *in response to a court order* and never corrected, explained, or acknowledged them. 2020 WL 2308319, at *7.
- In *Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, 2015 WL 12732433 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015), the defendants submitted fraudulent declarations in multiple court filings.

Here, by contrast, there has been no finding—indeed, no evidence—connecting any named Defendant to the alleged alterations. Plaintiffs ask the Court to leap from "someone with the username 'david' modified PDFs in Acrobat" to "FR8, Mr. Kajdic, and Ms. Begovic willfully fabricated evidence in bad faith." Clear and convincing evidence does not work by assumption.

#### B. Plaintiffs concede they cannot make individualized showings, and collective sanctions are improper.

Plaintiffs' own Motion concedes the attribution problem: "[e]ven if only one Defendant altered the documents," Plaintiffs argue, "sanctions would still be justified as to all Defendants because they each rely on the Altered Agreements in their respective discovery responses, which have not been amended." (Doc. 145 at 18 n.11.) That is an admission that Plaintiffs cannot say which Defendant—if any—did anything wrongful, and an invitation to impose the most severe sanction known to civil litigation *collectively*, without individualized findings of bad faith.

The invitation should be declined, most obviously as to Ms. Begovic. The only sworn representation Ms. Begovic has ever made to this Court concerning the contracts was her July 2024 Declaration attaching and authenticating the *authentic* agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19)—the very agreements Plaintiffs stipulate are true and correct, and the very agreements this Court construed in Plaintiffs' favor. It would be perverse to enter a default judgment against the Defendant who put the authentic contracts before the Court, on claims as to which she has already been partially dismissed (Doc. 135 at 15), based on documents she is not shown to have touched.

Nor do the unamended discovery responses supply the missing bad faith. Those responses—served by then-counsel, containing standard objections and identifying a Bates range within a production of personnel files maintained in the ordinary course—are not sworn representations of authenticity by any Defendant. *See Guzman v. Holiday CVS, LLC*, 2022 WL 22824873, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Oct. 13, 2022). And following Plaintiffs' September 24, 2025 letter, Defendants offered precisely the corrective Plaintiffs now say was required: a stipulation that any documents contradicting the agreements attached to Ms. Begovic's Declaration are not the operative agreements. (Doc. 145 at 13.) Whatever else may be said of that sequence, it is not clear and convincing evidence that each of three separate Defendants willfully fabricated evidence.

### III. Plaintiffs Have Not Been Prejudiced.

#### A. Plaintiffs always possessed the authentic agreements, and this Court has already adopted Plaintiffs' interpretation of them.

The second *Telectron* element fails for a simple reason: the alleged alteration could not, and did not, deprive Plaintiffs of anything. Plaintiffs signed the authentic agreements, kept them, attached them to their pleadings, and stipulate that the copies Defendants filed with Ms. Begovic's Declaration in July 2024 are true and correct. (Doc. 145 at 4.) This is therefore not a case in which falsified evidence "cast[] doubt on the veracity" of the operative proof, *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1180, because the operative proof has been fixed, filed, and undisputed since the first months of the case.

More fundamentally, the merits question the challenged documents supposedly targeted—the meaning of the compensation term—has *already been decided in Plaintiffs' favor on the authentic agreements*. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) The Court held the agreements unambiguous, rejected Defendants' interpretation as "absurd," and adopted Plaintiffs' reading. The challenged documents did not delay, distort, or in any way affect that ruling. A fabrication that influences nothing is not the stuff of case-ending sanctions. *Cf. Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (severity warranted "because the doctored evidence cuts to the very essence" of the claims). Here the allegedly doctored evidence cut to the essence of nothing: the essence of the case was decided on the authentic documents, against Defendants' interpretation, before this Motion was even filed.

#### B. The challenged documents were never filed, never used, and have been disavowed.

The 21 challenged documents were never presented to this Court, never offered in support of any motion, never used at any deposition or hearing, and—through Defendants' offered stipulation—affirmatively disavowed as non-operative. *See Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018 (N.D. Fla. 2025) (noting allegedly fabricated materials "were not filed with the Court (except as exhibits to this motion for sanctions)"); *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009) (where challenged exhibits are withdrawn "well before a finding of fact or before summary judgment has been considered," they "have not nor will they impact or subvert the functioning of the judicial process no matter how fraudulent the exhibits may or may not be").

#### C. The prejudice Plaintiffs actually allege is fee-based and curable by lesser means.

Stripped of rhetoric, the prejudice Plaintiffs identify is that their attorneys spent time investigating the documents and retained an expert, and that counsel-withdrawal events delayed the case. (Doc. 145 at 19-20.) Investigation costs, if ultimately found to flow from sanctionable conduct by a particular Defendant, are fully compensable through a tailored fee award—a quintessential lesser sanction. They are not a basis for terminating the case or deeming the merits established. And much of the claimed delay is of Plaintiffs' own making: Plaintiffs discovered the discrepancies around August 2025, waited until late September to raise them, and did not file the operative Motion until April 13, 2026—after the Court had already ruled on the motion to dismiss in their favor. (Doc. 145 at 8, 11-13.) Plaintiffs' suggestion that they now "must corroborate the validity of every piece of evidence" (Doc. 145 at 20) is precisely the sort of generalized distrust that an evidentiary hearing, targeted re-production, or verification protocol can address—not a reason to skip adjudication of 22 plaintiffs' claims on the merits.

### IV. Lesser Sanctions—If Any Sanction Is Warranted at All—Are Available and Adequate, and No Sanction May Be Imposed Without an Evidentiary Hearing.

The third *Telectron* element asks whether lesser sanctions would serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. Plaintiffs' answer—that nothing short of default will do—simply assumes the bad-faith finding they have not made. The graduated arsenal available to this Court includes exclusion of the challenged documents (already accomplished in substance by Defendants' stipulation), targeted fee-shifting, supervised or verified re-production, and adverse-inference instructions, among others. *See Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009). Plaintiffs' cited cases imposed terminating sanctions only after courts found, on developed records, that the *party itself* fabricated evidence and that the fabrication infected the merits. *See, e.g., Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1177-82; *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7-11. No such record exists here, and the merits—far from being infected—have already been adjudicated in Plaintiffs' favor at the pleading stage on the authentic documents. (Doc. 135.)

Moreover, the factual disputes at the heart of this Motion—who modified the documents, when, with what knowledge, and at whose direction—are quintessentially matters for live testimony and credibility determinations, not dueling paper submissions. Plaintiffs themselves acknowledge that discovery is ongoing and incomplete, and that they have not deposed any Defendant on these issues. (Doc. 145 at 18 n.10.) Due process forbids branding three litigants as evidence-fabricators, and extinguishing their defenses, on an untested expert declaration that attributes the conduct to no one but "david." If the Court is inclined to consider any inherent-power sanction, Defendants respectfully request an evidentiary hearing at which the provenance of the challenged documents, and each Defendant's knowledge (or lack thereof), can be examined and resolved on competent evidence.

### V. Plaintiffs' Alternative Relief Is Overbroad and Unwarranted.

#### A. The requested "adverse inference" is a merits windfall, not a remedy.

Plaintiffs ask the Court to "deem admitted and established" their allegations regarding both "the meaning and interpretation of the Independent Contractor Agreements *and Defendants' breach* of the agreements." (Doc. 145 at 23 (emphasis added).) The first half is already done: the Court construed the agreements and adopted Plaintiffs' interpretation. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) An "inference" decreeing what the Court has already held is no remedy at all. The second half—deeming *breach* established—would relieve Plaintiffs of proving the core disputed elements of their claims (performance, breach, causation, and damages as to 22 separate plaintiffs) based on documents that were never used against them. That is not an inference designed to restore an evidentiary balance; it is a default judgment wearing a different label.

Nor does spoliation doctrine support it. Spoliation "is the intentional destruction, mutilation, alteration, or concealment of evidence." *Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006). Adverse-inference instructions exist to compensate a party for evidence that has been *lost to it*. Here, nothing was destroyed or rendered unavailable: the authentic agreements exist, are in Plaintiffs' possession, are filed on this Court's docket (Docs. 16-3 through 16-23), and have been stipulated authentic by both sides. An adverse inference presupposes an evidentiary gap; Plaintiffs have none.

#### B. A special master is unnecessary.

Plaintiffs' request for a special master "at Defendants' sole expense" rests entirely on the premise that Defendants have been adjudicated untrustworthy. *See Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008) (master appointed with costs allocated to "party that committed discovery abuses"); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999). No such adjudication exists. Defendants are represented by new counsel, have offered a curative stipulation, and remain subject to the Court's ordinary supervisory tools—motions to compel, Rule 37, and tailored production protocols—which are more than sufficient to police the remainder of discovery.

#### C. Fees and costs are premature.

A fee award under the inherent power likewise requires a predicate finding of bad faith, which has not been and cannot be made on this record. *See Branch-Haines*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (fee request "unavailing" where movant "failed to prove fraud on the court or bad faith").

### VI. To the Extent Plaintiffs Seek Default-Judgment-Level Sanctions, Their Showing Also Fails Under the Fraud-on-the-Court Standard, Which Demands the Same Egregiousness.

Because the sanction Plaintiffs principally seek is case-terminating, the body of law governing fraud on the court remains instructive: a default-level sanction under the inherent power demands the same species of egregious, court-directed misconduct. The Eleventh Circuit defines fraud on the court as "embracing only that species of fraud which does or attempts to defile the court itself," *S.E.C. v. ESM Group, Inc.*, 835 F.2d 270, 273 (11th Cir. 1988) (quoting *Traveler's Indemnity Co. v. Gore*, 761 F.2d 1549, 1551 (11th Cir. 1985)), and only "egregious misconduct—such as an 'unconscionable plan or scheme' to influence the court's decision" qualifies. *DeBose v. USF Bd. of Trustees*, 844 F. App'x 99, 102 (11th Cir. 2021).

Deceit that occurs purely between the parties in discovery—even alleged fabrication—does not meet that bar. *See Gupta v. Walt Disney World Co.*, 482 F. App'x 458, 459 (11th Cir. 2012) ("Fraud between parties does not constitute fraud on the court[.]"); *Patterson v. Lew*, 265 F. App'x 767, 769 (11th Cir. 2008); *Bassett v. Wal-Mart Stores E., LP*, 2019 WL 4691824, at *2 (S.D. Fla. July 10, 2019); *Gulf Atl.*, 796 F. Supp. 3d at 1018-19 ("[t]hose documents were exchanged between the parties as part of the discovery process. That alone is sufficient to doom [the] fraud-on-the-court claim"); *Cerrato v. Nutribullet, LLC*, 2017 WL 1519815, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Apr. 27, 2017) ("at worst, Plaintiffs have committed perjury and fabricated evidence. Such actions constitute harm between the parties, not harm to the Court."); *Adams v. Cousins Transp. Sys., LLC*, 2020 WL 13348805, at *1 (S.D. Fla. July 6, 2020).

The ordinary remedy for such alleged evils is the adversarial process itself: "[p]erjury and fabricated evidence are evils that can and should be exposed at trial, and the legal system encourages and expects litigants to root them out as early as possible." *Idearc Media*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (quoting *Great Coastal Express v. Int'l Broth. of Teamsters*, 675 F.2d 1349, 1357 (4th Cir. 1982)); *Council v. Am. Fed'n of Gov't Employees (AFGE) Union*, 559 F. App'x 870, 873 (11th Cir. 2014). Here, the challenged documents were never presented to the Court, never relied upon, and have been disavowed by stipulation. Whatever happened to these 21 documents before production, it was not directed at this Court, did not influence—and now cannot influence—any judicial decision, and therefore cannot support a case-terminating sanction under any framework.

## CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs seek the most severe sanction known to civil practice against three separate Defendants without identifying who altered the challenged documents, without evidence that any named Defendant knew of or directed any alteration, without any cognizable prejudice—the authentic contracts having been in Plaintiffs' hands, on this Court's docket, and construed in Plaintiffs' favor all along—and without engaging the array of lesser measures, including Defendants' offered stipulation, that fully address any legitimate concern. The Motion should be denied. In the alternative, Defendants respectfully request that the Court set an evidentiary hearing before considering any sanction under its inherent power, and deny the requested default judgment, adverse inference, and special-master relief as disproportionate on this record.
