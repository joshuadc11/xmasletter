### INTRODUCTION

This is not a "fraud on the court" motion, and Plaintiffs do not invoke that doctrine. To foreclose any mislabeling at the outset: Plaintiffs do not move under Rule 60(d) or ask this Court to find the narrow "species of fraud which does or attempts to defile the court itself." *S.E.C. v. ESM Group, Inc.*, 835 F.2d 270, 273 (11th Cir. 1988). Plaintiffs move under this Court's *inherent power* to sanction a party's bad-faith abuse of the judicial process, *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1991), under the spoliation doctrine, which expressly reaches the "intentional destruction, mutilation, *alteration*, or concealment of evidence," *Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, No. 6:05-cv-1083-Orl-22DAB, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006) (emphasis added), and under Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1). None of those sources of authority requires that fabricated evidence first be filed with, or relied upon before, the Court. To the contrary, "[i]t would be strange to require a district court to wait until false evidence is introduced at trial before sanctions are appropriate, all the while wasting the time and resources of the court and the opposing party." *Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015).

The misconduct at issue is grave and, on this record, unrebutted. A central issue in this case is the drivers' compensation under the Independent Contractor & Non-Compete Agreements ("Independent Contractor Agreements") with FR8, which provide that FR8 shall pay the drivers "88% of linehaul rate."[^2] Plaintiffs contend that FR8 must pay each driver 88% of the revenue FR8 receives from the third-party broker for the load. Defendants have argued each truckload is "a separate subagreement [sic] or contract" between Plaintiffs and FR8. (Doc. 52 at 4.)

[^2]: There are slight, non-material variations in the wording of the authentic contracts, with some saying "88% of linehaul rate," "88% linehaul," "88% of line haul," or "88% of load." None of the authentic agreements, however, includes the "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" language with respect to the terms of Plaintiffs' compensation.

While Defendants' motion to dismiss — staking out that very contract-interpretation position — was pending before this Court, someone digitally altered the signed Independent Contractor Agreements to delete the "88% of linehaul rate" compensation term and replace it with "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" — language that tracks Defendants' litigation position. Forensic metadata shows the alteration was performed in Adobe Acrobat and saved on **February 17, 2025**. (Ex. E ¶ 15.) **Nine days later**, on February 26, 2025, Kajdic and Begovic served discovery responses — certified by counsel under Rule 26(g) — identifying *only* those altered agreements (Bates D002379-D005171) as the documents supporting Defendants' contention that Plaintiffs are not entitled to 88% of the gross revenue per load. (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) FR8 did the same on April 16, 2025. (Ex. C at 8 ¶ 29.) The altered agreements bear Plaintiffs' genuine signature pages, harvested from the authentic contracts. Plaintiffs never signed them. (Composite Ex. A ¶¶ 3-5.)

Defendants have never denied the alteration, never explained it, never identified the Adobe user "david" who performed it, never amended their discovery responses, and never produced corrected documents. What the record does show is that, after Plaintiffs raised the issue on September 24, 2025, two successive sets of defense counsel withdrew — the first citing "ethical concerns" and seeking a protective order because counsel could not "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations." (Doc. 107 at ¶ 7; Doc. 117 at 4.)

Pursuant to this Court's inherent power to safeguard the administration of justice and to preserve the integrity of the discovery process, Plaintiffs seek sanctions in the form of a default judgment against Defendants. In the alternative, Plaintiffs request that this Court: (1) award Plaintiffs an adverse inference against Defendants deeming admitted or established Plaintiffs' allegations with respect to the interpretation of the Independent Contractor Agreements and Defendants' breach of the agreements; (2) appoint a special master to oversee and/or audit the discovery fully at Defendants' expense; (3) award Plaintiffs their attorneys' fees and costs in connection with uncovering the fabricated evidence and pursuing sanctions; and (4) award any further relief the Court deems necessary or appropriate.

### RELEVANT BACKGROUND

#### A. Procedural Background

On May 23, 2024, Plaintiffs commenced this action in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, alleging claims for violation of the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act ("RICO"), 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c), against Kajdic and Begovic, and breach of contract against FR8. (Doc. 1 at ¶¶ 83-108.) On July 16, 2024, Defendants moved to dismiss and transfer venue. (Docs. 16, 17.) In support, Defendants submitted the sworn declaration of Defendant Begovic, as Chief Financial Officer of FR8, attesting that "true and accurate copies of the Independent Contractor Agreements, offered to and executed by each of the named Plaintiffs" were attached as exhibits to her Declaration and consisted of FR8's business records. (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18, 19.) Plaintiffs stipulate that these are the true and correct copies of the contracts they signed with FR8. (Composite Ex. A ¶ 5.)

The authentic agreements provide that Plaintiffs are to be paid "88% of linehaul rate" (Docs. 16-3, 16-5–16-9, 16-12–16-23), "88% linehaul" (Doc. 16-4), "88% of line haul" (Doc. 16-11), or "88% of load" (Doc. 16-10). The compensation terms in several of the Independent Contractor Agreements are handwritten. (See Docs. 16-4 at 4; 16-7 at 4; 16-10 at 4; 16-11 at 4; 16-13 at 4; 16-16 at 4; 16-21 at 4; 16-23 at 4.)

In September 2024, the parties stipulated to transfer this case to this Court. (Docs. 23, 26, 27, 28.) Also in September 2024, Plaintiffs filed their First Amended Class Action Complaint ("FAC"), realleging their RICO and breach of contract claims and adding claims under the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, Fla. Stat. § 501.201, et seq., and the Truth in Leasing Act, 49 U.S.C. § 14704(a)(2). (Doc. 22 at ¶¶ 87-144.)

On October 11, 2024, Defendants filed a Motion to Dismiss Plaintiffs' FAC. (Doc. 52.) Defendants argued that Plaintiffs' claims "rest[] on a fundamentally flawed misreading and misrepresentation" of the Independent Contractor Agreements (*id.* at 2), that each truckload was a "separate subagreement (sic) or contract between Plaintiffs and FR8" (*id.* at 4), and that Plaintiffs "repeatedly misstate[d] and attempt[ed] to unilaterally redefine the 'line-haul rate' that would be agreed upon per truckload basis" (*id.* at 3-4). **The meaning of the compensation term was therefore the crystallized, contested, dispositive question pending before this Court from October 11, 2024 forward.**

On February 19, 2026, the Court ruled on Defendants' Motion to Dismiss. (Doc. 135.) The Court determined the Independent Contractor Agreements are unambiguous, concluded that Defendants' proposed interpretation would lead to an "absurd result," and adopted Plaintiffs' "rational interpretation" of the plain contractual language. (*Id.* at 7, 10.) The Court dismissed Counts I and II against Begovic and allowed all counts against Kajdic and FR8 to proceed. (*Id.* at 15.)

#### C. The Forensic Evidence of Deliberate Digital Alteration Is Unrebutted

In around August 2025, Plaintiffs discovered that the Independent Contractor Agreements produced within Bates range D002379-D005171 contained different compensation terms than the agreements Plaintiffs possess and that Defendants themselves had filed with Begovic's sworn declaration (the "Altered Agreements," Composite Ex. D, Sub-Exs. 1-21). The Altered Agreements contain different compensation terms for all named Plaintiffs except two.[^4] Instead of "88% of linehaul," "88% of linehaul rate," or "88% of load," the Altered Agreements state that Plaintiffs' compensation is "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence[.]" (See, e.g., Ex. D-1 at 3.)

[^4]: An altered agreement for Alvir Bajrektarevic was not produced in discovery. Besir Catic was not a named Plaintiff at the time the Altered Agreements were produced, and Defendants did not produce any agreement for him.

The indicia of deliberate manipulation are extensive and mutually corroborating:

1. **Substituted pages.** In Plaintiff Adis Dogic's authentic agreement, the compensation term is handwritten: "88% of linehaul." (Doc. 16-4 at 3.) In the Altered Agreement produced for him (Ex. D-2 at 3, Bates D004094), the handwritten term has been replaced with a different, *typed* compensation term.

2. **The identical smudge.** A smudge appears in the bottom right corner of the compensation page of the Altered Agreement for Dogic that does not appear in his authentic agreement — and the *same smudge appears in the same spot on the compensation page in virtually every Altered Agreement for each named Plaintiff*. (Ex. D-1 at 3; D-2 at 3; D-4 through D-6 at 3; D-8 through D-21 at 3.) The text of the compensation term is also virtually identical across the Altered Agreements. Taken together, this evidence supports the finding that the same fabricated replacement page was inserted into almost every agreement.

3. **Forensic metadata.** Forensic Director Rich Hoffman confirms the Altered Agreements were modified prior to production. (Declaration of Rich Hoffman, Ex. E.) Page 3 of the Altered Dogic Agreement is in text-based (selectable, editable) format while the other pages are image-based — establishing that a hard copy was scanned and page 3 was subsequently altered. (*Id.* ¶ 15.) The metadata shows the Dogic Agreement was modified — with pages added — using Adobe Acrobat and saved as a new document on **February 17, 2025**. (*Id.*) The Irfan Bajrektarevic Agreement was altered by a username "**david**" using Adobe Acrobat prior to production; a scanned image of a hair on the original is *hidden* in the altered version because the Adobe modification changed its opacity, and blurring and gray shading surround the text and paragraph lines of Section 3 — indicia of digital manipulation. (*Id.* ¶ 12.) A hair on the originally scanned Dalibor Dragas contract was likewise covered up by digital manipulation, and that agreement is a hybrid of scanned images and digitally created or modified pages. (*Id.* ¶ 5.)

4. **The timing.** The forensic evidence places the alteration on February 17, 2025 — **after** Defendants' October 11, 2024 motion to dismiss put the meaning of the compensation term squarely before this Court, **while that motion was pending**, and **nine days before** Kajdic and Begovic served the certified discovery responses identifying the altered documents as their merits evidence.

Defendants have offered no declaration, no forensic rebuttal, and no explanation. Their position, as expressed in conferral, is to argue around the misconduct rather than to deny it.

### ARGUMENT

#### I. THIS MOTION ARISES UNDER THE COURT'S INHERENT POWER, THE SPOLIATION DOCTRINE, AND RULES 26 AND 37 — NOT THE "FRAUD ON THE COURT" DOCTRINE — AND NONE OF THOSE AUTHORITIES REQUIRES THAT FABRICATED EVIDENCE BE FILED WITH THE COURT.

##### A. The governing standard is bad-faith abuse of the judicial process, which expressly reaches the doctoring of evidence in discovery.

Federal district courts possess the inherent power "to protect the orderly administration of justice and to preserve the dignity of the tribunal." *Kleiner v. First Nat'l Bank of Atlanta*, 751 F.2d 1193, 1209 (11th Cir. 1985) (citing *Roadway Express v. Piper*, 447 U.S. 752, 764-65 (1980)). "This power includes 'the ability to fashion an appropriate sanction for conduct which abuses the judicial process[.]'" *VIP Auto Glass, Inc. v. GEICO Gen. Ins. Co.*, No. 16-CV-2012, 2018 WL 3649638, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Jan. 3, 2018) (quoting *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1991)). The inherent powers doctrine is "most often invoked where a party commits perjury or **destroys or doctors evidence**." *Access Innovators, LLC v. Usha Martin Ltd.*, No. 09-CV-2893, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1 (N.D. Ga. Apr. 28, 2010) (quoting *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1269 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (emphasis added).

Critically, the bad-faith predicate for inherent-power sanctions is disjunctive — it is *not* limited to fraud practiced on the court: "Bad faith exists when the court finds that a fraud has been practiced upon it, **or** that the very temple of justice has been defiled, **or** where a party or attorney knowingly or recklessly raises a frivolous argument, **delays or disrupts the litigation**, or hampers the enforcement of a court order." *Qantum Commc'ns Corp.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1268-69 (cleaned up; emphasis added). The same is true of spoliation, which is "the 'intentional destruction, mutilation, **alteration**, or concealment of evidence.'" *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (quoting Black's Law Dictionary 1437 (8th ed. 2004)) (emphasis added); *see Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009) (spoliation sanctions available under inherent powers, including default judgment, adverse inferences, and fees). And Rule 37(c)(1) independently authorizes sanctions for a party's failure to supplement or amend responses to requests for production. *See Dragon Jade Int'l, Ltd. v. Ultroid, LLC*, No. 17-CV-2422, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 12, 2019); Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(e), 37(c)(1). Counsel's signature on each discovery response further carried a Rule 26(g) certification that the response was complete and correct when made.

The Eleventh Circuit has squarely rejected the premise that false evidence must be put before the court before it is sanctionable: "false evidence is sufficient cause for sanctions," and "[i]t would be strange to require a district court to wait until false evidence is introduced at trial before sanctions are appropriate, all the while wasting the time and resources of the court and the opposing party." *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679. Consistent with that principle, courts impose case-ending sanctions for fabricated documents produced *in discovery* — without any requirement that they were filed or relied upon in court papers. *See Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962, 971 (E.D.N.C. 2017), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018) (fabricated business records produced in discovery); *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009) (documents fabricated and "produced during discovery"); *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, No. 18-CV-01479, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, *11 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020) (default judgment where "Defendants' document production contains numerous demonstrable forgeries that have never been corrected, explained, or even acknowledged"; that allegation "alone . . . is more than enough to require case-ending sanctions"); *Carroll-Harris v. Wilkie*, No. 17-CV-11711, 2019 WL 2205851, at *6-8 (E.D. Mich. May 22, 2019) (altered documents produced in discovery). "[C]ourts faced with the fabrication of evidence by a party frequently impose the sanction of dismissal or default." *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, No. 18-61912-CIV, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019) (collecting cases).

##### B. Defendants' anticipated "fraud on the court" reframing answers a motion Plaintiffs did not bring.

Plaintiffs anticipate, based on the parties' conferrals, that Defendants will respond by invoking the demanding "fraud on the court" standard — *ESM Group*, 835 F.2d at 273; *Gupta v. Walt Disney World Co.*, 482 F. App'x 458, 459 (11th Cir. 2012); *Patterson v. Lew*, 265 F. App'x 767, 769 (11th Cir. 2008) — and arguing that "fraud between the parties" in discovery cannot support relief because the Altered Agreements were never filed with the Court. That argument fails for three independent reasons.

*First*, it attacks a strawman. The "fraud on the court" doctrine defines the narrow species of fraud that justifies setting aside *judgments* and dismissing actions on that distinct ground. Plaintiffs invoke a different and well-established body of law: the Court's inherent power under *Chambers* to sanction bad-faith abuse of the judicial process, the spoliation doctrine, and Rules 26 and 37. As shown above, that authority is "most often invoked" precisely where a party "doctors evidence," *Access Innovators*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1, and it carries no filed-with-the-court prerequisite, *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679. Cases denying dismissal *for fraud on the court* — e.g., *Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018-19 (N.D. Fla. 2025); *Cerrato v. Nutribullet, LLC*, 2017 WL 1519815, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Apr. 27, 2017); *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009); *Bassett v. Wal-Mart Stores E., LP*, 2019 WL 4691824, at *2 (S.D. Fla. July 10, 2019); *Adams v. Cousins Transp. Sys., LLC*, 2020 WL 13348805, at *1 (S.D. Fla. July 6, 2020) — say nothing about whether the same conduct supports sanctions under the Court's inherent power, which is the question this Motion presents.

*Second*, those cases are factually inapposite in any event:

- ***Gulf Atlantic*** concerned allegedly fabricated *damages calculations and a spreadsheet* exchanged in discovery, analyzed under a "fraud-on-the-court claim." 796 F. Supp. 3d at 1018-19. Here, Defendants manufactured forged *signed contracts* — the foundational instruments of the entire case — by grafting Plaintiffs' genuine signature pages onto fabricated compensation terms, and then certified in discovery responses that those forgeries were "the Independent Contractor Agreements" and the *only* documents supporting their merits position.

- ***Cerrato*** and ***Idearc*** turned on the proposition that perjury and fabricated evidence "can and should be exposed at trial." *Idearc*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5; *Cerrato*, 2017 WL 1519815, at *3. The Eleventh Circuit has explained why that logic does not constrain inherent-power sanctions: requiring courts to wait for trial "wast[es] the time and resources of the court and the opposing party." *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679. Moreover, in *Idearc* the offending exhibits were "withdrawn well before a finding of fact." 2009 WL 928556, at *5. Defendants here have withdrawn nothing: their certified discovery responses identifying the Altered Agreements as their merits evidence remain operative and unamended to this day.

- ***Bassett*** and ***Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026)**, address dismissal for fraud on the court and caution against terminating sanctions for "inconsistencies, poor recollection, non-disclosure, mistaken beliefs, ambiguity, bad conduct, or even outright lies." *Bijou*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1. This record presents none of those quotidian failings. It presents forensically documented digital forgery of the operative contracts — page substitution, an identical copied smudge across twenty-one agreements, hidden artifacts, Adobe Acrobat metadata, and a named user — timed nine days before service of the discovery responses that vouched for the forgeries.

- ***Adams*** involved the production of *unrelated medical bills* — at most an over-inclusive production. 2020 WL 13348805, at *1. Producing irrelevant documents is not remotely comparable to manufacturing false versions of the dispositive contracts that mimic one's litigation position.

*Third*, Defendants' own authorities confirm that where, as in *Roche Diagnostics*, *Garcia*, *Franklin Livestock*, and *Carroll-Harris*, the misconduct is the production of forged or doctored documents in discovery, inherent-power sanctions — including default — follow. Defendants cannot escape that line of authority by relabeling Plaintiffs' motion.

##### C. In any event, Defendants' misconduct was aimed at this Court's truth-finding function, not merely at Plaintiffs.

Even if the "directed at the court" gloss had any purchase here, the record satisfies it. The compensation term's meaning was the contested question *pending before this Court* on Defendants' motion to dismiss when the agreements were altered on February 17, 2025. The forged term — "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" — was drafted to mirror Defendants' argument to this Court that each load is a separately negotiated "subagreement." (Doc. 52 at 3-4.) The forgeries were then placed into the judicial process through discovery responses bearing counsel's Rule 26(g) certifications, identifying them as "the Independent Contractor Agreements" and as the evidentiary support for Defendants' merits contention. Discovery is not a private exchange between parties; it is conducted under this Court's rules and authority, and its corruption "prejudices the court and its administration of the judicial process to the extent it introduces uncertainty, requires additional investigation into the fabrication, and undermines the validity of the litigation process." *Martin v. SGT, Inc.*, No. 19-cv-00289, 2022 WL 17975993, at *16 (D. Utah Dec. 28, 2022). Had Plaintiffs not detected the forgeries through painstaking review and forensic analysis, the Altered Agreements — Defendants' self-identified merits evidence — were on a path to summary judgment and trial. Defendants should not be heard to claim credit for the fortuity that they were caught first.

Defendants' related assertions — that they "never relied" on the Altered Agreements and "never made any representation" about them — are refuted by their own discovery responses. Identifying documents, under a Rule 26(g) certification, as "the Independent Contractor Agreements" and as the documents supporting one's central merits contention *is* both reliance and a representation of authenticity. So is producing forged contracts bearing Plaintiffs' genuine signatures within what were presented as Plaintiffs' personnel files. And the suggestion that Plaintiffs should simply have placed "one phone call" inverts the record: Plaintiffs did confer, in writing on September 24, 2025 and by call on October 3, 2025 — and the response was not an innocent explanation but the withdrawal of two successive sets of defense counsel, the first citing "ethical concerns." (Docs. 103, 104, 106, 107 ¶ 7, 117 at 4.)

#### II. THE COURT SHOULD ENTER A DEFAULT JUDGMENT AGAINST DEFENDANTS AS A SANCTION FOR THE FABRICATION OF EVIDENCE.

Before entering a case-terminating sanction, a court must find that: (1) Defendants acted willfully or in bad faith; (2) Plaintiffs were prejudiced by Defendants' conduct; and (3) lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *See Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987). All three elements are satisfied — by evidence that is not merely clear and convincing, but unrebutted.

##### A. Defendants acted willfully and in bad faith.

Fabricating and submitting knowingly false evidence constitutes willful and bad-faith conduct. *See Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, No. CV 12-6972, 2015 WL 12732433, at *23 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015); *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1177-82 (dismissal affirmed where plaintiff fabricated documents produced during discovery); *Franklin Livestock*, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 962, *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018) (same); *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *11 (default judgment against defendants who produced forged invoices in discovery).

Willfulness is established here by the convergence of means, motive, and timing:

- **Means**: The alterations required deliberate, skilled effort — scanning authentic contracts, substituting typed compensation pages for handwritten ones, replicating the same fabricated page (with its telltale identical smudge) across twenty-one agreements, and digital manipulation in Adobe Acrobat by user "david" that incidentally hid scanned hairs and left blurring artifacts. (Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15.) No innocent records-management process produces that result.
- **Motive**: The forged term — "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" — precisely tracks Defendants' litigation position that each load was a separately negotiated "subagreement." (Doc. 52 at 3-4.) If the authentic Independent Contractor Agreements supported Defendants' position, there would have been no reason to alter them.
- **Timing**: The Dogic agreement was altered and saved on February 17, 2025 (Ex. E ¶ 15) — months after Defendants' motion to dismiss crystallized the interpretation dispute, while that motion was pending, and nine days before Kajdic and Begovic served certified responses identifying the altered set as their merits evidence (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18).

The misconduct is "closely intertwined with the merits of the case," which "militates heavily in favor of the severe sanction of default." *Qantum*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1269; *see Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 ("The severity of [a case-ending] sanction is warranted in large part because the doctored evidence cuts to the very essence of [the] substantive claims."). Defendants' contrary suggestion — that the Altered Agreements are not "intertwined with the merits" because the authentic contracts govern — is irreconcilable with their own discovery responses, which identified the Altered Agreements, and *only* the Altered Agreements, as the documents supporting their merits contention. A party cannot designate forged documents as its merits evidence and then disclaim their relevance once the forgery is exposed.[^5]

[^5]: Even if only one Defendant altered the documents, sanctions are justified as to all Defendants: each identified the Altered Agreements in its or her own unamended discovery responses as evidence supporting the common merits position.

Nor does Defendants' proposed stipulation alter the analysis. In *Franklin Livestock*, dismissal was affirmed even though the offending party *self-disclosed* the doctored evidence, disavowed its authenticity, and produced the authentic records — because "the creation of false records that are passed off as originals and which materially contradict the original records later produced cannot be glossed over as a 'mistake.'" 251 F. Supp. 3d at 968, 970. Defendants' stipulation is far weaker than the corrective conduct found insufficient there: it comes months after Plaintiffs exposed the fabrication on September 24, 2025; it does not arise from self-disclosure; it does not acknowledge, explain, or even mention the alteration; it identifies no responsible person; and it leaves the Rule 26(g)-certified discovery responses identifying the forgeries untouched. *See Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, *9 (forgeries "never . . . corrected, explained, or even acknowledged"); *cf. Carroll-Harris*, 2019 WL 2205851, at *6 (rejecting explanation where fabricated documents replicated authentic headers but "completely replace[d] portions . . . without explanation or identification").[^6]

[^6]: Defendants may argue that Plaintiffs could have deposed Defendants about the Altered Agreements. But Plaintiffs are entitled to one seven-hour deposition of each party, and discovery is ongoing; Defendants have yet to produce their relevant text messages. Plaintiffs, confronted with altered documents produced in discovery, should not be required to prematurely expend their only opportunity to depose key witnesses. If Defendants had exculpatory evidence, it should have been produced during the parties' good-faith conferrals on this Motion.

##### B. Plaintiffs — and this Court — are prejudiced by Defendants' fabrication of evidence.

Prejudice from falsified evidence includes having to "consum[e] substantial resources to respond to and 'undo' the victimizer's lies and distortions" and to "vigorously attempt to pry information from the deceitful party." *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 680 (cleaned up). Further, "falsified evidence substantially prejudices an opposing party by casting doubt on the veracity of all of the culpable party's submissions throughout litigation," forcing the victim "either to attempt independent corroboration of each submission, at substantial expense of time and money, or to accept the real possibility that those discovery documents submitted by the opposing party are inaccurate." *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1180.

Each form of prejudice is concretely present:

1. **Investigative burden.** Plaintiffs' attorneys and staff spent significant time investigating the nature and origin of the Altered Agreements and retained and paid a forensic expert to confirm the manipulation.
2. **Delay.** The fabrication and its aftermath — including the successive withdrawals of defense counsel (Docs. 103, 104, 106, 116, 125, 126), the protective-order motion suspending production (Doc. 107 ¶ 7), the vacatur of case deadlines (Doc. 116), and the stay through January 2026 — derailed the case schedule and Plaintiffs' prosecution of their claims.
3. **Corruption of the entire production.** Confidence in the discovery as a whole is destroyed. This is acute here because, given the volume of documents, Plaintiffs had agreed to accept Excel spreadsheets *prepared by Defendants* purporting to summarize authentic underlying records — including damages evidence. Plaintiffs can no longer credit anything Defendants produce or prepare. *See White Cap., L.P. v. Heyden Enters., LLC*, No. 23-14248, 2025 WL 414721, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Feb. 6, 2025) ("The parties — and the Court — cannot trust that [Defendants] have produced, or will ever produce, all discoverable evidence.") (quoting *Bernal v. All. Am. Inv. Realty, Inc.*, 479 F. Supp. 2d 1291, 1339 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (cleaned up).
4. **Institutional prejudice.** The fabrication "prejudices the court and its administration of the judicial process." *Martin*, 2022 WL 17975993, at *16. Permitting a suit to proceed is "futile" when neither the Court nor Plaintiffs can have confidence that anything Defendants produce is accurate and authentic. *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *10.

The proposed stipulation cures none of this. The investigative costs are sunk; the delays have occurred; and a stipulation about which contracts govern says nothing about whether the *rest* of Defendants' 49,000-document production — and their forthcoming damages summaries — can be trusted. Defendants' credibility cannot be rehabilitated by stipulating to a fact they were forced to concede. *See Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *10 ("[T]he court cannot permit any party to 'defile' the 'very temple of justice' by creating and projecting a false reality.") (quoting *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 46).

##### C. Lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence.

Case-terminating sanctions are particularly appropriate where a party submits fabricated evidence. *See Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7; *VIP Auto Glass*, 2018 WL 3649638, at *4-5 ("Numerous courts have dismissed claims where a party has fabricated evidence or committed perjury."); *Access Innovators*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *3 ("When a party fabricates a document or provides false evidence relating to a key issue in a case, courts have made clear that the appropriate sanction is the ultimate sanction of dismissal.").

Mere exclusion of the Altered Agreements would be an "insufficient sanction," because "litigants could infer that they have everything to gain, and nothing to lose by manufacturing evidence." *Carroll-Harris*, 2019 WL 2205851, at *8 (internal quotations omitted). That logic applies with special force to Defendants' proposed stipulation, which is functionally *less* than exclusion: it would permit a party caught forging the operative contracts to reset to the status quo ante at no cost beyond conceding the forgery failed. Fees and costs alone likewise would not "sufficiently address [Plaintiffs'] concern about the veracity of other documents disclosed" by Defendants. *Id.* at *7. If the consequence of manufacturing the dispositive contract evidence — and certifying it as one's merits proof — is merely a stipulation restoring the truth once caught, the deterrence function of the Court's inherent power is a dead letter. A default judgment is the appropriate sanction.

#### III. ALTERNATIVELY, THE COURT SHOULD GRANT AN ADVERSE INFERENCE, APPOINT A SPECIAL MASTER, AND AWARD FEES AND COSTS.

##### A. Adverse inference.

This Court may impose spoliation sanctions pursuant to its inherent powers, including "default judgment, adverse inference or rebuttable presumption instructions to the jury, striking pleadings, and an award of fees and costs." *Swofford*, 671 F. Supp. 2d at 1280. Adverse inferences include instructing a jury "that certain facts are deemed admitted and must be accepted as true[.]" *Penick v. Harbor Freight Tools, USA, Inc.*, 481 F. Supp. 3d 1286, 1295 (S.D. Fla. 2020). Should the Court decline to enter default judgment, it should deem admitted and established the facts alleged in the FAC (or the SAC, should the Court permit amendment, see Doc. 140) regarding the meaning and interpretation of the Independent Contractor Agreements and Defendants' breach. The inference is fair on this record: if the authentic agreements supported Defendants' position, there would have been no reason to alter them.

##### B. Special master.

This Court has authority to appoint a special master pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 53 and its inherent powers, with costs allocated to the party that committed discovery abuses. *See Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, No. 06-20624-CIV, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008). Given that Defendants fed forged documents into their own counsel's production stream (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5), and that substantial discovery — including Defendants' text messages and damages materials — remains outstanding, a special master should be appointed at Defendants' sole expense to oversee and audit discovery going forward. "[D]efendants simply cannot be trusted to do this job themselves." *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999).

##### C. Fees and costs.

This Court has authority to assess fees and costs as a result of spoliation, *Swofford*, 671 F. Supp. 2d at 1280, for failure to amend or supplement discovery responses, Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(c)(1); *Dragon Jade*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4, and under its inherent power, *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45. Plaintiffs should recover the fees and costs incurred in uncovering the fabrication — including the forensic expert — and in pursuing sanctions. Notably, fee-shifting is appropriate here even under the authorities Plaintiffs anticipate Defendants will cite: unlike in *Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021), where fees were denied because neither fraud nor bad faith was proven, the bad faith here is established by unrebutted forensic evidence.
