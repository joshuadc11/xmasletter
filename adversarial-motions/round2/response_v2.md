## INTRODUCTION

Defendants do not minimize what Plaintiffs' forensic examiner has found. The record before the Court contains an expert declaration stating that 21 Independent Contractor Agreements within Bates range D002379–D005171 were digitally modified before production — with a specific tool (Adobe Acrobat), a specific date (a save on February 17, 2025), a username ("david"), and replicated artifacts across the set. That showing describes serious misconduct by *someone*, and it deserves a serious response — not minimization, and not a reflexive demand that the Court look away.

This Response gives that serious answer, in three parts.

**First, the remedy must follow the proof, and the proof has a hole at its center: attribution.** The inherent power authorizes sanctions against a *party* only upon a finding of that party's bad faith — and before terminating a case, the Court must find willfulness, prejudice, and the inadequacy of lesser sanctions, on clear and convincing evidence. *Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987); *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020). Mr. Hoffman's declaration identifies a username that belongs to none of the three Defendants. It does not say which human being altered the documents, at whose direction, or with which Defendant's knowledge. Plaintiffs' own Motion concedes the gap, arguing that "[e]ven if only one Defendant altered the documents," all three should be defaulted anyway. (Doc. 145 at 18 n.11.) Due process does not permit the most severe sanction in civil litigation to be imposed *collectively*, without individualized findings, on an inference stacked upon an anonymous username. *See In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) [VERIFY — due-process prerequisites to inherent-power sanctions].

**Second, Defendants come forward in this Response with a concrete remediation package** — not the bare stipulation offered in conferral, but the following commitments, set out in full in Section III: amended discovery responses within 14 days identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing any reliance on Bates range D002379–D005171; formal withdrawal and clawback of the altered documents, with a stipulation that the fact of alteration may be presented to the factfinder; sworn declarations from each Defendant and from the persons who assembled the production; a neutral third-party forensic examination of the relevant systems at Defendants' expense; payment of Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert costs without admission of wrongdoing; production of the underlying source records behind any Defendant-prepared damages summary; and agreement to be bound by this Court's contract interpretation (Doc. 135) for all purposes, including trial. These measures cure every item of prejudice Plaintiffs identify and answer the third *Telectron* element directly: lesser sanctions are not merely available — Defendants are volunteering them.

**Third, proportionality.** What Plaintiffs ask for is judgment, without trial, against three separate defendants on RICO claims carrying treble damages and attorneys' fees, FDUTPA claims, Truth in Leasing Act claims, and contract claims for 22 plaintiffs in a putative class action (Doc. 22 ¶¶ 87-144) — based on documents that were never filed, never used in any proceeding, and that could not have changed the outcome of the only merits ruling to date, which this Court decided *in Plaintiffs' favor on the authentic agreements*. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) Whatever sanction the facts ultimately support after a hearing, it is not that.

The questions this Motion actually turns on — who altered the documents, when, at whose direction, and with which Defendant's knowledge — cannot be answered on a paper record, by either side. Defendants therefore renew their request for an evidentiary hearing, commit to making each individual Defendant available to testify at it, and propose the provenance discovery described below so that the hearing proceeds on evidence rather than inference.

## ARGUMENT AND MEMORANDUM OF LAW

### I. The Governing Standard: Party-Specific Bad Faith, Proven by Clear and Convincing Evidence, with Due Process Before Any Sanction and Individualized Findings Before a Terminating One.

Plaintiffs invoke this Court's inherent power, the spoliation doctrine, and Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1). (Doc. 145 at 3.) Defendants accept the framework Plaintiffs propose, and accept two propositions Plaintiffs press: the inherent power reaches the doctoring of evidence in discovery, and there is no requirement that false evidence be filed with the Court before it may be sanctioned. *See Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015) (unpublished). The dispute is not whether discovery fabrication can ever be sanctioned. It is what must be *proven*, against *whom*, and by what *process*, before each tier of relief may issue.

Three principles govern. *First*, inherent-power sanctions require a finding of bad faith by the party to be sanctioned. *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1991); *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1268-69 (S.D. Fla. 2007). *Second*, the predicate misconduct must be "demonstrated by clear and convincing evidence." *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 — the standard applied in Plaintiffs' own lead authority, and the one this Court should require before any finding that a litigant fabricated evidence, given the quasi-criminal stigma and case-ending consequences such a finding carries. *Third*, due process requires notice and an opportunity to be heard before inherent-power sanctions issue, *In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) [VERIFY — holding and pin cite], and a case-terminating sanction additionally requires findings that (1) the party acted willfully or in bad faith, (2) the movant was prejudiced, and (3) lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron*, 116 F.R.D. at 131; (Doc. 145 at 14 (invoking the same elements)). Default remains "the most extraordinary of all remedies," "reserved for the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct." *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026).

Where a sanctions record leaves the *who* and the *with-what-knowledge* unresolved, the answer is not to skip the findings; it is to make them — at a hearing, on competent evidence, defendant by defendant. That is the relief Defendants request, and it is the relief the remainder of this Response is built around.

### II. The Record Does Not Permit — Yet — A Clear and Convincing Finding of Bad Faith Against Any Named Defendant, and Plaintiffs' Strongest Facts Are Reasons for a Hearing, Not Substitutes for One.

#### A. The February 17 date and the nine-day sequence: what the timeline shows, and what it cannot show without testimony.

Plaintiffs' most forceful argument is the timeline: a forensic save date of February 17, 2025 (Ex. E ¶ 15), nine days before Mr. Kajdic and Ms. Begovic served discovery responses identifying the soon-to-be-produced Bates range as the support for Defendants' merits contention (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18), all while the motion to dismiss contesting that term was pending. Defendants will not pretend that sequence is unremarkable. It is the central fact of this Motion, and it is entitled to a direct answer.

The direct answer is this: the timeline establishes *temporal proximity*, and temporal proximity is consistent with more than one account. February 17, 2025 falls squarely within the window in which Defendants' records were being assembled, scanned, and processed for the hardcopy production that went out in early March 2025 — a window in which it is undisputed that the documents passed through hands other than the three named Defendants', because then-counsel attests to receiving and producing them in the form provided. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) Who operated the computer on February 17; who the Adobe user "david" is; whether that person acted on instruction, on their own initiative, or for reasons not yet known; and whether any named Defendant knew the production set differed from the agreements Defendants had already filed with this Court — the present record answers none of these questions, for either side. Mr. Hoffman's declaration supplies a date, a tool, a username, and artifacts. It does not supply an actor, a direction, or knowledge.

Defendants candidly acknowledge what they cannot argue: they cannot tell the Court, on this record, *what happened* on February 17, 2025. That is precisely why Defendants commit, in Section III, to sworn declarations from each Defendant and from the persons involved in assembling the production — addressing, under penalty of perjury, their role (if any) in and knowledge of the alteration, and the identity of any person who used the username "david" — and to a neutral forensic examination of the relevant systems at Defendants' own expense. Plaintiffs' refrain that the forensic evidence is "unrebutted" is true only in the sense that no hearing has yet been held and no witness has yet been put to proof. "Unrebutted on a paper record" is not a finding, and it cannot substitute for one when the relief sought is judgment without trial against three separate parties.

#### B. The Rule 26(g)-certified responses are serious, but they do not carry the attribution weight Plaintiffs assign them.

Plaintiffs' second-strongest argument is that each Defendant "relied on" and "represented" the altered documents by identifying Bates range D002379–D005171, in responses certified under Rule 26(g), as the documents supporting Defendants' compensation contention — and that those responses remain unamended. (Doc. 145 at 18 & n.11.) Defendants withdraw the suggestion in their prior draft that these responses can be brushed aside as unsworn boilerplate; that undersold them. The responses identified the altered range as Defendants' merits support, and that matters. Three points, however, define what the responses can and cannot prove.

*First*, as to authorship of the falsity: the Rule 26(g) certification is made by "the signing attorney" after reasonable inquiry, Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(g)(1) — and the signing attorneys here are the same former counsel whom Plaintiffs' own Exhibit F exonerates of any alteration. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) The certified responses therefore tell the Court that counsel, after inquiry, identified the client-furnished agreements as responsive. They do not tell the Court which client furnished which documents, or that any individual Defendant reviewed the production set, compared it against the agreements already on file at Docket 16-3 through 16-23, and knowingly vouched for altered copies. A certification by counsel who indisputably did not alter the documents and is not alleged to have known of the alteration cannot, standing alone, establish each client's knowledge nine days earlier.

*Second*, as to concealment: the theory that Defendants schemed to pass off forged compensation terms must contend with an awkward fact — *Defendants themselves had already filed the authentic agreements with this Court*, under oath, seven months earlier (Doc. 16-2; Docs. 16-3 through 16-23), and continued litigating the motion to dismiss on those authentic agreements through the Court's February 19, 2026 ruling. A party bent on substituting fabricated contract terms into the case would be working against its own sworn public filing, which any comparison — including the one Plaintiffs eventually performed — would expose. This does not disprove misconduct by someone in the document-handling chain; it does cut against the inference that the three named Defendants jointly executed a knowing scheme.

*Third*, as to the failure to amend: that is a real deficiency, and Defendants will not excuse it — they will fix it. The procedural history (two counsel withdrawals, a court-ordered conferral period, a mediation stay) explains much of the interval, but explanation is not cure. The cure is amendment, and Defendants commit to serving amended responses within 14 days of the Court's order, identifying the authentic agreements (Docs. 16-3 through 16-23) and formally withdrawing any reliance on Bates range D002379–D005171. A failure to supplement under Rule 26(e) is remediable through Rule 37(c)'s calibrated toolkit — exclusion, fees, "other appropriate sanctions" — not through default judgment on RICO claims.

#### C. The counsel withdrawals and Defendants' silence to date: the inferences the record does and does not permit.

Plaintiffs feature the withdrawal of two successive sets of defense counsel — the first citing "ethical concerns" and an inability to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations" (Doc. 107 ¶ 7; Doc. 117 at 4) — as confirmation of guilt. Defendants owe the Court a straightforward response rather than silence.

The withdrawals happened; the docket says what it says; and Defendants do not ask the Court to ignore them. But a motion to withdraw is not evidence of any particular client's misconduct, and it identifies no client at all. Withdrawal obligations can be triggered by a range of circumstances — including an attorney's inability to verify facts, conflicts among jointly represented clients, breakdowns in communication, or prudential caution in the face of serious allegations — and the privileged reasons behind these withdrawals are not in the record. What the withdrawals *do* fairly show is that the September 24, 2025 letter was treated with gravity inside the defense camp, not waved off. The same is true of Defendants' silence to date: no Defendant has yet submitted a declaration, and Defendants recognize that continued silence would eventually permit adverse inferences. That is why this Response commits to ending it — with declarations from each Defendant on a court-ordered schedule and live testimony at the evidentiary hearing Defendants themselves request. The choice the Court faces is not between crediting silence and punishing it; it is between resolving attribution on inference now or on sworn evidence shortly.

#### D. Sanctions must be individualized, and the case for any sanction differs sharply by Defendant.

Plaintiffs' footnote answer to the attribution problem — sanction all three because each served responses identifying the altered range (Doc. 145 at 18 n.11) — is collective punishment by another name, and every terminating-sanctions case Plaintiffs cite involved a finding or admission that the *sanctioned party itself* fabricated or knowingly submitted the false evidence. *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009) (court found plaintiff herself fabricated documents); *Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962 (E.D.N.C. 2017) (party admitted producing doctored records), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018); *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 678 (criminal plea admission); *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019); *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7; *Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, 2015 WL 12732433 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015).

The individualized analysis here runs as follows:

- **Ms. Begovic.** The only sworn statement she has made to this Court attached and authenticated the *authentic* agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19) — the agreements Plaintiffs stipulate are correct and that the Court construed in Plaintiffs' favor. She has already been dismissed from Counts I and II. (Doc. 135 at 15.) Nothing in Mr. Hoffman's declaration touches her. A default against her would punish the Defendant who put the true contracts on the docket.
- **Mr. Kajdic.** No record evidence connects him to Adobe Acrobat, to the username "david," or to the February 17 save. The certified responses served over his name were signed by counsel whom Exhibit F exonerates. The case against him is, at present, an inference from corporate office.
- **FR8.** Defendants anticipate Plaintiffs will argue that a corporation acts through agents, and that an alteration occurring within FR8's document-collection process is attributable to FR8 whoever pushed the button. Defendants take that argument seriously rather than dismissing it: *if* the forensic examination and hearing establish that an FR8 agent, acting within the scope of FR8's document handling, altered the agreements, then tailored sanctions against FR8 — calibrated to the findings, including fee-shifting and evidentiary consequences — would be squarely on the table. But agency attribution still requires evidence of who "david" is and in what capacity that person acted; the record does not currently establish that "david" is an FR8 employee rather than, for example, outside personnel involved in scanning or processing. That is a fact question for the hearing, not a presumption.

Sequencing relief by defendant, after findings, is not leniency; it is what *Telectron*'s first element and due process require.

### III. Defendants' Remediation Commitments Establish That Lesser Sanctions Are Available, Adequate, and Already Volunteered.

The third *Telectron* element asks whether lesser sanctions would serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. Plaintiffs argue Defendants' conferral stipulation was "functionally less than exclusion." Defendants respond not with argument but with commitments. Subject to the Court's approval and on the schedule the Court directs, Defendants will:

1. **Amend their discovery responses within 14 days**, identifying the authentic Independent Contractor Agreements (Docs. 16-3 through 16-23) as the operative agreements and the documents responsive to the requests at issue, and formally withdrawing the identification of Bates range D002379–D005171.
2. **Formally withdraw and claw back the altered documents** (Bates range D002379–D005171 to the extent altered), and **stipulate that the fact of the alteration, and the forensic evidence of it, may be presented to the factfinder at trial**, with the parties free to argue the inferences it supports.
3. **Serve sworn declarations** from each of Mr. Kajdic, Ms. Begovic, and a corporate representative of FR8, and from each person involved in assembling, scanning, or transmitting the production set to former counsel, addressing under penalty of perjury: each declarant's role, if any, in and knowledge of the alteration; the identity of every person known to have used the username "david" on relevant systems; and the chain of custody of the personnel files between collection and production.
4. **Submit to a neutral third-party forensic examination**, at Defendants' sole expense, of the computers and systems used to assemble the production — conducted by an examiner jointly selected by the parties or appointed by the Court, with the examiner's report provided simultaneously to both sides and the Court. This is the proportionate version of Plaintiffs' special-master request: it targets the actual open question (provenance and attribution) rather than placing all future discovery under receivership.
5. **Pay Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert costs** incurred in detecting the alteration, without admission of wrongdoing by any Defendant.
6. **Produce the underlying source records for any Defendant-prepared summary**, including the Excel damages spreadsheets Plaintiffs identify (Doc. 145 at 20), so that no element of Plaintiffs' case need rest on Defendant-prepared compilations.
7. **Be bound by the Court's construction of the Independent Contractor Agreements (Doc. 135) for all purposes, including trial** — extinguishing the first half of Plaintiffs' requested adverse inference by consent.

This package answers *Franklin Livestock*, the case Plaintiffs press hardest. There, the court found terminating sanctions warranted notwithstanding the offending party's self-disclosure — but the self-disclosure in *Franklin Livestock* was an *admission by the party itself* that it had created the false records, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 968, 970; party culpability was established, and the only question was whether contrition mitigated it. Here culpability is the open question. Defendants do not offer these commitments as penance for adjudicated wrongdoing; they offer them because they cure every concrete harm Plaintiffs identify while the attribution question is resolved on real evidence. *Franklin Livestock* says remediation cannot erase a proven fabrication by the sanctioned party. It does not say remediation is irrelevant to whether lesser sanctions suffice as to parties whose involvement remains unproven. And unlike the conduct condemned in *Roche Diagnostics* — forgeries "never . . . corrected, explained, or even acknowledged," 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 — the alteration here will be formally acknowledged as a fact of record, withdrawn, investigated by a neutral examiner, and presented to the factfinder.

Nor does this package create the moral hazard Plaintiffs invoke (the fabricator "reset[s] to the status quo ante at no cost"). Under these commitments, Defendants bear the forensic-examination costs, pay Plaintiffs' expert costs, lose the use of the documents, concede the contract interpretation through trial, hand the alteration evidence to the jury, and remain fully exposed to escalated sanctions — up to and including default — if the examination or hearing attributes the alteration to any of them. That is not "nothing to lose"; it is a structured path to findings with consequences attached.

### IV. Plaintiffs Have Not Shown Prejudice That These Measures Leave Uncured.

#### A. The merits were decided on the authentic agreements, in Plaintiffs' favor, before this Motion was filed.

The alteration deprived Plaintiffs of nothing on the merits. Plaintiffs signed the authentic agreements, kept them, and stipulate that the copies Defendants filed in July 2024 are true and correct. (Doc. 145 at 4.) The interpretation dispute the altered term supposedly targeted was decided *for Plaintiffs, on the authentic documents*, on February 19, 2026. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) The altered documents were never filed (except as exhibits to the sanctions motion), never used at any deposition or hearing, and never influenced any ruling. *See Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018 (N.D. Fla. 2025); *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009). Defendants do not cite these cases for the proposition that unfiled fabrications are unsanctionable — *Forsberg* forecloses that, and Defendants do not argue it. They cite them for what they actually bear on: the *magnitude of prejudice* and the *proportionality of remedy*, both of which turn on whether the challenged materials ever distorted, or could still distort, the adjudication. Here they did not and — once withdrawn, clawed back, and replaced by amended responses — cannot.

#### B. The forward-looking prejudice Plaintiffs identify is specifically addressed, item by item.

*Garcia* teaches that falsified evidence prejudices an opponent by "casting doubt on the veracity of all of the culpable party's submissions," forcing costly independent corroboration. 569 F.3d at 1180. Plaintiffs press exactly that theory: they say they can no longer trust the 49,000-document production or the Defendant-prepared Excel damages summaries. (Doc. 145 at 20.) Defendants' commitments meet that concern in kind, not in rhetoric: underlying source records will be produced so Plaintiffs need not rely on any Defendant-prepared summary; a neutral forensic examiner will test the integrity of the production systems at Defendants' expense; Plaintiffs' verification costs to date (the forensic expert) will be reimbursed; and the Court retains every supervisory tool — including escalation — if the examination reveals further irregularities. The remaining items of claimed prejudice are investigation costs (cured by payment), delay (substantially attributable to counsel transitions and a stipulated mediation stay, and in any event not remediable by default), and generalized distrust (addressed by verification, not by terminating 22 plaintiffs' claims without proof of breach, causation, or damages).

### V. Plaintiffs' Requested Relief Is Disproportionate at Every Tier.

#### A. Default judgment: the stakes make the disproportion concrete.

A default here would not merely end the case; it would enter judgment on RICO claims carrying treble damages and attorneys' fees, FDUTPA claims, and Truth in Leasing Act claims, for 22 plaintiffs, in a putative class action, against three separate defendants — including one (Ms. Begovic) already partially dismissed and connected to the altered documents by nothing in the record, and another (Mr. Kajdic) connected only by his corporate office. (Doc. 22 ¶¶ 87-144; Doc. 135 at 15.) "Because it is the most extraordinary of all remedies, [it] should be reserved for the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct," *Bijou*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 — and "explicit" misconduct means misconduct attributed to the party sanctioned, not to a username.

#### B. The adverse inference remains half-moot and half-default.

Deeming the *interpretation* of the agreements established asks the Court to decree what Doc. 135 already decided — and Defendants now consent to be bound by that construction through trial, mooting the request entirely. Deeming *breach* established for 22 plaintiffs would excuse Plaintiffs from proving performance, breach, causation, and damages — a default judgment in costume. Nor does spoliation doctrine fit: spoliation remedies compensate for evidence rendered unavailable, *see Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006); *Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009), and Plaintiffs face no evidentiary gap — they hold the authentic agreements, which are on the docket and stipulated authentic by both sides. What the alteration evidence *is* good for — argument to the factfinder about credibility and consciousness of weakness — Defendants' second commitment expressly preserves for Plaintiffs.

#### C. A special master is the disproportionate version of relief Defendants have already volunteered.

The special-master request (at Defendants' sole expense) presupposes an adjudication that Defendants as a group cannot be trusted with any discovery task. *See Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999). No such adjudication exists, and the targeted alternative — a neutral forensic examination of the actual systems at issue, at Defendants' expense, plus source-record production — polices the genuine risk at a fraction of the burden.

#### D. Fees.

An inherent-power fee award requires a bad-faith finding not yet made. *Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021). But the practical issue is largely resolved by Defendants' fifth commitment: payment of Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert costs without admission. Any broader fee request should await the hearing's findings, which will identify whether, and whose, conduct warrants it.

### VI. The Court Should Set an Evidentiary Hearing with Targeted Provenance Discovery Before Imposing Any Inherent-Power Sanction.

The dispositive questions — who altered the documents, at whose direction, and with which Defendant's knowledge — are questions of identity, intent, and credibility that no paper record can resolve. Due process requires notice and an opportunity to be heard before inherent-power sanctions, *In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) [VERIFY], and the findings *Telectron* demands cannot responsibly be made without testing the evidence. Defendants therefore request that the Court:

1. Set an evidentiary hearing on the Motion;
2. Order the provenance measures Defendants have volunteered (amended responses, declarations, neutral forensic examination) on a schedule that completes before the hearing, so the hearing proceeds on a developed record;
3. Permit Plaintiffs to examine each individual Defendant and the production custodians at the hearing — Defendants commit to producing them — without prejudice to Plaintiffs' merits depositions; and
4. Reserve ruling on the tier of any sanction until findings are made, defendant by defendant.

Defendants do not request the hearing to delay. They request it because every authority both sides cite imposed terminating sanctions only on a record establishing the sanctioned party's own culpability — and because, if the record after examination and testimony establishes any Defendant's culpability here, the Court will possess both the findings and the full arsenal, and Defendants will have no complaint about process. What the Court should not do is enter judgment first and find facts never.

## CONCLUSION

The forensic record establishes that documents in Defendants' production were altered; it does not establish by whom, at whose direction, or with which Defendant's knowledge. Defendants meet that record not with minimization but with remediation: amended responses, withdrawal and clawback of the altered range, sworn declarations, a neutral forensic examination at their expense, payment of Plaintiffs' expert costs, source-record production, and consent to be bound by this Court's contract interpretation through trial. The Motion's demand for collective default — judgment on treble-damages RICO claims for 22 plaintiffs without trial and without individualized findings — should be denied. Defendants respectfully request that the Court adopt the remediation and hearing framework set out in Sections III and VI, and deny the requested default judgment, adverse inference, and special-master relief as disproportionate on the present record.
