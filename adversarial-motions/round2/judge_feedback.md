# Judge's Round 2 Feedback (Final Pre-Submission Conference)

*Husidic v. FR8 Solutions, Inc., No. 3:24-cv-00963 (M.D. Fla.) — Simulated exercise. This is the final coaching round before submission. No winner is picked here, but I will tell each side candidly where its case is strongest and weakest as briefed.*

The Round 2 drafts are the first versions of these papers I would not be embarrassed to see on a real docket. Both sides took the Round 1 feedback seriously — in places, almost verbatim — and the dispute has now converged to its true core. Remarkably, **both sides now request the same procedural vehicle**: an evidentiary hearing preceded by provenance discovery. That convergence is healthy, but it means the final drafts will be judged on the narrow residue that is *actually* disputed: (1) whether FR8 can be sanctioned at the terminating level on agency attribution without identifying the human actor; (2) whether a remediation package volunteered after exposure, with a sanctions motion pending, can satisfy or defeat *Telectron*'s third element; and (3) who bears the consequences of nine months of party silence. Brief those three questions with real law, and cut nearly everything else.

## To Plaintiffs' Counsel

### Implementation Grade

**A− on substance; C on discipline.** You implemented nearly every structural instruction:

- Defendant differentiation (default vs. FR8; hearing-calibrated relief vs. Kajdic; tailored relief vs. Begovic) — done, and it transforms the motion. Section III is now the spine of your case instead of its soft underbelly.
- Burden-shifting/exclusive-custody argument, the silence theme, and the affirmative hearing request with provenance discovery — done (§§ I.D, II.B, V.A). Joining Defendants' own hearing request was the single smartest move in the draft; you have taken their most reasonable-sounding ask and made it yours.
- Alternative relief rebuilt around the damages phase; the half-moot adverse inference ("deem interpretation and breach established") is gone, replaced by the Doc. 135-binding order, source-document production, verification presumption, neutral examiner, and trial admissibility — done, and § V.B is now a menu a cautious judge can actually order from.
- Clear-and-convincing burden embraced up front — done, and the "no innocent hypothesis explains them together" framing of the Introduction's bullet list is effective.
- Delay narrative corrected with the October 7, 2025 first motion bolded; deposition-limit citation fixed (Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii), 30(d)(1) at n.5); "path to summary judgment" softened to "absent detection, nothing prevented their use" — all done.

Where you lost the letter grade: **I told you to compress, and the draft grew by roughly 1,000 words.** And the two published Eleventh Circuit authorities I told you to anchor the motion in — *Eagle Hospital* and *In re Mroz* — appear as bare [VERIFY] placeholders with no pin cites, no parentheticals, and no integration into the argument. A citation you have not verified is not an anchor; it is a liability.

### Remaining Weaknesses

1. **The FR8 agency theory — your lead ask — cites no agency law.** Section III.A argues "a corporation has no hands" and that FR8 is responsible "[w]hoever 'david' is," but the only authority offered is *Roche Diagnostics*, where corporate culpability was not seriously contested. You assert the dispositive legal proposition of your entire motion — that a corporation may suffer terminating sanctions for discovery fabrication by an unidentified agent within its document-collection process — **without a single case so holding**. Defendants' § II.D answers you directly: "agency attribution still requires evidence of who 'david' is and in what capacity that person acted," and posits that "david" could be an outside vendor. Right now you have rhetoric against their fact question. Find the law: cases sanctioning corporate parties for misconduct by unidentified or low-level personnel in the discovery process; respondeat-superior principles applied to litigation misconduct; and note that *Telectron* itself — the test both sides accept — defaulted a *corporate* defendant for document destruction carried out within its organization **[VERIFY the actor-identification facts of Telectron before using it this way]**. Also answer the vendor hypothetical head-on: a vendor processing FR8's production at FR8's direction is FR8's agent for exactly this purpose, and FR8 chose its agents.

2. **Your own hedge undercuts your lead ask.** Section IV.C closes: "Should the Court prefer to make its FR8 findings after the same evidentiary hearing . . . Plaintiffs do not object." Once you concede the hearing works for FR8 too, "default on the papers" reads as an opening bid rather than a serious request — and Defendants' v2 will exploit it. Pick a position: either (a) explain *why* no hearing is needed for corporate attribution (the agency facts — exclusive custody, FR8's records, FR8's production — are undisputed on the paper record; only individual culpability needs testimony), or (b) restructure the ask as "default against FR8 following the hearing unless the Court finds the existing record sufficient." Option (a) is bolder and defensible if you find the law in point 1; option (b) is more grantable. Do not keep both.

3. **The burden-shifting argument (§ II.B) is asserted, not supported.** "On that showing, the burden shifts to Defendants" cites nothing. There is doctrine in the neighborhood — exclusive-control inferences in spoliation law, the principle that a party cannot benefit from withholding evidence uniquely in its possession, and civil adverse inferences from silence — but you must cite it or soften the claim to a *permissive inference* the Court may draw. A federal judge will not invent a formal burden-shifting framework for you, and claiming one without authority invites Defendants to call the whole section makeweight.

4. **Defendants' remediation package is coming, and your draft cannot see it.** Their v2 § III volunteers nearly everything in your § V.A and much of § V.B: amended responses in 14 days, withdrawal/clawback, declarations from each Defendant and each production custodian, a neutral forensic examination at their expense, payment of your expert costs, source-record production, and consent to be bound by Doc. 135 through trial. Your final draft must answer it or your *Telectron* third-element argument (§ IV.C) collapses into a fight about a stipulation they have already superseded. The answers available to you: (i) the package arrived only after exposure, only with default on the table, and remains contingent ("[s]ubject to the Court's approval"), which is precisely the *Franklin Livestock* sequence — remediation after being caught does not negate the bad faith that preceded it; (ii) nothing in the package required a court order — the Rule 26(e) duty to amend has been self-executing for nine months, and a promise to comply with existing obligations is not a sanction; (iii) the package still identifies no one and admits nothing, so it addresses deterrence not at all; (iv) accept the package — and ask the Court to *order* it as a floor, with fees and the hearing on top, so the commitments carry contempt consequences rather than goodwill.

5. **Smaller points.** (a) Clarify Begovic's current posture: Doc. 135 dismissed Counts I and II against her — state plainly which claims still run against her, because the answer affects what sanctions are even available. (b) Your fee theory against Begovic ("fees attributable to the maintenance of her unamended response") sits awkwardly with your concession that the responses were signed and certified by *counsel*; tie her exposure to her personal knowledge of the authentic terms (her own declaration) plus her personal Rule 26(e) duty, or fold her fees into the general award. (c) The Introduction's bullet timeline, the Background, and §§ II.A–B repeat the same five facts (February 17, nine days, "david," the withdrawals, the silence) at least three times each. State the timeline once — a short table would serve you well — and cross-reference.

### Final-Round Instructions (prioritized)

1. **Cut to no more than 20 pages — roughly 5,500–6,000 words of body text.** The current draft is ~7,650 words and would push past 25 pages in 13-point Book Antiqua with proper spacing, bumping the Local Rule 3.01(a) limit. A busy district judge wants the motion at 15–18 pages. Where to cut: collapse the Introduction's bullets and Background §§ A–C into a single chronological statement of facts (you will save 2+ pages of repetition); compress § I.C (the fraud-on-the-court strawman) to one paragraph — you won that framing fight in Round 1 and Defendants' v2 expressly concedes *Forsberg* and abandons the frame, so a section "answering a motion Plaintiffs did not bring" now answers a *response that was not filed*; trim the case-distinguishing in § I.C's second paragraph to the two distinctions that matter (*Idearc* withdrawal-before-findings; *Bijou* "quotidian failings"); cut the institutional-prejudice item (it adds a citation, not a fact).
2. **Resolve every [VERIFY] tag or delete the citation.** *Eagle Hospital* and *In re Mroz* must appear with confirmed holdings, pin cites, and one-line parentheticals — these should become your lead authorities in § I.A, not afterthoughts. Same for *Gulf Atlantic*, *White Cap*, and *Bijou* (flagged since Round 1; still unverified). And brief the Eleventh Circuit standard of proof properly: you currently take clear-and-convincing from *Roche*, an out-of-circuit district case. Find what the Eleventh Circuit itself requires for inherent-power bad-faith findings, or say candidly that you accept the more demanding standard *arguendo*.
3. **Arm the FR8 agency theory with law** (Weakness 1) and resolve the paper-default-versus-hearing hedge (Weakness 2). This is where your motion will be won or lost.
4. **Add a section answering the remediation package** (Weakness 4) — one page, four moves: too late, already obligatory, admits nothing, order it as a floor.
5. **Support or soften the burden-shift** (Weakness 3).

## To Defendants' Counsel

### Implementation Grade

**A− on advocacy; incomplete on evidence.** This is a dramatically better brief than v1, and the best-written document either side has produced:

- The minimization is gone. The Introduction's first paragraph — reciting the tool, the date, the username, and the artifacts, and calling it "serious misconduct by *someone*" that "deserves a serious response" — buys you more credibility than anything else in the draft. The express withdrawal of the *Guzman*/unsworn-boilerplate argument ("that undersold them") is the kind of candor judges remember.
- Section VI's fraud-on-the-court overlay is gone; *Gulf Atlantic* and *Idearc* are now cited honestly for magnitude-of-prejudice and proportionality, with an explicit disclaimer that you do not argue unfiled fabrications are unsanctionable. Exactly as instructed.
- The delay argument is corrected — indeed, gracefully conceded ("Defendants do not contend that Plaintiffs slept on this issue"). The counsel-withdrawal elephant is addressed head-on in § II.C rather than ignored.
- The remediation package (§ III, seven commitments) is the single most consequential addition either side made in Round 2, and the *Franklin Livestock* counter-distinction (party culpability was *established* there; here it is the open question) is the right answer to their a-fortiori argument. The proportionality section now quantifies the stakes and severs the analysis by defendant.
- The February 17 timeline finally gets a direct answer (§ II.A), and the candor of "Defendants candidly acknowledge what they cannot argue: they cannot tell the Court, on this record, *what happened* on February 17, 2025" is well judged.

What keeps this from an A: **Round 1 told you the single highest-value addition would be declarations. You scheduled them instead of filing them.** A brief whose architecture rests on "the record does not establish our clients' culpability" while the clients — nine months after the accusation — still have not denied culpability under oath is a brief with a hole where its evidence should be.

### Remaining Weaknesses

1. **Promised declarations are not evidence, and the promise itself raises the question it defers.** Commitment 3 pledges sworn declarations "on the schedule the Court directs." Nothing prevents your clients from signing them *today*. Every reader — and certainly this one — will ask: if each Defendant can truthfully swear "I did not alter, direct, or know," why is that one-page declaration not attached to this Response? The deferral has only two plausible explanations: strategic sequencing (bad optics) or an inability to obtain truthful denials (far worse). The same goes for the amended discovery responses: the Rule 26(e) duty is self-executing; serving amendments "within 14 days of the Court's order" concedes you have spent nine months not doing something you could do unilaterally this afternoon. **For the final draft: attach the declarations and serve the amendments before filing, and report both as accomplished facts** — or state forthrightly why you cannot (privilege, Fifth Amendment exposure, an ongoing investigation), because at the hearing you requested, "we were waiting for a schedule" will not survive its first minute.

2. **Two record overstatements that will be punctured.** *First*, the Introduction asserts the username "belongs to none of the three Defendants." The record shows a username, "david"; it shows nothing about who it *belongs to*. Say what the record supports: the username matches none of the Defendants' names. *Second*, § II.A asserts February 17 fell within "a window in which it is undisputed that the documents passed through hands other than the three named Defendants'." Exhibit F establishes only that *counsel* made no alterations after receipt; it says nothing about whose hands touched the documents in February, and the production reached counsel *from Defendants*. That "undisputed" is doing unsupported work, as is § II.D's speculation that "david" might be "outside personnel involved in scanning or processing." Either support the processing-window account with a sworn custodian declaration (see Weakness 1) or strip the assertions to what Exhibit F actually says. After the credibility you earned by abandoning minimization, do not spend it on record-stretching.

3. **The Begovic-filed-the-authentic-agreements argument is double-edged, and you only show one edge.** Section II.B's second point — that Defendants had already filed the true agreements, so a substitution scheme would be self-defeating — is clever. But the same fact proves Begovic (and FR8, whose records she authenticated) *knew exactly what the authentic compensation terms said* when the contradictory certified responses issued and sat unamended for fourteen months. Plaintiffs' v2 makes precisely this move against Begovic. Anticipate it: the answer is presumably that knowledge of the authentic terms is not knowledge of the *altered production set*, and that no evidence shows any Defendant compared Bates range D002379–D005171 against the docket exhibits. Say so expressly.

4. **The *Bijou* gloss and the unresolved [VERIFY] tags.** Section V.A quotes *Bijou*'s "explicit and intentional misconduct" and then asserts that "'explicit' misconduct means misconduct attributed to the party sanctioned, not to a username." That definitional gloss is yours, not the court's; presented as it is, it reads as holding. Soften it to argument or find authority. And *In re Mroz* still carries [VERIFY] tags in three places — it is the load-bearing due-process citation for your entire process theory and cannot go to final in placeholder form. *Bijou* itself (2026 WL) remains unverified since Round 1.

5. **Structural repetition.** The remediation package now appears three times: summarized in the Introduction's second paragraph, enumerated in § III, and re-walked in § IV.B. State it once in § III; the Introduction gets one sentence; § IV.B should cross-reference by commitment number. You will recover a page and a half.

### Final-Round Instructions (prioritized)

1. **Convert promises into record evidence.** Attach the individual Defendants' declarations and an FR8 custodian/IT declaration; serve the amended discovery responses before filing and report it. If any client cannot truthfully sign, restructure the brief now around the position you can actually hold — including, if necessary, severing Begovic's defense more sharply from the others' (her separate counsel exists for a reason; use it). This is worth more than every other instruction combined.
2. **Attach a proposed order.** Your best strategic asset is that you are offering the Court an easy, appealable-error-proof path: ordered remediation, provenance discovery, hearing, findings, reserved escalation. Hand the Court that path in executable form — a one-page proposed order with dates. Make granting your version of relief the path of least resistance.
3. **Fix the two record overstatements** (Weakness 2) and the *Bijou* gloss; resolve every [VERIFY] tag or cut the cite. Like Plaintiffs, you take clear-and-convincing from *Roche* — brief what the Eleventh Circuit itself requires, because the standard of proof is your friend and deserves better than an out-of-circuit district citation.
4. **Pre-empt the "too little, too late" attack on the package.** Plaintiffs' final draft will say the package is post-exposure, contingent, and merely promises compliance with existing duties. Your § III answer should be: the commitments exceed any existing duty (neutral examination at your expense, expert-cost payment, trial admissibility of the alteration evidence, consent to be bound by Doc. 135); they are offered to be *ordered*, with contempt consequences; and performance has already begun (see Instruction 1). Also confront *Telectron* itself before Plaintiffs do — it defaulted a corporate defendant for document destruction within its organization; distinguish it on its facts (the identity and seniority of the responsible actor there) **[VERIFY Telectron's facts on this point before relying on the distinction]**.
5. **Hold the length.** You are at ~5,300 words; after de-duplicating the package (Weakness 5), the final should land at 15–17 pages. Do not let rebuttal additions push you past 18. Your brevity relative to Plaintiffs is itself advocacy.

## Judge's General Observations

**Length and readability — both sides, hear this.** The drafts have grown every round. For final submission: Plaintiffs at or under 20 pages (target 15–18; hard ceiling Local Rule 3.01(a)'s 25), Defendants at or under 18. A district judge deciding a sanctions motion reads the introduction, the timeline, the requested relief, and then samples the argument; if your decisive point is not visible in those four places, it does not exist. Both sides should consider a one-page chronology table (date | event | record cite) — this is a timeline case, and the side whose timeline the Court adopts into its order has half-won.

**The dispute has converged; brief the residue.** Both sides now want a hearing with provenance discovery; both accept clear-and-convincing; both accept *Telectron*; Defendants concede *Forsberg* and the seriousness of the forensic showing; Plaintiffs concede individualized process for the natural persons. The genuinely contested questions remaining are exactly three: (1) **corporate attribution** — whether FR8 can face terminating sanctions for an unidentified agent's fabrication, on which *neither side has yet cited a case in point*; (2) **the legal effect of post-exposure remediation** on *Telectron*'s third element; and (3) **the inferences permitted from party silence**, on which Plaintiffs cite nothing and Defendants offer only the promise to end it. The final drafts should spend their pages there.

**Citation discipline — final warning.** Every [VERIFY] tag must be resolved before submission; a final draft containing one will be treated as containing an unverified citation, with the consequences that carries in this district. Outstanding items: *Eagle Hospital* and *In re Mroz* (both sides; holdings and pin cites); *Bijou v. Kidd*, *Gulf Atlantic*, and *White Cap* (flagged in Round 1 by me, re-cited by both sides in Round 2 without verification); *Telectron*'s actor-identification facts (both sides now need them); and *Dragon Jade*'s fit with RFP-response amendment (Plaintiffs, carried over from Round 1, still unconfirmed). Both sides also still source the clear-and-convincing standard exclusively to *Roche*, an out-of-circuit district opinion; whichever side correctly states the Eleventh Circuit's actual requirement will own the standard-of-proof section of my order.

**Where each side currently stands — candidly, without picking a winner.** Plaintiffs' case is strongest on the *fact* of deliberate fabrication (means/motive/timing remains the best-supported showing in either brief) and weakest on the *law* of attribution — the FR8 agency theory is presently a syllogism without a citation. Defendants' case is strongest on process and proportionality — the remediation-plus-hearing architecture is the most grantable package before me — and weakest on evidence: nine months of client silence, now extended by a brief that promises oaths instead of swearing them. Each side's final draft should assume its opponent will fix its own worst flaw, and write accordingly.
