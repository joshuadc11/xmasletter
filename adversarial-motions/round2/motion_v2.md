### INTRODUCTION

This is not a "fraud on the court" motion. Plaintiffs do not invoke Rule 60(d) or the narrow "species of fraud which does or attempts to defile the court itself." *S.E.C. v. ESM Group, Inc.*, 835 F.2d 270, 273 (11th Cir. 1988). Plaintiffs move under this Court's *inherent power* to sanction a party's bad-faith abuse of the judicial process, *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45; under the spoliation doctrine, which reaches the "intentional destruction, mutilation, *alteration*, or concealment of evidence," *Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, No. 6:05-cv-1083-Orl-22DAB, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006) (emphasis added); and under Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1). None of those authorities requires that fabricated evidence first be filed with, or relied upon by, the Court. As the Eleventh Circuit has explained in an unpublished but persuasive decision, "[i]t would be strange to require a district court to wait until false evidence is introduced at trial before sanctions are appropriate, all the while wasting the time and resources of the court and the opposing party." *Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015).

Plaintiffs accept — indeed, embrace — the burden Defendants invoke: the predicate misconduct must be "demonstrated by clear and convincing evidence." *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, No. 18-CV-01479, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020). The following facts meet that standard now, on the existing record, because no innocent hypothesis explains them together:

- The compensation term of the Independent Contractor & Non-Compete Agreements ("Independent Contractor Agreements") — whether Plaintiffs are owed "88% of linehaul rate"[^2] — was the crystallized, contested question pending before this Court on Defendants' motion to dismiss from October 11, 2024 forward. (Doc. 52 at 2-4.)
- On **February 17, 2025**, while that motion was pending, the signed agreements were digitally altered in Adobe Acrobat — the authentic compensation terms (several handwritten) deleted and replaced with "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence," language that tracks Defendants' litigation position — with Plaintiffs' genuine signature pages retained. (Ex. E ¶ 15; Composite Ex. A ¶¶ 3-5; Doc. 52 at 3-4.)
- The alteration occurred while the documents were in **Defendants' exclusive custody**: Defendants' own counsel attests that the production sets were provided to counsel *by Defendants* and that counsel made no alterations beyond Bates stamping and redactions. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.)
- **Nine days later**, on February 26, 2025, Kajdic and Begovic served discovery responses — certified by counsel under Rule 26(g) — identifying *only* the altered agreements (Bates D002379-D005171) as the documents supporting Defendants' merits contention. (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) FR8 did the same on April 16, 2025. (Ex. C at 8 ¶ 29.) The *authentic* agreements were identified nowhere, and the personnel folder produced for each Plaintiff contained only the altered version. Those responses remain unamended today.
- In the eight-plus months since Plaintiffs raised the issue on September 24, 2025, **no Defendant has submitted a sworn declaration** denying alteration, direction, or knowledge; no one has identified the Adobe user "david"; no internal investigation has been described; and two successive sets of defense counsel withdrew, the first citing "ethical concerns" and an inability to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations." (Doc. 107 ¶ 7, at 3, 6; Doc. 117 at 4.)

[^2]: There are slight, non-material variations in the wording of the authentic contracts ("88% of linehaul rate," "88% linehaul," "88% of line haul," "88% of load"). None includes the "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" language. (Docs. 16-3 through 16-23.)

Defendants' Response answers this record with a single theme: that Plaintiffs cannot prove *which* Defendant "pushed the button." That argument misunderstands both the law of corporate responsibility and the posture of this Motion. As to **FR8**, no attribution gap exists at all: a corporation acts only through its agents, and the forgeries were created in FR8's own business records, within FR8's document-collection process, while in Defendants' exclusive custody, and were delivered by Defendants to counsel for production. Whoever "david" is, FR8 is responsible for the corruption of its own files and its own production. As to the **individual Defendants**, Plaintiffs do not ask the Court to skip process: Plaintiffs join in Defendants' own request for an **evidentiary hearing**, ask for targeted provenance discovery in advance of it, and seek calibrated relief against Kajdic and Begovic based on the findings made there. What Plaintiffs do not accept is the suggestion that Defendants may manufacture an attribution mystery inside their own walls, decline for eight months to offer a single sworn word about it, and then claim the mystery as their shield.

Plaintiffs therefore seek: (1) a default judgment against **FR8**; (2) as to **Kajdic and Begovic**, an evidentiary hearing, preceded by expedited provenance discovery, with terminating or other sanctions calibrated to the Court's findings; and (3) interim and remedial relief — amended discovery responses, damages-phase safeguards, and fees and costs — set out in the Requested Relief section below.

The authentic agreements provide that Plaintiffs are to be paid "88% of linehaul rate" (Docs. 16-3, 16-5–16-9, 16-12–16-23), "88% linehaul" (Doc. 16-4), "88% of line haul" (Doc. 16-11), or "88% of load" (Doc. 16-10). On October 11, 2024, Defendants moved to dismiss the FAC, arguing that Plaintiffs' claims "rest[] on a fundamentally flawed misreading and misrepresentation" of the Independent Contractor Agreements, that each truckload was a "separate subagreement (sic) or contract," and that Plaintiffs "misstate[d]" the "'line-haul rate' that would be agreed upon per truckload basis." ### ARGUMENT

#### I. GOVERNING STANDARDS: INHERENT POWER, SPOLIATION, AND RULES 26 AND 37 — UNDER A CLEAR-AND-CONVINCING BURDEN PLAINTIFFS ACCEPT — WITH THE PROCESS DUE PROCESS REQUIRES.

##### A. The governing standard is bad-faith abuse of the judicial process, which expressly reaches the doctoring of evidence in discovery.

Federal district courts possess the inherent power "to protect the orderly administration of justice and to preserve the dignity of the tribunal." *Kleiner v. First Nat'l Bank of Atlanta*, 751 F.2d 1193, 1209 (11th Cir. 1985) (citing *Roadway Express v. Piper*, 447 U.S. 752, 764-65 (1980)). That power "includes 'the ability to fashion an appropriate sanction for conduct which abuses the judicial process[.]'" *VIP Auto Glass, Inc. v. GEICO Gen. Ins. Co.*, No. 16-CV-2012, 2018 WL 3649638, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Jan. 3, 2018) (quoting *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45). It is "most often invoked where a party commits perjury or **destroys or doctors evidence**." *Access Innovators, LLC v. Usha Martin Ltd.*, No. 09-CV-2893, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1 (N.D. Ga. Apr. 28, 2010) (quoting *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1269 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (emphasis added). The Eleventh Circuit has confirmed in published authority that the inherent power extends to dismissing an action or entering default where a party perpetrates fraud in the litigation, and that bad faith is the key to its exercise. *See Eagle Hosp. Physicians, LLC v. SRG Consulting, Inc.*, 561 F.3d 1298 (11th Cir. 2009) **[VERIFY — published Eleventh Circuit authority affirming inherent-power terminating sanctions; confirm holding and pin cite]**; *In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) **[VERIFY — published Eleventh Circuit authority on bad-faith predicate and due-process prerequisites for inherent-power sanctions; confirm holding and pin cite]**.

The bad-faith predicate is disjunctive — not limited to fraud practiced on the court: "Bad faith exists when the court finds that a fraud has been practiced upon it, **or** that the very temple of justice has been defiled, **or** where a party or attorney knowingly or recklessly raises a frivolous argument, **delays or disrupts the litigation**, or hampers the enforcement of a court order." *Qantum*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1268-69 (cleaned up; emphasis added). The same is true of spoliation, which reaches the "intentional destruction, mutilation, **alteration**, or concealment of evidence." *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (quoting Black's Law Dictionary 1437 (8th ed. 2004)) (emphasis added); *see Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009) (available sanctions include default judgment, adverse inferences, and fees). Rule 37(c)(1) independently authorizes sanctions for a party's failure to supplement or amend responses to requests for production, *see Dragon Jade Int'l, Ltd. v. Ultroid, LLC*, No. 17-CV-2422, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 12, 2019); Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(e), 37(c)(1) — and even if the fit between Rule 26(e) and a "we will produce Bates range X" response were debated, the Court's inherent power independently reaches the maintenance of knowingly false certified responses. Counsel's signature on each response carried a Rule 26(g) certification, made "after a reasonable inquiry" of the clients, that the response was complete and correct when made.

Courts impose case-ending sanctions for fabricated documents produced *in discovery*, without any requirement that they were filed with the court. *See Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962, 971 (E.D.N.C. 2017), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018); *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009); *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, *11 (default where "Defendants' document production contains numerous demonstrable forgeries that have never been corrected, explained, or even acknowledged"); *Carroll-Harris v. Wilkie*, No. 17-CV-11711, 2019 WL 2205851, at *6-8 (E.D. Mich. May 22, 2019); *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, No. 18-61912-CIV, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019) (collecting cases); *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679 ("false evidence is sufficient cause for sanctions").

##### B. Plaintiffs accept the clear-and-convincing burden.

Plaintiffs do not duck the standard of proof; they invoke it. The predicate misconduct must be "demonstrated by clear and convincing evidence," *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, and Sections II and III below explain why the existing forensic record satisfies that standard as to the fact of deliberate fabrication within Defendants' custody, and why it satisfies it now as to FR8's responsibility. Where individualized findings as to the natural-person Defendants require credibility determinations, Plaintiffs ask the Court to make them the right way: at an evidentiary hearing, on competent evidence, after targeted provenance discovery. Clear and convincing proof may rest on circumstantial evidence — means, motive, timing, and exclusive custody — particularly where the direct evidence of "who pushed the button" lies entirely in the accused party's hands and the accused party declines to produce it.

##### C. Defendants' residual "fraud on the court" framing answers a motion Plaintiffs did not bring.

Defendants' Response continues to argue (§ VI) that a default-level inherent-power sanction "demands the same species of egregious, court-directed misconduct" as fraud on the court. That is not the law: *Chambers* requires bad faith, not court-directedness, and the *Qantum* formulation is expressly disjunctive. The fraud-on-the-court cases Defendants invoke — *Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018-19 (N.D. Fla. 2025); *Cerrato v. Nutribullet, LLC*, 2017 WL 1519815, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Apr. 27, 2017); *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009); *Bassett v. Wal-Mart Stores E., LP*, 2019 WL 4691824, at *2 (S.D. Fla. July 10, 2019); *Adams v. Cousins Transp. Sys., LLC*, 2020 WL 13348805, at *1 (S.D. Fla. July 6, 2020); *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026) — address whether judgments may be set aside or cases dismissed *on that distinct ground*; they say nothing about inherent-power sanctions for doctoring evidence in discovery, which is "most often" precisely where the inherent power operates. *Access Innovators*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1.

They are also factually inapposite: *Gulf Atlantic* involved damages spreadsheets, not forged signed contracts grafting genuine signature pages onto fabricated terms; *Idearc*'s offending exhibits were "withdrawn well before a finding of fact," 2009 WL 928556, at *5, whereas Defendants' certified responses identifying the Altered Agreements remain operative and unamended today; *Cerrato*'s "expose it at trial" logic is the very approach *Forsberg* rejected as "strange," 634 F. App'x at 679; *Bassett* and *Bijou* concern quotidian failings — "inconsistencies, poor recollection, . . . or even outright lies," *Bijou*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 — not forensically documented digital forgery of the operative contracts replicated across twenty-one documents; and *Adams* involved over-production of unrelated medical bills. And in any event, the misconduct here was placed into the judicial process: the forgeries entered the case through Rule 26(g)-certified responses identifying them as "the Independent Contractor Agreements" and as Defendants' merits evidence, while the motion to dismiss contesting that exact term was pending. Discovery is conducted under this Court's rules and authority, and its corruption "prejudices the court and its administration of the judicial process." *Martin v. SGT, Inc.*, No. 19-cv-00289, 2022 WL 17975993, at *16 (D. Utah Dec. 28, 2022). Absent detection, nothing prevented the Altered Agreements' use at summary judgment or trial.

##### D. Plaintiffs join the request for an evidentiary hearing.

Defendants close their Response by requesting an evidentiary hearing. (Resp. § IV.) Plaintiffs agree — and go further. Due process requires notice and an opportunity to be heard before inherent-power sanctions issue, *see In re Mroz*, 65 F.3d 1567 (11th Cir. 1995) **[VERIFY]**, and Plaintiffs affirmatively request a hearing as to the individual Defendants, preceded by the expedited provenance discovery described in Section V.A, so the hearing is conducted on evidence rather than mutual speculation. If the forensic record is as innocent as Defendants imply, a hearing is their opportunity to say so under oath — an opportunity they have had, and declined, for more than eight months.

#### II. THE FACT OF DELIBERATE FABRICATION WITHIN DEFENDANTS' EXCLUSIVE CUSTODY IS ESTABLISHED BY CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE, AND THE BURDEN OF AN INNOCENT EXPLANATION SHIFTS TO DEFENDANTS.

##### A. Means, motive, and timing establish willful fabrication.

- **Means**: The alterations required deliberate, skilled effort — scanning authentic contracts, substituting typed compensation pages for handwritten ones, replicating the same fabricated page (with its telltale identical smudge) across twenty-one agreements, and digital manipulation in Adobe Acrobat by user "david" that incidentally hid scanned hairs and left blurring artifacts. (Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15.) No innocent records-management process produces a single counterfeit compensation page inserted into twenty-one different signed contracts.
- **Motive**: The forged term — "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" — tracks Defendants' litigation position that each load was a separately negotiated "subagreement." (Doc. 52 at 3-4.) If the authentic agreements supported Defendants' position, there would have been no reason to alter them.
- **Timing**: The Dogic agreement was altered and saved on February 17, 2025 (Ex. E ¶ 15) — months after the motion to dismiss crystallized the interpretation dispute, while that motion was pending, and nine days before Kajdic and Begovic served certified responses identifying the altered set as their merits evidence (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18).

Fabricating and tendering knowingly false evidence is willful, bad-faith conduct. *See Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, No. CV 12-6972, 2015 WL 12732433, at *23 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015); *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1177-82; *Franklin Livestock*, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 971; *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *11.

##### B. Exclusive custody shifts the burden of explanation to Defendants — and their silence is itself evidence.

Whoever performed the alteration did so while the documents were in Defendants' exclusive custody. That is not inference; it is the testimony of Defendants' own former counsel: the hardcopy production was delivered "in the form [it was] provided by Defendants to then-Defendants' counsel," and the electronic set was likewise "provided by Defendants," with counsel making "no alterations . . . besides Bates stamping and making certain redactions." (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) The documents are FR8's business records (Doc. 16-2 ¶ 19), assembled into FR8's personnel files, altered on February 17, 2025 — before any of it reached counsel or Plaintiffs.

On that showing, the burden shifts to Defendants to come forward with an innocent explanation. Every fact bearing on provenance — who assembled the production, who scanned the agreements, who had access to the files in February 2025, who "david" is — lies exclusively within Defendants' knowledge and control. Plaintiffs cannot subpoena the inside of FR8's file room; Defendants can simply describe it. Yet in the eight-plus months since September 24, 2025: no Defendant has sworn a declaration denying alteration, direction, or knowledge; no one has identified "david"; no internal investigation has been described; no corrected documents have been produced; and the certified responses remain unamended. What the record does contain is the reaction of the professionals closest to the documents: after "a reasonable review and conferral addressing the issues raised in the [September 24 letter]," Defendants' counsel cited "ethical concerns," declared themselves unable to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations," and withdrew — followed within weeks by the withdrawal of their successors. (Doc. 107 ¶ 7, at 3, 6; Doc. 117 at 4.) A party with an innocent provenance story does not respond to a forgery accusation with two waves of counsel withdrawals and eight months of silence.

#### III. ATTRIBUTION: THE COURT SHOULD ANALYZE EACH DEFENDANT SEPARATELY — AND THE ANALYSIS SUPPORTS DEFAULT AGAINST FR8 AND HEARING-CALIBRATED SANCTIONS AGAINST THE INDIVIDUALS.

Defendants' core argument is that Plaintiffs' forensic showing "identifies a username, not a Defendant." (Resp. § II.A.) Plaintiffs take the point seriously, and this revised Motion differentiates the three Defendants accordingly. What the argument cannot do is what Defendants ask of it: defeat all sanctions against all Defendants because the misconduct occurred anonymously inside their own organization.

##### A. FR8: the attribution "gap" does not exist for a corporation, which acts only through its agents.

A corporation has no hands. It acts — assembles personnel files, scans contracts, collects documents for production, responds to discovery — only through natural persons acting within its document-management and litigation processes. The Altered Agreements are FR8's business records (Doc. 16-2 ¶ 19), were maintained in FR8's personnel files, were altered on February 17, 2025 while in Defendants' custody, and were furnished by Defendants to counsel for production as FR8's response to discovery served on FR8. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5; Ex. C at 2 ¶ 3, 8 ¶ 29.) Whether "david" is an officer, an employee, or a vendor acting within FR8's collection process, the corruption of FR8's own production is FR8's responsibility. The sanctioned "party" in *Roche Diagnostics* was likewise a corporate defendant whose "document production contain[ed] numerous demonstrable forgeries that have never been corrected, explained, or even acknowledged" — and that allegation "alone . . . is more than enough to require case-ending sanctions." 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. FR8 cannot avoid responsibility by declining to identify which of its own agents corrupted its own files; if anything, that refusal compounds the misconduct. The *Telectron* elements as applied to FR8 are addressed in Section IV.

##### B. Kajdic: the existing record warrants a hearing, with terminating sanctions on the table.

Kajdic is FR8's CEO. (Doc. 151 at 1.) His certified discovery response — served February 26, 2025, nine days after the forensic alteration date — identified the altered Bates range, and only that range, as the support for his merits contention, and it remains unamended. (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) Plaintiffs do not ask the Court to find today that Kajdic personally altered or directed the alteration of the agreements; Plaintiffs ask the Court to convene the evidentiary hearing Defendants themselves request, after the provenance discovery described below, and to calibrate sanctions against Kajdic — up to and including default — to the findings made there. In the interim, the Court should order Kajdic to amend his discovery responses and to account, under oath, for the provenance of Bates range D002379-D005171.

##### C. Begovic: tailored relief now; further relief only on hearing findings.

Plaintiffs acknowledge the facts Defendants marshal for Begovic: her July 2024 declaration attached the authentic agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19), and Counts I and II against her have been dismissed (Doc. 135 at 15). Accordingly, Plaintiffs do not seek default judgment against Begovic at this time. But her declaration is not the absolution Defendants present. It establishes that Begovic — FR8's CFO and the custodian who authenticated its contract records under oath — knew exactly what the authentic compensation terms said. Seven months later, her own certified discovery response identified a contradictory set of agreements as her sole merits evidence (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18), and in the fourteen-plus months since, she has neither amended that response nor offered a sworn word about how FR8's files came to contain forged versions of documents she personally authenticated. As to Begovic, Plaintiffs seek now: an order compelling amended responses and a sworn provenance accounting; her examination at the evidentiary hearing; and fees attributable to the maintenance of her unamended response. Any further relief should abide the hearing.

##### D. Defendants' authorities confirm that attribution findings are made after process — which is what Plaintiffs request.

Defendants observe that in *Garcia*, *Franklin Livestock*, *Forsberg*, *Roche Diagnostics*, *Art Remedy*, and *Am. Rena*, courts found or the parties admitted that the sanctioned party fabricated or knowingly submitted the evidence. True — and in each case that finding emerged from exactly the kind of process Plaintiffs now invoke: developed records, hearings, and adverse inferences from the sanctioned party's conduct. Those cases do not hold that a movant must produce a confession before process begins; they hold that findings must precede terminating sanctions. Plaintiffs' request — default against the corporate party whose production was corrupted, plus a hearing with provenance discovery as to the individuals — honors that sequence.

#### IV. AS TO FR8, ALL THREE *TELECTRON* ELEMENTS ARE SATISFIED, AND DEFAULT JUDGMENT IS THE APPROPRIATE SANCTION.

Before entering a case-terminating sanction, a court must find that: (1) the party acted willfully or in bad faith; (2) the movant was prejudiced; and (3) lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987).

##### A. Willfulness and bad faith: established by the means/motive/timing showing and FR8's responsibility for its own production.

Sections II and III.A establish, by clear and convincing evidence, that FR8's contract records were deliberately forged within its custody to mirror its litigation position, certified as its merits evidence, and never corrected. The misconduct is "closely intertwined with the merits of the case," which "militates heavily in favor of the severe sanction of default." *Qantum*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1269; *see Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. FR8's suggestion that the Altered Agreements are not intertwined with the merits because the authentic contracts govern is irreconcilable with its own discovery responses, which identified the Altered Agreements — and only the Altered Agreements — as the documents supporting its merits contention. (Ex. C at 2 ¶ 3, 8 ¶ 29.) A party cannot designate forged documents as its merits evidence and then disclaim their relevance once the forgery is exposed.[^5]

[^5]: Nor can Defendants fault Plaintiffs for not first deposing them on the issue. Plaintiffs are presumptively entitled to one deposition of each party, not to be repeated without leave, and limited to one day of seven hours. Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(a)(2)(A)(ii), 30(d)(1). Discovery is ongoing — Defendants have yet to produce their relevant text messages — and Plaintiffs should not be required to expend their only deposition of each key witness prematurely. That is precisely why Section V.A requests provenance depositions that do not count against the merits-deposition limits.

Nor does the proposed stipulation alter the analysis. In *Franklin Livestock*, dismissal was affirmed even though the offending party *self-disclosed* the doctored evidence, disavowed it, and produced the authentic records — because "the creation of false records that are passed off as originals and which materially contradict the original records later produced cannot be glossed over as a 'mistake.'" 251 F. Supp. 3d at 968, 970. Defendants' stipulation is weaker than the corrective conduct found insufficient there: it followed exposure rather than self-disclosure; it acknowledges and explains nothing; it identifies no responsible person; and it leaves the certified responses untouched. *See Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, *9; *Carroll-Harris*, 2019 WL 2205851, at *6.

##### B. Prejudice: Doc. 135 did not cure it, because the prejudice was never limited to the contract-interpretation ruling.

Defendants argue Plaintiffs suffered no prejudice because they always possessed the authentic agreements and this Court has "already construed those authentic agreements in Plaintiffs' favor." (Resp. §§ II-III; Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) That argument mistakes the narrowest slice of the harm for the whole of it — and it asks the Court to grade an attempted fraud by its failure.

*First*, Doc. 135 is a pleading-stage ruling. Defendants have never stipulated to be bound by its construction through summary judgment and trial; to the contrary, their stipulation offer carefully concedes only which documents are "operative," not what they mean or that Defendants will not re-litigate their meaning. If Defendants intend Doc. 135 to extinguish prejudice, the Court should hold them to it: Plaintiffs' alternative relief includes an order binding Defendants to Doc. 135's interpretation for all purposes, including trial.

*Second*, cognizable prejudice from falsified evidence has never required that the falsification succeed. It includes having to "consum[e] substantial resources to respond to and 'undo' the victimizer's lies and distortions," *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 680 (cleaned up), and the corrosion of the entire production: "falsified evidence substantially prejudices an opposing party by casting doubt on the veracity of all of the culpable party's submissions throughout litigation," forcing the victim "either to attempt independent corroboration of each submission, at substantial expense of time and money, or to accept the real possibility that those discovery documents submitted by the opposing party are inaccurate." *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1180. Each is concretely present:

1. **Investigative burden.** Plaintiffs' attorneys and staff spent significant time investigating the Altered Agreements and retained and paid a forensic expert. Those costs are real and were imposed by the fabrication, not by Plaintiffs' caution.
2. **Delay.** The fabrication and its aftermath — counsel withdrawals (Docs. 103, 104, 106, 116, 125, 126), the protective-order motion suspending production (Doc. 107 ¶ 7), vacated deadlines (Doc. 116), and the stay (Docs. 123-133) — derailed the schedule and Plaintiffs' prosecution of their claims.
3. **The damages phase — where the prejudice lives going forward.** Given the volume of records, Plaintiffs agreed to accept Excel spreadsheets *prepared by Defendants* purporting to summarize authentic underlying records — the very evidence from which 22 Plaintiffs' damages will be computed. A party whose production has been forensically shown to contain forgeries cannot ask its adversary, or this Court, to take its self-prepared damages summaries on faith. *See White Cap., L.P. v. Heyden Enters., LLC*, No. 23-14248, 2025 WL 414721, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Feb. 6, 2025) ("The parties — and the Court — cannot trust that [Defendants] have produced, or will ever produce, all discoverable evidence." (quoting *Bernal v. All. Am. Inv. Realty, Inc.*, 479 F. Supp. 2d 1291, 1339 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (cleaned up)). The contract-interpretation ruling cures none of this; the case is now substantially *about* damages, and the damages evidence flows entirely through the party that corrupted its own production.
4. **Concealment.** The prejudice is not only what was added but what was withheld: the authentic agreements were absent from the personnel folders produced and were identified in no response. Had Plaintiffs not kept their own copies and compared them, the substitution was undetectable from the production itself. Spoliation includes "concealment of evidence." *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7.
5. **Institutional prejudice.** The fabrication "prejudices the court and its administration of the judicial process." *Martin*, 2022 WL 17975993, at *16; *see Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *10.

*Third*, the "no harm" argument proves too much. On Defendants' theory, any fabrication detected before merits use is costless. That is the precise logic *Forsberg* rejected — courts need not "wait until false evidence is introduced at trial," 634 F. App'x at 679 — and it would teach litigants that forging the dispositive contracts carries no downside beyond a stipulation restoring the status quo if caught. Absent detection, nothing prevented the Altered Agreements' use at summary judgment and trial.

##### C. Lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence as to FR8.

Case-terminating sanctions are appropriate where a party fabricates evidence on a key issue. *See Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7; *VIP Auto Glass*, 2018 WL 3649638, at *4-5; *Access Innovators*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *3. Exclusion of the Altered Agreements would be an "insufficient sanction," because "litigants could infer that they have everything to gain, and nothing to lose by manufacturing evidence," *Carroll-Harris*, 2019 WL 2205851, at *8 (internal quotations omitted), and fees alone would not "sufficiently address [Plaintiffs'] concern about the veracity of other documents disclosed," *id.* at *7. Defendants' stipulation is functionally less than exclusion: a reset to the status quo ante at no cost beyond conceding the forgery failed. Plaintiffs recognize that default against FR8 is a grave remedy carrying consequences across RICO, FDUTPA, Truth in Leasing Act, and contract claims; that is what the forgery of the operative contracts of 21 class representatives, certified as merits evidence and never corrected, warrants. Should the Court prefer to make its FR8 findings after the same evidentiary hearing requested for the individual Defendants, Plaintiffs do not object — provided the interim relief in Section V issues now.

#### V. ALTERNATIVE AND INTERIM RELIEF: AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING WITH PROVENANCE DISCOVERY, AND REMEDIES BUILT AROUND THE DAMAGES PHASE.

##### A. Evidentiary hearing, preceded by expedited provenance discovery and compelled amended responses.

If the Court declines to enter default against FR8 on the papers — and as to Kajdic and Begovic in all events — Plaintiffs request that the Court set an evidentiary hearing and order, in advance of it:

1. **Provenance interrogatories/declarations within 14 days**: each Defendant to identify, under oath, all persons who collected, scanned, assembled, or had access to the documents produced as Bates range D002379-D005171; the identity of the Adobe user "david"; and a description of any investigation undertaken since September 24, 2025.
2. **Forensic preservation and imaging** of the computers and systems used in the February 2025 document-collection process, by agreement or by a neutral examiner (see § V.B).
3. **Provenance depositions** — including of "david" once identified and of each Defendant on provenance topics only — *not counted against* Plaintiffs' merits-deposition entitlements under Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii) and 30(d)(1).
4. **Amended Rule 26 responses within 14 days**, identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing the identification of Bates range D002379-D005171, with an accounting of that range's provenance. Rule 26(e); Rule 37(c)(1); *Dragon Jade*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4.

At the hearing, the Court can make the individualized findings due process requires — and can draw the inferences the law permits from any continued silence by parties uniquely positioned to explain their own files.

##### B. Damages-phase remedies in lieu of the prior adverse-inference request.

Plaintiffs no longer ask the Court to "deem established" the interpretation of the agreements (Doc. 135 resolved it at the pleading stage) or Defendants' breach. In their place, Plaintiffs request remedies proportioned to where the prejudice actually lies:

1. **An order binding Defendants to Doc. 135's construction** of the Independent Contractor Agreements for all purposes in this case, including summary judgment and trial — converting Defendants' "the merits are already decided" argument into a commitment.
2. **Production of all underlying source documents** for any damages-related data, at Defendants' expense, in native format with metadata; Plaintiffs shall not be required to accept Defendant-prepared Excel summaries in lieu of source records.
3. **A rebuttable presumption against the accuracy of any Defendant-prepared summary or compilation**, unless verified against source documents, with the costs of verification shifted to Defendants.
4. **A neutral forensic examiner** (a more economical alternative to a Rule 53 special master, which Plaintiffs request in the further alternative, *see Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, No. 06-20624-CIV, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999)), at Defendants' expense, to audit the integrity of Defendants' remaining production, including text messages and damages materials.
5. **A ruling that the evidence of fabrication is admissible at trial**, including as evidence of Defendants' consciousness of the weakness of their contractual position. This is a true evidentiary remedy — not a deemed admission — and it answers Defendants' objection that adverse inferences "presuppose an evidentiary gap": the spoliation here took the form of *alteration and concealment*, *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7, and the factfinder is entitled to know what was done to the operative documents and to weigh it.

##### C. Fees and costs.

The Court should award Plaintiffs the fees and costs incurred in uncovering the fabrication — including the forensic expert — and in pursuing sanctions, under its inherent power, *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45, the spoliation doctrine, *Swofford*, 671 F. Supp. 2d at 1280, and Rule 37(c)(1), *Dragon Jade*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4. Unlike *Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021), where fees were denied because neither fraud nor bad faith was proven, the bad-faith predicate here is supported by forensic evidence Defendants have not answered with a single sworn word.
