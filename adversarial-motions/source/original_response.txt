                                  INTRODUCTION

       FR8 Solutions, Inc. (“FR8”) is a logistics company that, among other things,

 hires independent contractors to transport freight throughout the United States.

 Mr. Kadjic and Ms. Begovic are FR8’s Chief Executive Officer and Chief Financial

 Officer, respectively. Plaintiffs are 22 drivers who previously contracted with FR8

 to transport freight.

       Setting aside its more sensational allegations, Plaintiffs’ lawsuit is, at

 bottom, a straightforward action to recover damages for the alleged breach of their

 respective contracts. Unfortunately, what should have begun—and remained—as

 a commercial dispute over the terms and performance of written agreements has

 instead been prosecuted by Plaintiffs as a cosmic battle between good and evil.

       Alas, Plaintiffs now contend that 21 contracts Defendants produced as part

 case is governed by the terms and conditions of the 21 Independent Contractor &

 Non-Compete      Agreements      (the   “Independent     Contractor   Agreements”)

 submitted with Plaintiffs’ complaints and attested to in Ms. Begovic’s Declaration,

 which was filed in connection with Defendants’ Motion to Dismiss on July 16,

 2024. Thus, contrary to Plaintiffs’ lamentations, the allegedly “fabricated”

 contracts are not “closely intertwined with the merits of the case,” assuming they

 have any relevance at all.

        Second, and more fundamentally, deceit that occurs between parties to

 litigation—including perjury or the fabrication of evidence—does not constitute

 fraud on the court. Instead, the alleged deceit must be directed toward the court

 itself and for the purpose of influencing the court’s decision making.

        Here, the challenged contracts were never presented to the Court, much less

 relied on by Defendants for any purpose. At most, the alleged deceit Plaintiffs

 complain of occurred, if at all, purely between the parties as part of the discovery

 process. As such, no grounds exist for imposing any sanction for “fraud on the

 court,” much less the extraordinary remedy Plaintiffs demand here 1.

                         ARGUMENT AND MEMORANDUM OF LAW

        The Eleventh Circuit defines “fraud on the court” as “embracing only that

 species of fraud which does or attempts to defile the court itself, or is a fraud

 perpetrated by the officers of the court so that the judicial machinery cannot perform

 in the usual manner its impartial task of adjudging cases that are presented for

 adjudication[.]” S.E.C. v. ESM Group, Inc., 835 F.2d 270, 273 (11th Cir. 1988)

 (quoting Traveler’s Indemnity Co. v. Gore, 761 F.2d 1549, 1551 (11th Cir. 1985))

 (emphasis added) (alterations omitted). Under this exacting standard, only the

 most “egregious misconduct—such as an ‘unconscionable plan or scheme’ to

 influence the court’s decision”—constitutes fraud on the court. DeBose v. USF Bd. of

 Trustees, 844 F. App’x 99, 102 (11th Cir. 2021) (emphasis added).

       In other words, fraud on the court requires conduct directed towards the

 court itself. See Bassett v. Wal-Mart Stores E., LP, 2019 WL 4691824, at *2 (S.D. Fla.

 July 10, 2019) (“[O]nly a party’s egregious misconduct towards the Court will result

 in dismissal for fraud on the court. Chicanery—even deceit between the parties—

 will thus rarely rise to the level of iniquity required to dismiss a complaint for

 fraud on the court.”) (emphasis added). Thus, fraud between parties does not

 constitute fraud on the court. See Gupta v. Walt Disney World Co., 482 F. App’x 458,

 459 (11th Cir. 2012) (“Fraud between parties does not constitute fraud on the court,

 as it does not carry the same threat of public injury.”) (citing ESM Group, 835 F.2d

 at 273); Patterson v. Lew, 265 F. App’x 767, 769 (11th Cir. 2008) (“We have

 consistently held that a fraud between parties is not fraud on the court.”).

       This principle applies even where a party is alleged to have committed

 perjury or fabricated documents. See, e.g., Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC,

 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018–19 (N.D. Fla. 2025) (denying motion for sanctions and

Case 3:24-cv-00963-WWB-SJH        Document 151     Filed 05/07/26   Page 5 of 11 PageID

 stating, “[t]hose documents were exchanged between the parties as part of the

 discovery process. That alone is sufficient to doom [the movant’s] fraud-on-the-

 court claim as it relates to those documents.”); Cerrato v. Nutribullet, LLC, 2017 WL

 1519815, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Apr. 27, 2017) (“Here, the evidence shows that at worst,

 Plaintiffs have committed perjury and fabricated evidence. Such actions constitute

 harm between the parties, not harm to the Court.”).

       In such circumstances, the remedy is not sanctions but exposure through the

 discovery process and trial. See Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Associates, P.A., 2009

 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009) (denying motion to dismiss for fraud on

 the court and stating, “[p]erjury and fabricated evidence are evils that can and

 should be exposed at trial, and the legal system encourages and expects litigants

 to root them out as early as possible”) (quoting Great Coastal Express v. International

 Broth. of Teamsters, Chauffeurs, Warehousemen, 675 F.2d 1349, 1357 (4th Cir.1982));

 Council v. Am. Fed’n of Gov't Employees (AFGE) Union, 559 F. App’x 870, 873 (11th

 Cir. 2014) (“Perjury and fabricated evidence do not constitute fraud upon the

 court, because they are evils that can and should be exposed at trial[.]”) (internal

 quotation marks and citation omitted).

       Such is the case here. Plaintiffs do not contend that Defendants made any

 representation to them, much less to the Court, as to the provenance, authenticity,

 or admissibility of the 21 documents. See Guzman v. Holiday CVS, LLC, 2022 WL

 22824873, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Oct. 13, 2022) (“To constitute fraud on the court, the

 plaintiff must direct his actions to the court[.]”) (internal quotation marks and

 citation omitted). Indeed, aside from producing them in discovery, Defendants

 have never relied on the 21 contracts for any purpose, much less filed them with

 this Court. Cf. Gulf Atl., 796 F. Supp. 3d at 1018 (denying motion for sanctions and

 stating, “[t]he Court also wishes to make clear that the damages calculations,

 spreadsheet, and other discovery responses were not filed with the Court (except

 as exhibits to this motion for sanctions)”).

       To be clear, the only representation Defendants have ever made—both to

 the Court and Plaintiffs’ counsel—is that the Independent Contractor Agreements

 attached to Ms. Begovic’s Declaration are the operative agreements governing this

 dispute. See Dkt. 16-2 ¶ 18 (attesting to the authenticity of the 21 Independent

 Contractor Agreements executed between Plaintiffs and FR8 Solutions, Inc.); Dkt.

 145 p. 13 (“As part of the conferrals on this Motion, Defendants stated that they

 are willing to stipulate that ‘any documents containing writings that contradict

       Nevertheless, Defendants feel compelled to point out that even if Plaintiffs’

 allegations of attempted deceit occurred, any such alleged deceit Plaintiffs

 complain of would have occurred purely between the parties as part of the

 discovery process. Cf. Gulf Atl., 796 F. Supp. 3d at 1018–19 (denying motion for

 sanctions and stating, “[the allegedly fabricated] documents were exchanged

 between the parties as part of the discovery process. That alone is sufficient to

 doom [the movant’s] fraud-on-the-court claim as it relates to those documents.”).

 This should end the inquiry: no grounds exist for imposing any sanction for “fraud

 on the court,” much less “the most extraordinary of all remedies” Plaintiffs

 demand here. Bijou v. Kidd, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026)

 (“Because it is the most extraordinary of all remedies, [dismissal] should be

 reserved for the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct.

 It is not enough to simply show inconsistencies, poor recollection, non-disclosure,

 mistaken beliefs, ambiguity, bad conduct, or even outright lies.”); Branch-Haines v.

 E. Coast Waffles, Inc., 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021) (“CW’s request

 for an award of attorneys’ fees and costs it has incurred as a result of Plaintiff’s

 by the parties’ proposed stipulation that the original contracts govern the claims

              The dispute over the evidence in the filings offered

       Plaintiffs’ authorities do not change the result. To be sure, each involved a

 party that knowingly submitted false evidence to a court and then relied on the

 evidence for the purpose of influencing the court’s decision. See, e.g., Forsberg v.

 Pefanis, 634 F. App’x 676, 678 (11th Cir. 2015) (“In the plea agreement, Pefanis

 admitted that he knew the statement was false and had been submitted to the

 district court and that he testified falsely that Popke provided Pefanis with the

 information that was put into the statement.”) (internal quotation marks and

 alterations omitted); Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC, 2019 WL 13063496, at

 *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019) (“[I]n this case, Moes fabricated blog posts and

 repeatedly relied upon and presented them, not only to support her defense, but

 to obtain a dismissal and a sum of money from Plaintiff.”); Roche Diagnostics Corp.

 v. Priority Healthcare Corp., 2020 WL 2308319, at *2 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020)

 (production of “hundreds of digitally altered test-strip invoices in response to the

 court’s discovery order,” among other discovery abuses, warranted terminating

 sanctions); Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co., Ltd., 2015 WL 12732433, at *8

 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015) (defendants’ submission of three fraudulent declarations

 in multiple filings warranted terminating sanctions).

       In short, Plaintiffs have not articulated conduct that any court within the

 Eleventh Circuit recognizes as fraud on the court, much less proven it by clear and

 convincing evidence. For these reasons, the Court should deny Plaintiffs’ motion.

 See Adams v. Cousins Transp. Sys., LLC, 2020 WL 13348805, at *1 (S.D. Fla. July 6,

 2020) (denying motion for to dismiss for fraud on the court and stating, “Plaintiffs’

 production of [unrelated medical bills] in discovery [does] not amount to fraud on

 the court.”) (emphasis in original).
