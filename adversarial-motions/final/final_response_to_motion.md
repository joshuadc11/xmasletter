## ARGUMENT AND MEMORANDUM OF LAW

### I. The Governing Standard Is Now Common Ground: Party-Specific Bad Faith, Clear and Convincing Proof, and Telectron Findings Before Any Terminating Sanction.

Plaintiffs invoke the inherent power, the spoliation doctrine, and Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1). (Doc. 145 at 3.) Defendants accept that framework, and accept two propositions Plaintiffs press: the inherent power reaches the doctoring of evidence in discovery, and false evidence need not be filed with the Court to be sanctionable. *See Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015) (unpublished).

Three principles govern what remains disputed. *First*, inherent-power sanctions require a finding of bad faith by the party to be sanctioned. *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45; *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1268-69 (S.D. Fla. 2007). *Second*, both sides have embraced the clear-and-convincing standard applied in Plaintiffs' own lead authority, *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7; that agreed standard is therefore the law of this Motion, and it is the right one — a finding that a litigant fabricated evidence carries quasi-criminal stigma and, here, case-ending consequences. *Third*, a terminating sanction requires findings that (1) the party acted willfully or in bad faith, (2) the movant was prejudiced, and (3) lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron*, 116 F.R.D. at 131; (Doc. 145 at 14 (invoking the same elements)). Default remains "the most extraordinary of all remedies," "reserved for the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct." *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026). Defendants submit — as argument, for the Court's consideration — that every terminating-sanctions decision either side has cited fits that description the same way: in each, the misconduct was found or admitted to be the *sanctioned party's own*. (*See* § II.D.)

The process question, finally, is no longer contested. Both sides now request an evidentiary hearing preceded by provenance discovery before any individualized sanction issues. Defendants do not burden the Court with additional citation for the proposition that findings should precede punishment; it is built into *Chambers*' bad-faith predicate and *Telectron*'s findings requirement, and Plaintiffs have joined the request. The only live questions are the three this Court must actually decide: whether FR8 may be defaulted on the papers on an agency theory; what legal effect Defendants' remediation — now partly performed, partly tendered for ordering — has on *Telectron*'s third element; and what inferences nine months of party silence permit. Sections II through V address them in turn.

### II. The Record Does Not Permit a Clear and Convincing Finding of Bad Faith Against Any Named Defendant Without a Hearing — Including FR8.

#### A. The February 17 timeline: what it shows and what it cannot show without testimony.

Plaintiffs' most forceful fact is the sequence: a forensic save on February 17, 2025 (Ex. E ¶ 15), nine days before Mr. Kajdic and Ms. Begovic served responses identifying the soon-to-be-produced Bates range as their merits support (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18), while the motion to dismiss contesting that very term was pending. Defendants will not pretend that sequence is unremarkable. It is the central fact of this Motion.

The direct answer is that the timeline establishes temporal proximity, and temporal proximity does not identify an actor. February 17, 2025 falls within the period in which Defendants' records were being assembled and processed for the early-March hardcopy production. What the record establishes about custody in that window comes from Exhibit F alone: former counsel received the production sets from Defendants and made no alterations beyond Bates stamping and redactions. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) The record does not establish who physically handled, scanned, or assembled the files before they reached counsel, who operated the computer on February 17, who the Adobe user "david" is, whether that person acted on instruction or alone, or whether any named Defendant knew the production set differed from the agreements already filed at Docket 16-3 through 16-23. Defendants candidly acknowledge what they cannot argue: on this record, they cannot tell the Court what happened on February 17, 2025 — and they do not ask the Court to credit speculation about who "david" might be. That is precisely what the Proposed Order's declarations and neutral forensic examination exist to answer, under oath and at Defendants' expense, before the hearing. Plaintiffs' refrain that the forensic evidence is "unrebutted" is true only in the sense that no witness has yet been put to proof. "Unrebutted on a paper record" is not a finding, and cannot substitute for one when the relief sought is judgment without trial.

Nor does "exclusive custody" convert proximity into proof. Plaintiffs ask the Court to hold that custody plus an unexplained alteration *shifts the burden* to Defendants to prove innocence — but they cite no authority adopting such a framework, and the doctrine they gesture toward supports at most a *permissive inference* available to a factfinder after evidence is taken. Defendants do not resist that inference being available at the hearing; the Proposed Order affirmatively builds the record on which the Court can weigh it. What due process does not permit is treating an unproven inference as an adjudicated fact and entering judgment on it.

#### B. The certified responses are serious; they do not carry the attribution weight Plaintiffs assign them.

Plaintiffs argue each Defendant "represented" the altered documents by identifying Bates range D002379–D005171, in Rule 26(g)-certified responses, as their merits support — responses that, until today, remained unamended. (Doc. 145 at 18 & n.11.) Defendants do not brush this aside. Three points define what the responses can and cannot prove.

*First*, the Rule 26(g) certification is the *signing attorney's*, made after reasonable inquiry, Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(g)(1) — and the signing attorneys are the same former counsel whom Plaintiffs' own Exhibit F establishes made no alterations. The certifications show counsel identified client-furnished agreements as responsive. They do not show that any individual Defendant reviewed the production set, compared it against the docket exhibits, and knowingly vouched for altered copies.

*Second*, the concealment theory must contend with an awkward fact: Defendants themselves had filed the authentic agreements with this Court, under oath, seven months earlier, and litigated the motion to dismiss on them through the Court's ruling. A party scheming to substitute fabricated terms would be working against its own sworn public filing — exposed by exactly the comparison Plaintiffs performed. Defendants anticipate the reply that this cuts the other way as to Ms. Begovic: that her declaration proves she *knew* the authentic terms when the contradictory responses issued. It proves knowledge of the authentic terms; it does not prove knowledge of the *altered production set*. Nothing in the record shows that she — or anyone — compared Bates range D002379–D005171 against the docket exhibits before Plaintiffs did. Knowing what the true contracts say is not knowing that someone has altered copies of them.

*Third*, the failure to amend was a real deficiency. As of this filing it is cured: amended responses identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing the Bates range are being served contemporaneously, and the withdrawal notice claws back the altered documents. A belatedly cured failure to supplement is remediable through Rule 37(c)'s calibrated toolkit — exclusion, fees, "other appropriate sanctions" — not through default judgment on RICO claims.

#### C. The counsel withdrawals and Defendants' silence.

Plaintiffs feature the two counsel withdrawals — the first citing "ethical concerns" and an inability to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations" (Doc. 107 ¶ 7; Doc. 117 at 4) — as confirmation of guilt. The withdrawals happened; the docket says what it says; Defendants do not ask the Court to ignore it. But a motion to withdraw identifies no client and no cause; the privileged reasons are not of record, and withdrawal can follow from conflicts among jointly represented clients, inability to verify facts, or prudential caution. What the withdrawals fairly show is that the September 24, 2025 letter was treated with gravity inside the defense camp, not waved off.

As for Defendants' silence: it is true that no Defendant has yet sworn a word about provenance, and Defendants accept that continued silence would eventually permit adverse inferences. The Proposed Order ends the silence on a date certain, with consequences attached — each Defendant must swear, invoke any privilege on the record, or accept an adverse inference on attribution at the hearing. Section III explains why that is the honest sequence rather than an evasion.

#### D. Attribution must be individualized — and the agency theory against FR8 changes the analysis less than Plaintiffs claim.

Every terminating-sanctions case Plaintiffs cite involved a finding or admission that the sanctioned party itself fabricated or knowingly submitted the false evidence. *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009) (court found plaintiff herself fabricated documents); *Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962, 968, 970 (E.D.N.C. 2017) (party admitted producing doctored records), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018); *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 678 (criminal plea admission); *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019); *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7; *Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, 2015 WL 12732433 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015). Plaintiffs have identified no case — and Defendants are aware of none in the materials before the Court — entering default against a corporation for fabricated discovery where the responsible human being was never identified and the corporation was never first put to an evidentiary hearing. Plaintiffs ask this Court to write that opinion. The Court need not, because both sides have already requested the hearing that makes it unnecessary.

The individualized analysis:

- **Ms. Begovic.** Her only sworn statement to this Court attached and authenticated the *authentic* agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19) — the ones Plaintiffs stipulate are correct and the Court construed in their favor. Counts I and II against her are dismissed (Doc. 135 at 15); nothing in Mr. Hoffman's declaration touches her. Plaintiffs' revised motion itself no longer seeks default against her.
- **Mr. Kajdic.** No record evidence connects him to Adobe Acrobat, to "david," or to the February 17 save. The responses served over his name were certified by counsel whom Exhibit F clears. The case against him is, at present, an inference from corporate office — and Plaintiffs themselves now ask only for a hearing as to him.
- **FR8.** Defendants accept the general principle that a corporation acts through agents and may answer for an agent's conduct within the scope of its document-handling processes. But the agency theory has an antecedent factual layer — who "david" is, in what capacity that person acted, and whether the act occurred within any scope FR8 conferred — and those are the very facts the record does not contain and the neutral examination and hearing will supply. More fundamentally, agency at most addresses *Telectron*'s first element as to FR8. Elements two and three remain: the prejudice analysis (Section IV) and the lesser-sanctions analysis (Section V) are unaffected by *how* bad faith would be imputed. Even if the Court were inclined to treat FR8's responsibility as established by custody alone, default would still founder on the third element, because the relief in the Proposed Order — performed and ordered remediation, neutral examination at FR8's expense, fee exposure, trial admissibility of the alteration evidence, and reserved escalation up to default — serves punishment and deterrence without ending 22 plaintiffs' claims by fiat. And FR8 does not stand on attribution to avoid responsibility for its files: regardless of who "david" proves to be, FR8 has volunteered to bear the full cost of finding out, to pay Plaintiffs' expert costs, and to accept the evidentiary consequences at trial.

### III. Candor to the Court: Why the Declarations Are Tendered by Proposed Order Rather Than Attached, and What Investigation Has and Has Not Occurred.

Defendants anticipate two questions and answer them directly rather than waiting to be asked.

**Why are the declarations not attached to this Response?** Declarations require a pen, not an order — Defendants do not pretend otherwise. They are not attached because present counsel, who appeared in November 2025 into a record corrupted before their appearance, will not tender sworn provenance accounts they have not yet been able to verify against the very systems whose integrity is in question. A denial sworn *before* the neutral forensic examination images those systems would be worth little to the Court the day it was filed and potentially less the day the examiner reports; declarations that follow the imaging, and that the examination can corroborate or contradict, are worth something. Defendants are not asking to delay the oath — the Proposed Order makes the declarations due on a fixed date *before* the examiner's report and the hearing, and attaches self-executing consequences: any Defendant who neither swears nor invokes a privilege on the record by the deadline consents that the Court may draw adverse inferences on attribution against that Defendant at the hearing. No Defendant is hiding behind counsel. Each will answer under oath, or state on the record why not, by a date certain — and if any Defendant has a Fifth Amendment problem, the Proposed Order forces that disclosure now rather than on the eve of trial. Defendants respectfully submit that a dated, contempt-backed obligation to swear, with adverse inferences for silence, gives the Court strictly more than a hastily drafted denial would.

**What investigation has FR8 conducted since September 24, 2025?** The record describes none, and Defendants will not dress up the interval as diligence. The candid account is the docket's: the letter was followed within days by counsel's withdrawal, then by a second withdrawal, a court-ordered conferral period, the appearance of present counsel in November 2025, and a stay through January 2026. That explains the period; it does not justify going forward without one. But the answer to a corporation that has not investigated itself is not to default it — it is to order the investigation done *properly*, which is what FR8 now proposes against its own interest in opacity: a neutral examiner, not FR8, will image and analyze the systems, at FR8's sole expense, reporting simultaneously to both sides and the Court. FR8 should not be its own forensic examiner, and after this record, Plaintiffs would not accept its self-examination anyway. If post-exposure dilatoriness is itself sanctionable, the calibrated remedy is fees and ordered remediation — both of which the Proposed Order delivers — not judgment on treble-damages claims. And the conduct condemned in *Roche Diagnostics* — forgeries "never . . . corrected, explained, or even acknowledged," 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 — is, as of this filing, no longer this case: the alteration is acknowledged as a fact of record, the documents are withdrawn, the responses are amended, and the explanation is being compelled by a mechanism Defendants ask the Court to order against them.

### IV. Plaintiffs Have Not Shown Prejudice That the Performed and Proposed Measures Leave Uncured.

The alteration deprived Plaintiffs of nothing on the merits. They signed the authentic agreements, kept them, and stipulate the copies Defendants filed in July 2024 are true. (Doc. 145 at 4.) The interpretation dispute the altered term targeted was decided for Plaintiffs, on the authentic documents. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10.) The altered documents were never filed (except with the sanctions motion), never used in any deposition or hearing, and never influenced any ruling — and Defendants are now bound, by stipulation made in this filing, to Doc. 135's construction through trial, so the ruling's pleading-stage posture no longer limits its effect. *See Gulf Atl. Constructors, Inc. v. Webb, LLC*, 796 F. Supp. 3d 1011, 1018 (N.D. Fla. 2025); *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009) — cited not for the proposition that unfiled fabrications are unsanctionable (*Forsberg* forecloses that, and Defendants do not argue it), but for what they bear on: the magnitude of prejudice and proportionality of remedy.

The forward-looking prejudice Plaintiffs identify is met item by item. *Garcia* teaches that fabrication prejudices an opponent by casting doubt on the whole production, forcing costly corroboration. 569 F.3d at 1180. The cures, in kind: underlying source records for the Defendant-prepared Excel damages summaries will be produced in native format so no element of Plaintiffs' damages case rests on Defendant-prepared compilations (Doc. 145 at 20; Proposed Order ¶ 6); a neutral examiner will test the integrity of the production systems at Defendants' expense (¶ 4); Plaintiffs' forensic-expert costs will be reimbursed without admission (¶ 5); and the alteration evidence goes to the jury by stipulation. Investigation costs are cured by payment; delay is substantially attributable to counsel transitions and a stipulated stay, and is not remediable by default in any event; generalized distrust is addressed by verification — not by terminating 22 plaintiffs' claims without proof of breach, causation, or damages.

### V. Plaintiffs' Requested Relief Is Disproportionate at Every Tier, and the Remediation Package Is Not "Too Little, Too Late."

**Default.** A default would enter judgment on RICO claims carrying treble damages and fees, FDUTPA claims, and Truth in Leasing Act claims, for 22 plaintiffs in a putative class action (Doc. 22 ¶¶ 87-144) — against a record that does not identify who altered the documents. Whatever sanction the facts ultimately support after the hearing, entering that judgment first and finding the facts never is not it.

**The Telectron third element.** Plaintiffs will say the remediation package is post-exposure, contingent, and merely promises compliance with existing duties — the *Franklin Livestock* sequence. Four answers. *First*, it is no longer contingent: the amended responses and withdrawal are served, and the Doc. 135 and trial-admissibility stipulations are made, with this filing; the remainder is tendered for *ordering*, with contempt consequences and self-executing inferences — Defendants are asking to be bound, not asking for credit. *Second*, the package exceeds any existing duty: no rule obligated Defendants to fund a neutral examination of their own systems, pay Plaintiffs' expert costs, stipulate the alteration evidence to the jury, or consent to be bound by a pleading-stage construction through trial. *Third*, *Franklin Livestock* does not control: there, the offending party *admitted* creating the false records, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 968, 970 — culpability was established and only contrition was left to weigh. Here culpability is the open question, and remediation is offered not as penance but as proof that lesser sanctions are available and adequate while attribution is resolved on evidence. *Fourth*, the package does not reset the fabricator "to the status quo ante at no cost": Defendants bear the examination costs, pay Plaintiffs' expert, lose the documents, concede the interpretation, hand the alteration evidence to the jury, and remain exposed to escalation up to default if the examination or hearing implicates any of them. That is a structured path to findings with consequences attached — the opposite of moral hazard.

**Damages-phase relief, examiner, and fees.** Plaintiffs' rebuilt alternative relief (Doc. 145-era § V) largely converges with what Defendants volunteer: the Doc. 135-binding order (now stipulated), source-record production (Proposed Order ¶ 6), a neutral examiner (¶ 4 — the proportionate version of the special-master request, *see Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999)), and trial admissibility (now stipulated). Two items should be denied: a blanket *presumption* against the accuracy of Defendant-prepared materials, which presupposes the bad-faith finding not yet made and is mooted by source-record production; and any inherent-power fee award beyond expert costs before the hearing, which requires a bad-faith finding, *Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021) — the Proposed Order reserves the broader fee question for post-hearing findings, where it belongs. Spoliation-based inferences fit poorly here for the independent reason that Plaintiffs face no evidentiary gap: they hold the authentic agreements, which are on the docket and stipulated authentic by both sides. *See Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006); *Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009).

### VI. The Court Should Enter the Proposed Order.

The dispositive questions — who altered the documents, at whose direction, with which Defendant's knowledge — are questions of identity, intent, and credibility that no paper record can resolve, and both sides now agree on the vehicle. The attached Proposed Order (Appendix A) supplies it in executable form: amended responses (confirmed served), sworn declarations on a date certain with adverse-inference consequences for silence, a neutral forensic examination at Defendants' expense on a fixed schedule, provenance depositions that do not count against Plaintiffs' merits limits, expert-cost reimbursement, source-record production, the two stipulations confirmed as orders, an evidentiary hearing at which each Defendant will appear and testify, defendant-by-defendant findings, and every tier of sanction — expressly including default — reserved and available on the record the process builds. If that record establishes any Defendant's culpability, the Court will have both the findings and the full arsenal, and Defendants will have no complaint about process. What the Court should not do is enter judgment first and find facts never.

## CONCLUSION

The forensic record establishes that documents in Defendants' production were altered; it does not establish by whom, at whose direction, or with which Defendant's knowledge. Defendants meet that record with performance, not promises: amended responses and withdrawal served with this filing; stipulations binding them to this Court's contract ruling and putting the alteration evidence before the jury; and a Proposed Order placing declarations, a neutral examination, cost-shifting, and an evidentiary hearing on dated, self-executing terms. Defendants respectfully request that the Court deny the requested default judgment, presumptions, special master, and premature fee award, and enter the Proposed Order at Appendix A.
