### INTRODUCTION

This is not a fraud-on-the-court motion, and Plaintiffs do not invoke the "defile the court itself" standard of *S.E.C. v. ESM Group, Inc.*, 835 F.2d 270, 273 (11th Cir. 1988). The motion rests on inherent power, spoliation, and the discovery rules — authorities that reach the doctoring of evidence in discovery without any requirement that it first be filed with or relied upon by the Court. *Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015) (unpublished). Defendants now concede both points. (Resp. § I.)

The parties' briefing has therefore converged. Both sides accept that the predicate misconduct must be shown by clear and convincing evidence; both accept the three-element test of *Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987), before any terminating sanction; both request an evidentiary hearing preceded by provenance discovery; and Defendants concede that Plaintiffs' forensic showing "describes serious misconduct by *someone*." (Resp. at Intro.) Three questions remain genuinely disputed, and this Motion is confined to them:

1. **Corporate attribution.** Whether FR8 — whose business records were forged, within whose document-collection process the forgery occurred, and whose certified discovery responses designated the forgeries as its merits evidence and remain unamended — may be sanctioned at the terminating level without Plaintiffs first naming the human being who operated the keyboard. (§ III.A, infra.)
2. **The remediation package.** Whether seven commitments volunteered fourteen months after the false responses were served, nearly nine months after exposure, and only with default on the table can satisfy *Telectron*'s third element. They cannot; the Court should *order* the package as a floor, not accept it as a substitute. (§ IV.C, infra.)
3. **Silence.** What inferences flow from the fact that, nearly nine months after Plaintiffs' September 24, 2025 letter, no Defendant has offered a single sworn word about the provenance of its own production — and that Defendants' Response, while *promising* declarations on a future court-ordered schedule, attaches none. (§§ II.B, IV.C, infra.)

Plaintiffs accordingly request: (1) an order converting Defendants' seven remediation commitments into court-ordered obligations on fixed dates; (2) the evidentiary hearing both sides seek, preceded by expedited provenance discovery; (3) default judgment against FR8 upon the conclusion of that hearing — or now, should the Court find the existing record sufficient — because the facts material to FR8's responsibility are already undisputed; (4) sanctions against Kajdic, up to and including default, calibrated to the hearing's findings; (5) tailored relief as to Begovic; and (6) damages-phase safeguards, fees, and costs.

### ARGUMENT

#### I. GOVERNING STANDARDS

Federal courts possess inherent power "to protect the orderly administration of justice and to preserve the dignity of the tribunal," *Kleiner v. First Nat'l Bank of Atlanta*, 751 F.2d 1193, 1209 (11th Cir. 1985) (citing *Roadway Express v. Piper*, 447 U.S. 752, 764-65 (1980)), including "the ability to fashion an appropriate sanction for conduct which abuses the judicial process," *VIP Auto Glass, Inc. v. GEICO Gen. Ins. Co.*, No. 16-CV-2012, 2018 WL 3649638, at *3 (M.D. Fla. Jan. 3, 2018) (quoting *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45). The power is "most often invoked where a party commits perjury or **destroys or doctors evidence**." *Access Innovators, LLC v. Usha Martin Ltd.*, No. 09-CV-2893, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1 (N.D. Ga. Apr. 28, 2010) (quoting *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1269 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (emphasis added). The bad-faith predicate is disjunctive: it exists where "a fraud has been practiced upon [the court], **or** . . . where a party or attorney knowingly or recklessly . . . **delays or disrupts the litigation**." *Qantum*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1268-69 (cleaned up; emphasis added). Spoliation doctrine independently reaches "alteration[] or concealment of evidence," *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7, with available sanctions including default judgment, adverse inferences, and fees, *Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009). And Rule 37(c)(1) authorizes sanctions for failure to supplement responses to requests for production, *see Dragon Jade Int'l, Ltd. v. Ultroid, LLC*, No. 17-CV-2422, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 12, 2019); Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(e) — and whatever the precise fit of Rule 26(e) to a "we will produce Bates range X" response, the inherent power independently reaches the knowing maintenance of false certified responses. Courts impose case-ending sanctions for fabrications produced in discovery and never used in court. *E.g.*, *Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962, 971 (E.D.N.C. 2017), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018); *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009); *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, No. 18-CV-01479, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7, *11 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020); *Art Remedy, LLC v. Lana Moes Art, LLC*, No. 18-61912-CIV, 2019 WL 13063496, at *4 (S.D. Fla. Sept. 26, 2019) (collecting cases); *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679.

**Standard of proof.** Plaintiffs accept that the predicate misconduct must be "demonstrated by clear and convincing evidence." *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. Plaintiffs state candidly that the authorities of record on this Motion contain no controlling Eleventh Circuit articulation of the proof standard for inherent-power bad-faith findings; rather than overclaim, Plaintiffs accept the clear-and-convincing standard — the most demanding plausibly applicable — and meet it. Clear and convincing proof may rest on circumstantial evidence, particularly where every fact bearing on the disputed question lies in the accused party's exclusive possession.

**Process.** Defendants invoke due process, and Plaintiffs agree it requires notice and an opportunity to be heard before inherent-power sanctions issue. This Motion supplies both: Plaintiffs join Defendants' request for an evidentiary hearing, ask that it be preceded by the provenance discovery described in Section V.A, and seek individualized, defendant-by-defendant findings. The disagreement is not over process; it is over what the undisputed record already establishes as to FR8 (§ III.A) and what the hearing must resolve as to the individuals (§§ III.B-C).

**The residue of the fraud-on-the-court framing.** To the extent any of Defendants' original authorities retain force, two distinctions dispose of them: in *Idearc Media Corp. v. Kimsey & Assocs., P.A.*, the offending exhibits were "withdrawn well before a finding of fact," 2009 WL 928556, at *5 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 31, 2009), whereas Defendants' certified responses identifying the forgeries as their merits evidence remain operative today; and *Bijou v. Kidd* reserves terminating sanctions for "explicit and intentional misconduct" as opposed to "inconsistencies, poor recollection, . . . or even outright lies," 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026) — and a single counterfeit compensation page digitally grafted into twenty-one signed contracts is the former, not the latter.

#### II. THE FACT OF DELIBERATE FABRICATION WITHIN DEFENDANTS' EXCLUSIVE CUSTODY IS ESTABLISHED BY CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE.

##### A. Means, motive, and timing.

**Means**: the alteration required scanning authentic contracts, substituting typed compensation pages for handwritten ones, and replicating the same fabricated page — identical smudge and all — across twenty-one agreements, with Adobe manipulation that incidentally hid scanned hairs and left blurring artifacts. (Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15.) No innocent records-management process inserts a single counterfeit page into twenty-one different signed contracts. **Motive**: the forged term tracks Defendants' litigation position verbatim in substance (Doc. 52 at 3-4); if the authentic agreements supported that position, there would have been no reason to alter them. **Timing**: the save date of February 17, 2025 falls after the motion to dismiss put the compensation term squarely before this Court, while that motion was pending, and nine days before Kajdic and Begovic served certified responses identifying the altered set as their merits evidence. (Ex. E ¶ 15; Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) Fabricating and tendering knowingly false evidence is willful, bad-faith conduct. *Am. Rena Int'l Corp. v. Sis-Joyce Int'l Co.*, No. CV 12-6972, 2015 WL 12732433, at *23 (C.D. Cal. Dec. 14, 2015); *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1177-82; *Franklin Livestock*, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 971.

##### B. Exclusive custody, concealment, and the inference from silence.

Whoever performed the alteration did so before the documents reached counsel: the production was delivered "in the form [it was] provided by Defendants," and counsel "made no alterations . . . besides Bates stamping and making certain redactions." (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.) Plaintiffs do not ask the Court to adopt a formal burden-shifting rule. They ask the Court to treat exclusive custody as what it is — powerful circumstantial evidence — and to recognize a *permissive inference* available on this record: when forged documents emerge from a party's own files and own collection process; when every fact bearing on provenance (who scanned, who assembled, who had access in February 2025, who "david" is) lies uniquely within that party's knowledge; and when that party elects, for nearly nine months, to offer no sworn explanation, identify no one, and describe no investigation, the Court may infer that the explanation, if given, would not be innocent. That inference is reinforced by spoliation doctrine, which condemns "concealment" no less than alteration, *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 — and concealment is on this record twice over: the authentic agreements were absent from every produced personnel folder and identified in no discovery response, so that the substitution was undetectable from the production itself. The record also contains the contemporaneous reaction of the professionals closest to the documents: after "a reasonable review and conferral addressing the issues raised in the [September 24 letter]," defense counsel cited "ethical concerns," declared themselves unable to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations," and withdrew — followed within weeks by their successors. (Doc. 107 ¶ 7, at 3, 6; Doc. 117 at 4.) Plaintiffs acknowledge that withdrawals identify no client and may have privileged explanations; they are corroboration, not proof. But Defendants' own Response concedes that "continued silence would eventually permit adverse inferences" (Resp. § II.C). "Eventually" arrived some months ago.

#### III. ATTRIBUTION, DEFENDANT BY DEFENDANT.

Defendants' central argument is that the forensic record "identifies a username, not a Defendant." That argument has real force as to the natural persons, and Plaintiffs' requests as to Kajdic and Begovic honor it. It has no force as to FR8.

##### A. FR8: the facts material to corporate responsibility are undisputed, and they establish FR8's bad faith twice over.

A corporation acts only through natural persons. Its discovery responses are its own acts; its production is its own production; and Rule 26(g)'s "reasonable inquiry" architecture presupposes that the responding *party* answers for the integrity of the collection process behind its responses. Two independent paths establish FR8's bad faith, and neither requires naming "david."

*First, the corruption of FR8's own production.* The altered documents are FR8's business records (Doc. 16-2 ¶ 19), maintained in FR8's personnel files, altered on February 17, 2025 within the document-collection process that FR8 conducted, and furnished by Defendants to counsel as FR8's response to discovery served on FR8. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5; Ex. C at 2 ¶ 3, 8 ¶ 29.) Whether "david" is an officer, an employee, or — as Defendants hypothesize — an outside vendor "involved in scanning or processing" (Resp. § II.D), the answer is the same: a vendor processing FR8's records for FR8's production at FR8's direction is FR8's agent for precisely this purpose, and FR8 chose its agents. The hypothetical relocates the wrongdoer within FR8's organization; it does not move him outside FR8's responsibility. *Roche Diagnostics* — Plaintiffs' lead authority and a case in which the defaulted party was a corporate defendant — states the operative test in production-state terms, not actor-identification terms: a "document production [that] contains numerous demonstrable forgeries that have never been corrected, explained, or even acknowledged" is "alone . . . more than enough to require case-ending sanctions." 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. That description fits FR8's production verbatim.

*Second, FR8's own corporate conduct after exposure — conduct that requires no attribution at all.* Since September 24, 2025, FR8 has: maintained certified discovery responses it knows identify forged documents as its merits evidence, in derogation of its continuing Rule 26(e) duty; described no internal investigation into how counterfeit versions of twenty-one contracts came to sit in its own personnel files; identified no person who used the username "david" on its systems; and submitted no sworn account from any officer or custodian. Those are not the acts of "david," whoever he is. They are the institutional choices of FR8 itself, made over months and through three successive sets of counsel — and bad faith under *Qantum* expressly includes conduct that "delays or disrupts the litigation," 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1268-69, while spoliation expressly includes "concealment," *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7. A corporation that responds to forensic proof of forgery inside its own production with nine months of institutional silence has adopted the forgery as its own.

*The candid answer on remedy and sequence.* Plaintiffs will not overclaim: no authority in the record of this Motion entered default against a corporation for fabricated discovery where the responsible human was never identified *and* no evidentiary hearing was first held. Plaintiffs therefore do not ask this Court to write that opinion. They ask the Court to take the hearing both sides have requested — and to recognize what the hearing is for. As to FR8, the facts material to responsibility are already undisputed: the documents are its records; the alteration occurred in its custody and collection process; its certified responses designated the forgeries as its merits evidence; those responses remain unamended; and no investigation has been described. None of that will change at a hearing, because Defendants do not dispute any of it — their Response disputes only the identity and knowledge of individuals. What the hearing can supply is the identity of "david" and findings on the individual Defendants' knowledge — facts that matter to *who shares* FR8's responsibility and to calibration against the natural persons, not to whether FR8 bears it. Accordingly, Plaintiffs request that the Court enter default judgment against FR8 at the conclusion of the evidentiary hearing — or earlier, if the Court concludes the existing record suffices — and that the Court make clear now that a hearing at which FR8 again offers no sworn provenance account, or at which its systems prove incapable of identifying its own user, will resolve attribution against FR8, not in its favor. A party cannot manufacture an attribution mystery inside its own walls and then claim the mystery as a shield.

##### B. Kajdic: hearing-calibrated sanctions, with default on the table.

Kajdic is FR8's CEO. (Doc. 151 at 1.) His certified response — served nine days after the forensic alteration date — identified the altered range, and only that range, as the support for his merits contention, and remains unamended. (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) Defendants respond that the Rule 26(g) certification was counsel's, and that counsel's signature cannot establish the client's knowledge. (Resp. § II.B.) That answers a charge Plaintiffs do not press: the response is *Kajdic's* response, made after counsel's "reasonable inquiry" of the client, on facts that — per Exhibit F — came from the clients; and the duty to correct it under Rule 26(e) has been his, personally and continuously, ever since. Plaintiffs do not ask the Court to find today that Kajdic altered or directed the alteration. They ask that he be examined at the hearing, that he serve an amended response and a sworn provenance accounting now, and that sanctions against him — up to and including default — be calibrated to the findings made there.

##### C. Begovic: tailored relief now; anything further only on hearing findings.

Plaintiffs acknowledge the facts that favor Begovic: her July 2024 declaration attached the authentic agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19), and the Court has dismissed Counts I and II against her (Doc. 135 at 15). Plaintiffs do not seek default against her. But her declaration cuts both ways: it establishes that Begovic — FR8's CFO and the custodian who authenticated its contract records under oath — knew exactly what the authentic compensation terms said when her own certified response identified a contradictory set as her merits evidence, and she has neither amended that response nor offered a sworn word in the fifteen-plus months since. At minimum, maintaining the response was reckless in light of her personal knowledge and her personal Rule 26(e) duty. The relief sought against her — an amended response, a sworn provenance accounting, and examination at the hearing — attaches to her conduct as declarant and discovery respondent and is available regardless of the final scope of the claims against her; any fee exposure on her part should be determined with the general fee award on the hearing's findings.

Finally, Defendants' observation that every terminating-sanctions case in the record involved a finding or admission of the sanctioned party's own culpability (Resp. § II.D) proves Plaintiffs' sequencing, not Defendants' immunity: those findings emerged from developed records, hearings, and inferences from the sanctioned party's conduct — exactly the process Plaintiffs invoke here.

#### IV. AS TO FR8, ALL THREE *TELECTRON* ELEMENTS ARE SATISFIED.

Before a case-terminating sanction, the Court must find (1) willfulness or bad faith, (2) prejudice, and (3) that lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron*, 116 F.R.D. at 131.

##### A. Willfulness and bad faith.

Sections II and III.A establish, by clear and convincing evidence, that FR8's contract records were deliberately forged within its custody to mirror its litigation position, certified as its merits evidence, never corrected, and answered with institutional silence. The misconduct is "closely intertwined with the merits," which "militates heavily in favor of the severe sanction of default." *Qantum*, 473 F. Supp. 2d at 1269. FR8 cannot now disclaim the documents' merits relevance: its own certified responses designated them — and only them — as the documents supporting its merits contention. (Ex. C at 2 ¶ 3, 8 ¶ 29.)[^3]

[^3]: Nor can Defendants fault Plaintiffs for not first deposing them. Plaintiffs are presumptively entitled to one seven-hour deposition per party, Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(a)(2)(A)(ii), 30(d)(1); discovery (including Defendants' text messages) remains incomplete; and Plaintiffs should not be forced to spend their only deposition of each key witness prematurely — which is why Section V.A requests provenance depositions that do not count against merits limits.

##### B. Prejudice: Doc. 135 did not cure it.

Defendants argue Plaintiffs suffered no prejudice because they always held the authentic agreements and the Court has construed them in Plaintiffs' favor. That mistakes the narrowest slice of the harm for the whole, and grades an attempted fraud by its failure. Cognizable prejudice from falsified evidence has never required that the falsification succeed: it includes "consum[ing] substantial resources to respond to and 'undo' the victimizer's lies and distortions," *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 680 (cleaned up), and the corrosion of the entire production — "falsified evidence substantially prejudices an opposing party by casting doubt on the veracity of all of the culpable party's submissions," forcing independent corroboration "at substantial expense of time and money," *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1180. Concretely: (1) Plaintiffs' counsel expended substantial time and retained a paid forensic expert to detect and prove the fabrication; (2) the fabrication's aftermath — serial counsel withdrawals, a protective-order motion suspending production, vacated deadlines, and a stay — derailed the schedule (Docs. 103-133); (3) the prejudice now lives in the damages phase: given the production's volume, Plaintiffs had agreed to accept *Defendant-prepared* Excel summaries as the basis for computing 22 Plaintiffs' damages, and a party whose production is forensically proven to contain forgeries cannot ask its adversary or this Court to take its self-prepared summaries on faith, *see White Cap., L.P. v. Heyden Enters., LLC*, No. 23-14248, 2025 WL 414721, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Feb. 6, 2025) ("The parties — and the Court — cannot trust that [Defendants] have produced, or will ever produce, all discoverable evidence." (quoting *Bernal v. All. Am. Inv. Realty, Inc.*, 479 F. Supp. 2d 1291, 1339 (S.D. Fla. 2007)) (cleaned up)); and (4) the concealment was complete from the production itself — had Plaintiffs not kept and compared their own copies, the substitution was undetectable. Doc. 135, moreover, is a pleading-stage ruling Defendants have never been bound by beyond the pleadings; their Response now consents to be bound through trial (Resp. § III, item 7), and the Court should enter that consent as an order. Finally, the "no harm" theory proves too much: on it, any fabrication caught before merits use is costless — the precise logic *Forsberg* rejected as "strange." 634 F. App'x at 679.

##### C. Lesser sanctions — and Defendants' remediation package — would not serve punishment and deterrence. The package should be ordered as a floor, not accepted as a substitute.

Defendants' seven-item package (Resp. § III) is the centerpiece of their third-element answer. It fails for four reasons, and then a fifth that disposes of the argument entirely.

**It is too late to negate bad faith.** The package arrived fourteen months after the false certified responses were served, nearly nine months after exposure, and only with a default motion pending. In *Franklin Livestock*, dismissal was affirmed though the offending party *self-disclosed* the doctored records, disavowed them, and produced the authentic versions — because "the creation of false records that are passed off as originals . . . cannot be glossed over as a 'mistake.'" 251 F. Supp. 3d at 968, 970. Post-exposure remediation may mitigate going forward; it does not erase the antecedent bad faith, and Defendants' package is weaker than the corrective conduct held insufficient there.

**Most of it is already obligatory.** Amended responses are not a concession; Rule 26(e)'s duty has been self-executing for fifteen months. Source records underlying damages summaries are ordinary discoverable material. Sworn declarations "require a pen, not a court order" — nothing has ever prevented any Defendant from signing one, and the Response's promise of future oaths, with none attached, is itself evidence: a party that could truthfully deny would have denied by now. A promise to begin complying with existing duties, on a court-ordered schedule, is not a sanction.

**It admits nothing and identifies no one.** The expert-cost payment is expressly "without admission of wrongdoing"; no commitment names "david," acknowledges responsibility, or describes what FR8 has done — over nine months — to investigate the corruption of its own files. A remediation that leaves the wrongdoer unidentified and unacknowledged "addresses deterrence not at all." *Cf. Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (forgeries "never . . . corrected, explained, or even acknowledged").

**Its genuinely "extra" items cost FR8 nothing of value.** Consent to be bound by Doc. 135 surrenders an interpretation the Court has already adopted; trial admissibility of the alteration evidence concedes what Plaintiffs would seek in limine anyway; the neutral examination of FR8's own systems is the investigation FR8 owed in October 2025; expert-cost payment is restitution. Cure is not punishment.

**Therefore: order it as a floor.** Plaintiffs do not ask the Court to refuse the package; they ask the Court to *order* every item of it, on fixed dates, so that the commitments carry contempt consequences rather than goodwill — and to treat it as the floor beneath sanctions, not the ceiling above them. That sequencing is what *Telectron*'s third element actually demands. Suppose the Court orders the full package, holds the hearing, and the neutral examination still fails to identify "david." On that record, punishment and deterrence remain wholly unserved: FR8 would stand exactly where it would have stood had it never fabricated anything — its duties performed late, its costs reimbursed as restitution, its forgery withdrawn, its already-lost interpretation conceded. A wrongdoer left no worse off than honest compliance would have left it has been deterred from nothing; "litigants could infer that they have everything to gain, and nothing to lose by manufacturing evidence," *Carroll-Harris v. Wilkie*, No. 17-CV-11711, 2019 WL 2205851, at *8 (E.D. Mich. May 22, 2019) (internal quotations omitted), and fees alone would not "sufficiently address [Plaintiffs'] concern about the veracity of other documents disclosed," *id.* at *7. And a failure of FR8's own systems and personnel to identify FR8's own user — after FR8 chose not to investigate while the evidence aged for nine months — cannot inure to FR8's benefit; that is the concealment principle of spoliation doctrine applied to its natural conclusion. *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7. On that hypothetical record, *Telectron*'s third element is satisfied as to FR8, and default — the sanction courts impose when "a party fabricates a document or provides false evidence relating to a key issue in a case," *Access Innovators*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *3; *see VIP Auto Glass*, 2018 WL 3649638, at *4-5; *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 — is the proportionate one: the forged documents were the operative contracts of twenty-one class representatives, designated as merits evidence and never corrected. Plaintiffs recognize default's gravity across RICO, FDUTPA, Truth in Leasing Act, and contract claims; that gravity is commensurate with the misconduct.

#### V. RELIEF STRUCTURE.

##### A. Ordered remediation, provenance discovery, and the evidentiary hearing.

The Court should (1) **order Defendants' seven commitments** (Resp. § III) as mandatory obligations: amended Rule 26 responses within 14 days identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing Bates D002379-D005171, with a provenance accounting; withdrawal/clawback with a stipulation of trial admissibility of the alteration evidence; sworn declarations within 14 days from each Defendant and each person involved in assembling, scanning, or transmitting the production, addressing role, knowledge, the identity of every known user of "david," and chain of custody; a neutral forensic examination of the relevant systems at Defendants' expense, with simultaneous reporting; payment of Plaintiffs' forensic-expert costs; production of all source records underlying any Defendant-prepared summary, in native format with metadata; and entry of Defendants' consent to be bound by Doc. 135 for all purposes, including trial. The Court should further order (2) **forensic preservation and imaging** of the systems used in the February 2025 collection, and (3) **provenance depositions** — including of "david" once identified and of each Defendant on provenance topics only — not counted against Plaintiffs' merits-deposition entitlements (Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(a)(2)(A)(ii), 30(d)(1)); and (4) **set the evidentiary hearing** after that discovery completes, at which the Court can make individualized findings — and draw the permissive inferences the law allows from any continued silence by parties uniquely positioned to explain their own files.

##### B. Damages-phase safeguards.

Because the forward-looking prejudice lives in the damages phase, the Court should additionally impose: a **rebuttable presumption against the accuracy of any Defendant-prepared summary or compilation** unless verified against source documents, with verification costs shifted to Defendants; and audit authority in the neutral examiner over the integrity of Defendants' remaining production, including text messages and damages materials — a more economical alternative to the Rule 53 special master Plaintiffs request only in the further alternative. *See Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, No. 06-20624-CIV, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999).

##### C. Fees and costs.

The Court should award the fees and costs of uncovering the fabrication and pursuing sanctions under its inherent power, *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45, spoliation doctrine, *Swofford*, 671 F. Supp. 2d at 1280, and Rule 37(c)(1), *Dragon Jade*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4. Unlike *Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021), where neither fraud nor bad faith was proven, the bad-faith predicate here rests on forensic evidence Defendants have not answered with a single sworn word — and Defendants' own offer to pay expert costs concedes the core of the award.
