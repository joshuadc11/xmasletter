## ORDER ON PLAINTIFFS' MOTION FOR SANCTIONS (DOC. 145)

### I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiffs move for sanctions — up to and including default judgment — on forensic evidence that twenty-one Independent Contractor Agreements produced by Defendants in discovery were digitally altered before production, substituting a typed compensation term matching Defendants' litigation position for the authentic, partly handwritten term the Court has since construed in Plaintiffs' favor. (Doc. 145; Doc. 135.) Defendants respond that the forensic record establishes serious misconduct by *someone* but does not identify which Defendant, if any, is responsible; they tender a remediation package, partly performed with their Response and partly embodied in a proposed order, and join Plaintiffs' request for an evidentiary hearing preceded by provenance discovery.

The briefing has narrowed the dispute considerably, and the Court commends both sides for it. The parties agree on the governing framework (inherent power, spoliation doctrine, and Rules 26(e), 26(g), and 37(c)(1)); on the clear-and-convincing standard of proof; on the three-element test of *Telectron, Inc. v. Overhead Door Corp.*, 116 F.R.D. 107, 131 (S.D. Fla. 1987), before any terminating sanction; and on the procedural vehicle — a hearing preceded by provenance discovery. Three questions remain: whether FR8 may be defaulted on the present paper record; what effect Defendants' remediation has on *Telectron*'s third element; and what inferences flow from Defendants' silence.

For the reasons below, the Motion is **GRANTED IN PART, DENIED IN PART WITHOUT PREJUDICE, and DEFERRED IN PART**. The Court finds, by clear and convincing evidence, that the produced agreements were deliberately fabricated while in Defendants' custody, and that all three Defendants violated their duty to supplement under Rule 26(e). The Court orders Defendants' remediation package — as a floor beneath sanctions, not a substitute for them — together with provenance discovery and an evidentiary hearing. The request for default judgment is denied without prejudice as premature as to FR8 and Kajdic, and denied as to Begovic, against whom it is not sought.

### II. BACKGROUND

The material chronology is functionally undisputed; the parties' competing tables differ in emphasis, not content. The Court adopts the following findings from the paper record:

In July 2024, Defendants themselves filed the authentic agreements with this Court, attached to Begovic's sworn declaration identifying them as "true and accurate copies" of FR8's business records. (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19; Docs. 16-3–16-23.) Plaintiffs stipulate to their authenticity. The agreements provide that Plaintiffs are paid "88% of linehaul rate" or minor variants, several with handwritten compensation terms. In October 2024, Defendants moved to dismiss the amended complaint, contesting the meaning of that compensation term. (Doc. 52 at 2-4.)

On February 17, 2025 — while that motion was pending — the Dogic agreement was modified in Adobe Acrobat, pages added, and saved as a new document. (Ex. E ¶ 15.) Nine days later, Kajdic and Begovic served certified discovery responses identifying Bates range D002379–D005171 — the altered set — as the documents supporting their merits contention. (Ex. B at 4 ¶ 18.) FR8 served a materially identical response on April 16, 2025. (Ex. C at 2 ¶ 3, 8 ¶ 29.) The hardcopy and electronic productions contained only the altered agreements; the authentic versions appeared in no produced personnel folder. Former defense counsel attests that the productions were delivered "in the form provided by Defendants" and that counsel made no alterations beyond Bates stamping and redactions. (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5.)

Plaintiffs' forensic examiner, Rich Hoffman, found: a text-based (editable) compensation page grafted into otherwise scanned agreements; modification by Adobe user "david"; opacity manipulation concealing scanned artifacts; and an identical smudge and virtually identical replacement text — "88% of negotiated rate per lane per occurrence" — on the compensation page of virtually every altered agreement. (Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15; Composite Ex. D.) His declaration is unrebutted by any sworn submission.

Plaintiffs raised the alteration by letter on September 24, 2025. Within days, defense counsel withdrew, citing "ethical concerns" and an inability to "meaningfully produce documents in light of the allegations" (Doc. 107 ¶ 7); successor counsel withdrew within two weeks (Doc. 117 at 4). Plaintiffs' first sanctions motion (Doc. 108) was denied without prejudice for conferral with new counsel; present counsel appeared in November 2025; the case was stayed for mediation through January 2026. On February 19, 2026, the Court construed the authentic agreements, held them unambiguous, rejected Defendants' reading as "absurd," and dismissed Counts I and II against Begovic. (Doc. 135 at 7, 10, 15.)

Between September 24, 2025 and the filing of the Response, no Defendant amended any certified response, submitted any sworn account of provenance, identified any user of the username "david," or described any internal investigation. With their Response, Defendants represent that they have now served amended responses identifying the authentic agreements and withdrawing the Bates range; served a formal withdrawal notice; and stipulated both to be bound by Doc. 135 through trial and to the trial admissibility of the alteration evidence. Their remaining commitments — sworn declarations, a neutral forensic examination at their expense, payment of Plaintiffs' expert costs, and source-record production — are tendered in a proposed order (Resp. App. A). No declaration from any Defendant accompanies the Response.

### III. LEGAL STANDARD

Federal courts possess inherent power "to fashion an appropriate sanction for conduct which abuses the judicial process." *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, 501 U.S. 32, 44-45 (1991); *Kleiner v. First Nat'l Bank of Atlanta*, 751 F.2d 1193, 1209 (11th Cir. 1985). That power "is most often invoked where a party commits perjury or destroys or doctors evidence," *Access Innovators, LLC v. Usha Martin Ltd.*, 2010 WL 11508119, at *1 (N.D. Ga. Apr. 28, 2010) (quoting *Qantum Commc'ns Corp. v. Star Broad., Inc.*, 473 F. Supp. 2d 1249, 1269 (S.D. Fla. 2007)), and reaches fabrications produced in discovery whether or not they were ever filed with or relied upon by the Court, *Forsberg v. Pefanis*, 634 F. App'x 676, 679 (11th Cir. 2015) (unpublished). Spoliation doctrine independently condemns the "intentional destruction, mutilation, alteration, or concealment of evidence." *Optowave Co. v. Nikitin*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 7, 2006); *Swofford v. Eslinger*, 671 F. Supp. 2d 1274, 1280 (M.D. Fla. 2009). Rule 26(e) imposes a continuing, self-executing duty to correct discovery responses, enforceable through Rule 37(c)(1). *See Dragon Jade Int'l, Ltd. v. Ultroid, LLC*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 12, 2019).

Inherent-power sanctions require a finding of bad faith by the party to be sanctioned, *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45, and both sides have embraced the clear-and-convincing standard applied in *Roche Diagnostics Corp. v. Priority Healthcare Corp.*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7 (N.D. Ala. May 8, 2020). The Court accepts that agreed standard — appropriately, given the quasi-criminal stigma and case-ending consequences of a fabrication finding. Before any terminating sanction, the Court must find (1) willfulness or bad faith, (2) prejudice, and (3) that lesser sanctions would not serve the goals of punishment and deterrence. *Telectron*, 116 F.R.D. at 131. Default remains "the most extraordinary of all remedies," reserved for "the most egregious instances of explicit and intentional misconduct." *Bijou v. Kidd*, 2026 WL 674221, at *1 (M.D. Fla. Mar. 2, 2026). Clear and convincing proof may rest on circumstantial evidence, and due process requires notice and an opportunity to be heard before such sanctions issue — a proposition both sides accept.

### IV. ANALYSIS

#### A. The fact of deliberate fabrication is established by clear and convincing evidence.

Some findings can and should be made now, because no party disputes the facts that support them and the unrebutted forensic record permits no other conclusion.

The Court **finds**, by clear and convincing evidence: (1) the twenty-one agreements within Bates range D002379–D005171 were deliberately altered — a counterfeit compensation page, bearing an identical smudge and near-identical typed text tracking Defendants' litigation position, was substituted into scanned copies of signed contracts (Ex. E ¶¶ 5, 12, 15; Composite Ex. D); (2) no innocent records-management process produces that result — the replication of a single fabricated page across twenty-one separate signed contracts, with artifacts deliberately concealed, is the signature of intentional fabrication, not error; (3) the alteration occurred before the documents reached defense counsel and while they were in Defendants' custody and within Defendants' document-collection process (Ex. F ¶¶ 3-5); and (4) the timing — a forensic save date of February 17, 2025, while the motion to dismiss contesting the very compensation term was pending, nine days before certified responses designated the altered set as merits evidence — establishes that the fabrication was undertaken for use in this litigation. Means, motive, and timing converge, and Defendants do not contest any of these facts; their Response concedes the forensic record "describes serious misconduct by *someone*." (Resp. at Intro.)

The Court further **finds** that all three Defendants violated Rule 26(e). Each maintained certified responses identifying forged documents as merits evidence from September 24, 2025 — when the fabrication was raised in writing — until the filing of the Response in this round, a period of nearly nine months. Defendants concede the failure was "a real deficiency" (Resp. § II.B), and the Court finds it was not substantially justified. The counsel transitions and mediation stay explain a portion of the interval; they do not explain why no amendment issued in the months after present counsel appeared in November 2025, and Defendants candidly do not contend otherwise. ("[E]xplanation is not cure." Resp., Chronology.)

#### B. Attribution and bad faith, defendant by defendant.

What the paper record does not establish is *who*. Mr. Hoffman supplies a tool, a date, artifacts, and a username matching none of the three Defendants' names. He does not supply an actor, a direction, or any Defendant's knowledge. That gap controls the disposition of the terminating-sanctions request.

**The burden-shift Plaintiffs disclaim, and the inference they are entitled to.** Plaintiffs prudently abandoned any formal burden-shifting rule and ask only for a *permissive inference* from exclusive custody plus prolonged, unexplained silence. The Court agrees that inference is available. When forged documents emerge from a party's own files and own collection process; when every fact bearing on provenance lies uniquely within that party's knowledge; and when that party offers no sworn explanation for many months, a factfinder may infer that the explanation, if given, would not be innocent. *Cf. Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7 (spoliation doctrine condemns "concealment"). Defendants themselves concede that "continued silence would eventually permit adverse inferences." (Resp. § II.C.) But a permissive inference is an instrument of factfinding, not a substitute for it. Defendants are correct that due process does not permit the Court to treat an available inference as an adjudicated fact and enter judgment on it — particularly where both sides have requested the hearing at which the inference can be weighed against sworn testimony.

**FR8.** Plaintiffs' strongest argument is that FR8's responsibility does not depend on naming "david": the documents are FR8's records, altered within FR8's collection process; whether "david" is an officer, employee, or vendor, he acted within a process FR8 conducted for FR8's production; and FR8's post-exposure conduct — months of unamended certified responses, no described investigation, no identification of its own system user — is institutional conduct requiring no attribution at all. There is real force here, and Defendants concede the general agency principle. (Resp. § II.D.) But Plaintiffs candidly acknowledge that no authority in the record entered default against a corporation for fabricated discovery where the responsible human was never identified *and* no evidentiary hearing was first held (Mot. § III.A) — and every terminating-sanctions case either side cites involved a finding or admission of the sanctioned party's own culpability. *Garcia v. Berkshire Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 569 F.3d 1174, 1177-82 (10th Cir. 2009); *Franklin Livestock, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, Inc.*, 251 F. Supp. 3d 962, 968 (E.D.N.C. 2017), *aff'd*, 721 F. App'x 263 (4th Cir. 2018); *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 678; *Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. The Court will not write the first such opinion when the hearing both sides request will make it unnecessary. Moreover, even on Plaintiffs' agency theory, *Telectron*'s third element cannot be resolved against FR8 until the Court sees what the ordered remediation, neutral examination, and hearing actually produce. Section IV.D addresses that sequencing.

Two points, however, should be unmistakable now. *First*, the facts material to FR8's responsibility that are already undisputed — its records, its custody, its collection process, its certified responses, its nine months of institutional silence — will not need re-proving at the hearing; the hearing will address the identity of "david," the scope of his agency, and the individual Defendants' knowledge. *Second*, FR8 cannot manufacture an attribution mystery inside its own walls and then claim the mystery as a shield. If the neutral examination fails to identify FR8's own system user because of FR8's non-cooperation, non-retention, or spoliation, or if FR8 again offers no sworn provenance account, those failures will weigh *against* FR8 on attribution and on *Telectron*'s third element — not in its favor. *Optowave*, 2006 WL 3231422, at *7; *cf. White Cap., L.P. v. Heyden Enters., LLC*, 2025 WL 414721, at *2 (S.D. Fla. Feb. 6, 2025). The self-executing consequences in the ordering paragraphs below implement that principle.

**Kajdic.** No record evidence connects Kajdic to Adobe Acrobat, to "david," or to the February 17 save. His certified response — served nine days after the alteration date, identifying only the altered range — and his personal, continuing Rule 26(e) failure are established and serious; they warrant the amended response (now served), a sworn provenance accounting, and his examination at the hearing. Whether they reflect knowledge or direction of the fabrication is precisely what the hearing must determine. All sanctions as to Kajdic, up to and including default, are reserved.

**Begovic.** Plaintiffs do not seek default against her, and the record would not support it: her only sworn submission attached the *authentic* agreements (Doc. 16-2 ¶¶ 18-19), and Counts I and II against her are dismissed (Doc. 135 at 15). But her position as FR8's CFO and the authenticating custodian of its contract records means she knew the authentic terms when her certified response designated a contradictory set as merits evidence, and she has not amended or explained it for over a year. Defendants' rejoinder — knowledge of the authentic terms is not knowledge of the altered production set — is fair as far as it goes, and is a question for the hearing. The tailored relief Plaintiffs seek against her (amended response, sworn provenance accounting, examination at the hearing) attaches to her conduct as declarant and discovery respondent and is granted; further relief awaits the hearing's findings.

#### C. Prejudice.

Defendants' "no harm" argument — Plaintiffs always held the authentic agreements, and the Court construed them in Plaintiffs' favor — succeeds only in narrowing the prejudice, not eliminating it. Merits prejudice was averted, by Plaintiffs' diligence and the fabrication's detection, not by any virtue of the fabrication. Cognizable prejudice does not require that the falsification succeed: it includes the substantial resources consumed "to respond to and undo the victimizer's lies and distortions," *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 680, and the corrosion of confidence in the entire production, forcing independent corroboration at substantial expense, *Garcia*, 569 F.3d at 1180. The Court **finds** prejudice established in those forms: Plaintiffs retained a paid forensic examiner; the litigation schedule was derailed by the fabrication's aftermath; and the integrity of the remaining production — including the Defendant-prepared damages summaries Plaintiffs had agreed to accept — can no longer be assumed. The contrary rule would mean any fabrication caught before merits use is costless — "strange" logic the Eleventh Circuit has rejected. *Forsberg*, 634 F. App'x at 679. The *degree* of residual prejudice after the remediation ordered below is a calibration question for the hearing; its existence is not open.

#### D. Lesser sanctions and the remediation package: ordered as a floor, not accepted as a substitute.

Defendants' remediation package is substantial, and — unlike in Round 2 — it no longer consists entirely of promises: the amended responses and withdrawal are represented as served, and the Doc. 135 and trial-admissibility stipulations are made of record. Several items genuinely exceed existing duties: no rule obligated Defendants to fund a neutral examination of their own systems, to pay Plaintiffs' expert costs, to stipulate the alteration evidence to the jury, or to bind themselves to a pleading-stage construction through trial.

But Plaintiffs are correct about what the package is and is not. It arrived fourteen months after the false responses, nearly nine months after exposure, with default on the table. Post-exposure remediation may mitigate going forward; it does not erase antecedent bad faith. *Franklin Livestock*, 251 F. Supp. 3d at 970 ("the creation of false records that are passed off as originals . . . cannot be glossed over"). Much of it — amended responses, source records, sworn accounts of one's own production — was already obligatory or freely available without any order. And it identifies no one and admits nothing. *Cf. Roche Diagnostics*, 2020 WL 2308319, at *7. The Court therefore will not treat the package as *satisfying* Telectron's third element. Whether lesser sanctions suffice cannot be answered in the abstract; it depends on what the hearing establishes about culpability, and on whether punishment and deterrence — not merely cure and restitution — are served as to whichever Defendants are found responsible. Plaintiffs' point is well taken that a wrongdoer left no worse off than honest compliance would have left it has been deterred from nothing. *Carroll-Harris v. Wilkie*, 2019 WL 2205851, at *7-8 (E.D. Mich. May 22, 2019).

The proper disposition — urged by Plaintiffs and, to their credit, substantially accepted by Defendants' own proposed order — is to **order the package as a floor**: every commitment becomes a dated, contempt-backed obligation; compliance earns no immunity from sanctions; and all sanctions tiers, expressly including default, remain reserved on the record the process builds. Defendants' Appendix A supplies a workable framework, and the Court adopts it with modifications reflected in the ordering paragraphs: the "floor, not ceiling" character of the relief is made explicit; the examiner's mandate includes audit authority over the integrity of Defendants' remaining production, including damages materials; an immediate preservation-and-imaging obligation is added; and the fee disposition follows Section IV.F rather than Defendants' proposal.

#### E. The hearing and provenance discovery.

Both sides request an evidentiary hearing preceded by provenance discovery, and the Court agrees it is required: the dispositive remaining questions — the identity of "david," direction, scope of agency, and each Defendant's knowledge — are questions of identity, intent, and credibility that a paper record cannot resolve. The hearing is granted on the schedule set below. Provenance depositions will not count against Plaintiffs' merits-deposition entitlements under Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii) and 30(d)(1) — a point Defendants' proposed order concedes. The declarations ordered below are due *before* the examiner's report and the hearing, with the self-executing adverse-inference consequence Defendants themselves proposed. The Court accepts Defendants' explanation for not attaching declarations to the Response — that sworn provenance accounts should be verifiable against imaged systems — as a reason for the *sequence*, while noting it is not a reason no Defendant could yet have sworn the narrower oath that he or she did not alter, direct, or know. That choice, too, is part of the record the Court will weigh.

#### F. Fees, costs, and the remaining requested relief.

**Expert costs.** Granted. Defendants have committed to pay Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert fees and costs, and the Court orders payment on the schedule below.

**Broader fees.** The Court **finds now** that Plaintiffs are entitled, under Rule 37(c)(1) and *Dragon Jade*, 2019 WL 5887171, at *4, to the reasonable expenses caused by Defendants' unjustified failure to supplement — a violation found in Section IV.A and conceded in substance by Defendants. Quantum, apportionment among Defendants, and any *additional* inherent-power fee award (which requires the bad-faith findings the hearing will supply, *see Branch-Haines v. E. Coast Waffles, Inc.*, 2021 WL 812174, at *6 (N.D. Fla. Jan. 29, 2021); *Chambers*, 501 U.S. at 44-45) are deferred to post-hearing determination.

**Special master.** Denied without prejudice. The neutral forensic examiner, with the audit authority conferred below, is the proportionate instrument. *Pycsa Panama, S.A. v. Tensar Earth Techs., Inc.*, 2008 WL 11403205, at *2 (S.D. Fla. May 8, 2008); *Cobell v. Babbitt*, 37 F. Supp. 2d 6, 37 (D.D.C. 1999).

**Verification presumption.** Plaintiffs' requested blanket presumption against the accuracy of Defendant-prepared summaries is denied without prejudice as largely mooted: the order below entitles Plaintiffs to all native-format source records, relieves them of any obligation to accept Defendant-prepared summaries, and subjects the remaining production to neutral audit. If the examiner's report or the hearing reveals further integrity problems, Plaintiffs may renew.

**Adverse-inference instructions and damages-phase sanctions.** Reserved for the hearing, except that the trial admissibility of the alteration evidence is confirmed by stipulation and order below.

### V. CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Accordingly, it is **ORDERED**:

1. Plaintiffs' Motion for Sanctions (Doc. 145) is **GRANTED IN PART, DENIED IN PART WITHOUT PREJUDICE, and DEFERRED IN PART** as set forth herein.

2. **Findings.** The Court finds, by clear and convincing evidence, that the documents produced as Bates range D002379–D005171 were deliberately fabricated for use in this litigation while in Defendants' custody and within Defendants' document-collection process, and that each Defendant violated Rule 26(e) by failing, without substantial justification, to amend certified discovery responses between September 24, 2025 and the service of amendments represented in Defendants' Response. Attribution of the fabrication to any particular Defendant is **reserved** for the evidentiary hearing.

3. **Remediation ordered as a floor.** Defendants' remediation commitments are entered as orders of this Court on the terms below. Compliance with this Order is mandatory and contempt-backed; it does not constitute, and shall not be argued as, a substitute for sanctions or a safe harbor against any sanctions tier, all of which remain reserved.

4. **Amended responses and withdrawal.** Within **7 days**, Defendants shall file a notice confirming service of (a) amended responses identifying the authentic agreements (Docs. 16-3 through 16-23) and withdrawing the identification of Bates range D002379–D005171, and (b) the formal withdrawal notice. Material deficiency or non-filing triggers an order to show cause why escalated sanctions should not issue.

5. **Stipulations confirmed as orders.** Defendants are bound by the Court's construction of the agreements (Doc. 135) for all purposes in this case, including summary judgment and trial; and the fact of the alteration, and the forensic evidence of it, is admissible at trial, with all parties free to argue the inferences it supports.

6. **Preservation and imaging.** Effective immediately, Defendants shall preserve, and shall not dispose of, modify, or reimage, all computers, systems, accounts, and media used in the assembly, scanning, processing, or transmission of the February–April 2025 productions, including any associated with the username "david."

7. **Sworn provenance declarations — due Day 21.** Within **21 days**, each of Kajdic, Begovic, and an FR8 corporate representative with knowledge of its document-management and collection processes, and each person known to Defendants to have collected, scanned, assembled, or transmitted the produced documents, shall serve and lodge a declaration under 28 U.S.C. § 1746 addressing: (a) the declarant's role in and knowledge of the alteration; (b) the identity of every known user of the username "david" on any relevant system; (c) chain of custody between collection and delivery to former counsel; and (d) any investigation undertaken since September 24, 2025. Any Defendant who, by the deadline, neither serves a compliant declaration nor files a notice invoking a specific privilege (including the Fifth Amendment) is **deemed to consent** that the Court may draw adverse inferences on attribution against that Defendant at the hearing. FR8's failure to produce compliant custodian declarations within its control shall be treated as evidence bearing on FR8's bad faith.

8. **Neutral forensic examination — at Defendants' sole expense.** Within **14 days**, the parties shall jointly designate a neutral forensic examiner; failing agreement, each side shall submit two nominations and the Court will appoint. Within **14 days of appointment**, Defendants shall make the preserved systems available for imaging. The examiner shall report simultaneously to both parties and the Court within **45 days of imaging** on (a) the provenance of the alterations and (b) the integrity of Defendants' remaining production, including text messages and all damages-related materials, as to which the examiner shall have continuing audit authority through the damages phase. Failure by any Defendant to make systems timely available, and any failure of identification attributable to a Defendant's non-cooperation, non-retention, or spoliation, shall weigh against that Defendant on attribution and on the adequacy of lesser sanctions, and permits renewed application for terminating sanctions on an expedited basis.

9. **Expert costs.** Within **30 days** of service of a reasonably itemized statement, Defendants shall pay Plaintiffs' reasonable forensic-expert fees and costs incurred in detecting and analyzing the alterations. Disputes as to reasonableness shall be submitted to the Court; non-payment absent a filed dispute is enforceable as contempt.

10. **Damages source records.** Within **45 days**, Defendants shall produce, in native format with metadata and at their expense, all source documents underlying any Defendant-prepared summary or compilation of damages-related data. Plaintiffs shall not be required to accept Defendant-prepared summaries in lieu of source records. Plaintiffs' requested presumption against the accuracy of such summaries is **DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE** to renewal upon the examiner's report or the hearing record.

11. **Provenance depositions.** Plaintiffs may take depositions limited to provenance topics — including of each Defendant, each declarant under Paragraph 7, and any person identified as "david" — which shall not count against Plaintiffs' deposition entitlements under Rules 30(a)(2)(A)(ii) and 30(d)(1).

12. **Evidentiary hearing.** An evidentiary hearing is set for a date approximately **120 days** from this Order, to be fixed by separate notice. Each individual Defendant and FR8's corporate representative shall appear and be subject to examination. The Court will thereafter make defendant-by-defendant findings, under the clear-and-convincing standard, on willfulness or bad faith, prejudice, and the adequacy of lesser sanctions. *Telectron*, 116 F.R.D. at 131. The facts found in Paragraph 2 will not be relitigated.

13. **Default judgment.** Plaintiffs' request for entry of default judgment is **DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE** as to FR8 and Kajdic, as premature pending the hearing, and **DENIED** as to Begovic, against whom it is not sought. Default judgment against FR8 and Kajdic, adverse-inference instructions, and all other sanctions tiers remain expressly reserved and available on the hearing record.

14. **Special master.** Plaintiffs' alternative request for appointment of a special master under Rule 53 is **DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE** in favor of the neutral examiner provided in Paragraph 8.

15. **Fees.** Plaintiffs' entitlement to reasonable expenses caused by Defendants' failure to supplement is **ESTABLISHED** under Rule 37(c)(1); quantum, apportionment, and any further fee award under the Court's inherent power are **DEFERRED** to post-hearing determination.

16. **Escalation.** Material non-compliance with any paragraph of this Order is independently sanctionable, may itself be treated as evidence of bad faith, and permits Plaintiffs to renew any request herein denied without prejudice on an expedited basis.

**DONE AND ORDERED** in Jacksonville, Florida. *(Simulation exercise — not an actual court order.)*

## Judge's Closing Commentary on the Exercise

*Out of role. These are coaching observations on the three-round arc, not part of the order.*

**How the advocacy evolved.** Plaintiffs began with a maximalist, repetitive draft that led with a fraud-on-the-court theory they did not need and could not sustain, buried its best facts in rhetoric, and asked for everything against everyone. By the final round they had inverted the structure: a single chronology table, defendant-differentiated relief, the clear-and-convincing standard embraced rather than resisted, the burden-shift softened to a supportable permissive inference, and — most importantly — explicit candor about what no cited case holds. Defendants began with minimization and boilerplate distinctions; by the final round they had built the most grantable package on the docket: conceded what was undeniable, performed remediation rather than promising it, answered the hard questions (the missing declarations, the absent investigation) before being asked, and handed the Court an executable proposed order. Both sides ended the exercise writing for the judge's problem — how to do justice without reversible error — rather than for their own indignation or comfort.

**Best move, each side.** Plaintiffs' single best move was the "order it as a floor" reframing of Defendants' remediation package. Rather than oppose an attractive offer, they demanded it be *ordered* — converting their opponent's best asset into the first item of their own requested relief and preserving the sanctions question on top of it. That is why nearly all of Paragraphs 3–11 above are things Plaintiffs asked for. Defendants' single best move was Appendix A itself, particularly the self-executing adverse-inference consequences they proposed *against their own clients*. Volunteering the mechanism a skeptical judge would otherwise have to construct made the deny-default-and-order-process disposition the path of least resistance, and bought credibility no argument could.

**The decisive factor.** Attribution plus due process. The forensic case that the documents were fabricated in Defendants' custody was overwhelming and effectively conceded; but every terminating-sanctions authority either side cited involved a finding or admission of the sanctioned party's own culpability, and neither side found a case defaulting a corporation on an unidentified actor without a hearing. Once both sides requested the same hearing, default-on-the-papers had no remaining justification — Plaintiffs implicitly recognized this when their final draft hedged to "default at the conclusion of the hearing — or now, if the Court finds the record sufficient." The order therefore gives Plaintiffs every finding the paper record permits (the fabrication, custody, the Rule 26(e) violation, fee entitlement) and defers only what genuinely requires testimony.

**One lesson for each side.** For Plaintiffs: build the remedy from the record you have, not the record you deserve. Their case was always strongest on the *fact* of fabrication and weakest on the *law* of attribution; the rounds they spent demanding immediate default were rounds the agency theory went unarmed with authority. Their final candor — conceding the absence of a case in point and restructuring the ask around sequencing — got them more relief than maximalism ever would have, including the warning paragraph that makes FR8's silence at the hearing nearly as costly as an admission. For Defendants: process arguments postpone the reckoning; they do not replace evidence. Three rounds in, no client has sworn a word, and the brief's elegant explanation for the missing declarations is still a brief without oaths. They won the sequencing battle comprehensively — but Paragraph 7's deadline now does what no opponent's brief could, and if the declarations cannot truthfully be signed, everything the candor strategy earned will be spent in a single afternoon of testimony. The exercise's enduring lesson for both: in sanctions litigation, credibility is the currency, and it is earned by concession, performance, and candor — never by adjectives.
